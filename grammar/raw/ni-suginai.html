～に過ぎない: nothing more than

～に過ぎない attaches to a noun (or a plain-form verb) and means "is nothing more than" or "is merely". It comes from the verb 過ぎる (to exceed): literally, "it does not go beyond X".

The speaker uses it to downplay something as small or unimportant. Unlike ～だけ, which neutrally says "only", ～に過ぎない adds a dismissive judgment: "it's just X, nothing special".

1. Downplaying what something is

Use ～に過ぎない to say that something does not deserve to be taken more seriously than it really is.

それは噂に過ぎない。

"That's nothing more than a rumor."

  • それ = that
  • は = topic marker
  • 噂（うわさ）= rumor
  • に過ぎない（にすぎない）= is nothing more than

2. Modestly downplaying yourself

～に過ぎない is also a common way to be modest about your own position or role.

私は一人の学生に過ぎない。

"I am nothing more than a single student."

  • 私（わたし）= I
  • は = topic marker
  • 一人（ひとり）= one (person)
  • の = connects the counter to the noun
  • 学生（がくせい）= student
  • に過ぎない（にすぎない）= am nothing more than

How to form it

Noun + に過ぎない (a plain-form verb can also come before it)

  • 噂（うわさ）= rumor → 噂に過ぎない（うわさにすぎない）= is just a rumor
  • 冗談（じょうだん）= joke → 冗談に過ぎない（じょうだんにすぎない）= is only a joke
  • 学生（がくせい）= student → 学生に過ぎない（がくせいにすぎない）= is merely a student

Key idea

～に過ぎない draws a low ceiling over something: whatever it looks like, it does not go beyond "just X", so don't overestimate it.
