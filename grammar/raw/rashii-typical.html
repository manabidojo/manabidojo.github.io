～らしい（典型）: typical of / just like a real

らしい after a noun can mean that something has the typical, ideal qualities of that noun. This is different from the らしい that means "I hear" or "it seems".

In English it maps to "typical of", "befitting" or "like a real...": 男らしい = manly, 春らしい = spring-like.

1. Having the typical qualities

The thing matches the image people have of the noun.

今日は春らしい天気だ。

"Today's weather is very spring-like."

  • 今日（きょう）= today
  • は = topic marker
  • 春らしい（はるらしい）= spring-like
  • 天気（てんき）= weather
  • だ = is

2. Fitting (or not fitting) someone's character

About a person, it says that an action is typical of them. The negative らしくない says the opposite.

遅刻するなんて、君らしくないね。

"Being late isn't like you."

  • 遅刻する（ちこくする）= to be late
  • なんて = something like
  • 君（きみ）= you
  • らしくない = not like (you)
  • ね = right?

"Typical" らしい vs "it seems" らしい

The other らしい ("it seems / I hear") can also follow a noun: 犯人は彼らしい (it seems he's the culprit). The "typical" らしい describes a quality, so it usually sits right before another noun or talks about how fitting something is.

How to form it

Noun + らしい

  • 子供（こども）= child → 子供らしい（こどもらしい）= childlike
  • 学生（がくせい）= student → 学生らしい（がくせいらしい）= like a proper student
  • 彼（かれ）= him → 彼らしい（かれらしい）= typical of him

Key idea

Noun + らしい praises (or criticizes) how well something matches the ideal image of that noun.
