～から: giving a reason ("because")

～から attaches to the end of a reason and means "because" or "so". The reason comes first, then から, then the result, the opposite of the usual English order.

Be careful: this is not the same から as in 東京から ("from Tokyo"). Directly after a noun, から means "from"; after a full statement (a verb, an adjective, or a noun + だ), it means "because".

1. Stating why

Put the reason before から and the conclusion after it. Compared with the similar pattern ～ので, ～から sounds more direct and personal. It is the everyday way to explain yourself.

忙しいから行けない。

"I can't go because I'm busy."

  • 忙しい（いそがしい）= busy
  • から = because (reason marker)
  • 行けない（いけない）= cannot go

2. Reason first, result second

In longer sentences, think of から as "so": everything before it is the reason, and everything after it is what you decided or what happened as a result.

明日はテストがあるから、今日は早く寝ます。

"I have a test tomorrow, so I'll go to bed early today."

  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • は = topic marker
  • テスト = test
  • が = subject marker
  • ある = there is
  • から = because / so
  • 今日（きょう）= today
  • 早く（はやく）= early
  • 寝ます（ねます）= will go to bed

How to form it

Plain statement + から (nouns and な-adjectives take だから). The polite form also works before から, as in 忙しいですから.

  • 忙しい（いそがしい）= busy → 忙しいから（いそがしいから）= because (I) am busy
  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行くから（いくから）= because (I) am going
  • 学生だ（がくせいだ）= is a student → 学生だから（がくせいだから）= because (I) am a student

Key idea

から points back at the reason you just said. Say the reason, add から, then say what follows from it: reason + から + result.
