～がち: tends to (usually something negative)

～がち attaches to the ます-stem of a verb or directly to a noun and means "tends to" or "is prone to": 忘れます becomes 忘れがち, "tend to forget". The result behaves like a な-adjective (がちだ, がちな + noun).

The tendency is almost always something undesirable like forgetting, skipping, getting sick. English equivalents are "tend to", "apt to", or "often ends up".

1. Verb stem + がち: a bad habit or recurring lapse

Use this when an unwanted action keeps happening, often without the person meaning it to.

最近、忘れがちなんです。

"Lately I tend to forget things."

  • 最近（さいきん）= lately, recently
  • 忘れがち（わすれがち）= tend to forget
  • なんです = explanatory ending ("the thing is...")

2. Noun + がち: a recurring condition

がち also attaches to a few nouns to describe a state that occurs frequently: 病気がち (often sick), 留守がち (often away from home), 曇りがち (mostly cloudy).

子供のころ、彼は病気がちだった。

"As a child, he was often sick."

  • 子供（こども）= child
  • の = links the two nouns (of)
  • ころ = time, the period around
  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 病気がち（びょうきがち）= prone to illness, often sick
  • だった = was (past of だ)

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + がち / Noun + がち

  • 忘れる（わすれる）= to forget → 忘れがち（わすれがち）= tend to forget
  • 休む（やすむ）= to be absent → 休みがち（やすみがち）= often absent
  • 病気（びょうき）= illness → 病気がち（びょうきがち）= often sick

Key idea

～がち flags a recurring tendency the speaker views negatively: it happens more often than it should. If the pattern is neutral or welcome, がち is usually the wrong choice.
