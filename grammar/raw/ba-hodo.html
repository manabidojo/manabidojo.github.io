～ば～ほど: the more ..., the more ...

～ば～ほど ties two changes together: as one thing goes up, the other goes up (or down) with it. You say the same verb or adjective twice — first in the ば-form, then in its plain form — and attach ほど.

It maps directly onto the English pattern "the more ..., the more ..." ("the cheaper, the better", "the more I think, the less I understand").

1. With verbs

Repeat the verb as ば-form + dictionary form + ほど. The rest of the sentence states what scales along with it.

練習すればするほど上手になる。

"The more you practice, the better you get."

  • 練習すれば（れんしゅうすれば）= if you practice (ば-form)
  • するほど = the more you do it
  • 上手（じょうず）= good at, skilled
  • に = particle marking the result of a change
  • なる = to become

2. With adjectives

い-adjectives repeat as ～ければ + plain adjective + ほど.

安ければ安いほどいい。

"The cheaper, the better."

  • 安ければ（やすければ）= if it is cheap (ば-form)
  • 安いほど（やすいほど）= to the extent that it is cheap
  • いい = good

How to form it

Verb ば-form + dictionary form + ほど / い-adjective ～ければ + plain form + ほど (な-adjectives commonly use ～であればあるほど)

  • 練習する（れんしゅうする）= to practice → 練習すればするほど（れんしゅうすればするほど）= the more you practice
  • 読む（よむ）= to read → 読めば読むほど（よめばよむほど）= the more you read
  • 安い（やすい）= cheap → 安ければ安いほど（やすければやすいほど）= the cheaper it is

Key idea

～ば～ほど expresses a proportional relationship: as one thing changes, another changes along with it. The greater the degree of A, the greater (or smaller) the degree of B.
