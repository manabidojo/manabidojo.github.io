～しかない: only / no choice but to

～しかない is built from the particle しか plus a negative verb, most often ない (the plain negative of ある, to exist). しか always needs a negative after it, and together the pair means "nothing but".

After a noun it works like English "only", with a feeling of "and that is not much". After a verb in dictionary form, it means "there is no choice but to do it".

1. Noun + しか + negative: only

Use しか with a negative verb to say there is nothing other than the thing mentioned. Unlike だけ, which is a neutral "only", しか suggests the amount is small or not enough.

財布に百円しかない。

"I only have 100 yen in my wallet."

  • 財布（さいふ）= wallet
  • に = location marker (in)
  • 百円（ひゃくえん）= 100 yen
  • しか = nothing but (always with a negative)
  • ない = there is not

2. Verb + しかない: no choice but to

After a verb in dictionary form, しかない says this action is the only option left. The speaker often sounds resigned or determined.

やるしかない。

"There's no choice but to do it."

  • やる = to do
  • しかない = there is no (option) other than

バスが来ないから、歩くしかない。

"The bus isn't coming, so there's nothing to do but walk."

  • バス = bus
  • が = subject marker
  • 来ない（こない）= doesn't come
  • から = because / so
  • 歩く（あるく）= to walk
  • しかない = no choice but

How to form it

Noun + しか + negative verb, or Verb (dictionary form) + しかない

  • 水（みず）= water → 水しか飲まない（みずしかのまない）= drink nothing but water
  • やる = to do → やるしかない = have no choice but to do it
  • 待つ（まつ）= to wait → 待つしかない（まつしかない）= can only wait

Key idea

しか erases every option except one: "apart from this, nothing". With a noun that means "only", and with a verb it means the action is the one path left.
