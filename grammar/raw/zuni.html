～ずに: without doing

～ずに means "without doing" something. It is made from the verb's ない-stem plus the classical negative ず and the particle に: 言わない → 言わずに ("without saying").

It means the same as ～ないで, but ～ずに sounds more formal and is more common in writing. One irregular form to memorize: する becomes せずに, not しずに.

Doing one thing without doing another

The clause before ～ずに describes an action that did not happen, while the main clause describes what was done instead, or done in that state.

何も言わずに出て行った。

"He left without saying anything."

  • 何も（なにも）= nothing (with a negative)
  • 言わずに（いわずに）= without saying
  • 出て行った（でていった）= went out, left

朝ご飯を食べずに学校へ行った。

"I went to school without eating breakfast."

  • 朝ご飯（あさごはん）= breakfast
  • を = object marker
  • 食べずに（たべずに）= without eating
  • 学校（がっこう）= school
  • へ = direction marker
  • 行った（いった）= went

How to form it

Verb ない-form → replace ない with ずに

  • 言う（いう）= to say → 言わずに（いわずに）= without saying
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べずに（たべずに）= without eating
  • する = to do → せずに = without doing (irregular)

Key idea

～ずに is the bookish twin of ～ないで: take the ない-form, swap ない for ずに, and you get "without doing X". Just remember せずに for する.
