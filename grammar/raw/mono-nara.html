～ものなら: if (I) could / if you dare

～ものなら has two uses. After a potential verb, it expresses a wish that is hard to fulfill: "if only I could". After a volitional verb, it gives a warning: "if you dare to do it, something bad will happen".

In English the first is "if (I) could...", the second "if you so much as..." or "just try and...".

1. A wish that's hard to fulfill

With a potential form, the speaker wishes for something they probably can't do.

できるものなら、もう一度やり直したい。

"If only I could, I'd do it all over again."

  • できる = can do
  • ものなら = if (I) could
  • もう一度（もういちど）= once more
  • やり直したい（やりなおしたい）= want to redo

2. A warning

With the volitional form, it warns about the consequences of an action.

遅刻しようものなら、先生にひどく怒られる。

"If you're so much as late, the teacher gets really angry."

  • 遅刻しよう（ちこくしよう）= be late
  • ものなら = if (you) dare
  • 先生（せんせい）= teacher
  • に = by
  • ひどく = terribly
  • 怒られる（おこられる）= get scolded

How to form it

Verb potential form + ものなら, Verb volitional form + ものなら

  • 帰れる（かえれる）= can go home → 帰れるものなら（かえれるものなら）= if only (I) could go home
  • 戻れる（もどれる）= can go back → 戻れるものなら（もどれるものなら）= if only (I) could go back
  • 言う（いう）= to say → 言おうものなら（いおうものなら）= if (you) dare say it

Key idea

～ものなら stretches toward something out of reach: a wish that likely won't come true, or an action that would bring trouble.
