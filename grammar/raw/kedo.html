～けど: but / however

～けど is a casual conjunction meaning "but". It attaches to the end of a clause and connects it to a second clause that contrasts with or qualifies the first.

It is one of the most common words in everyday Japanese conversation. In formal speech and writing, が plays the same role.

1. Contrast: "A, but B"

Say the first fact, add けど, then say the second fact that goes against it.

暑いけど、行く。

"It's hot, but I'll go."

  • 暑い（あつい）= hot
  • けど = but
  • 行く（いく）= will go

2. Softening the start of a conversation

Japanese speakers also use けど to introduce themselves or a topic gently, leaving the sentence open so the listener can respond. Here it does not really mean "but".

もしもし、田中ですけど。

"Hello, this is Tanaka (calling)."

  • もしもし = hello (on the phone)
  • 田中（たなか）= Tanaka (a family name)
  • です = polite "am / is"
  • けど = softener (politely leaves the sentence open)

How to form it

Plain or polite form + けど (nouns and な-adjectives need だ before けど)

  • 暑い（あつい）= hot → 暑いけど（あついけど）= it is hot, but
  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行くけど（いくけど）= (I) will go, but
  • 雨（あめ）= rain → 雨だけど（あめだけど）= it is raining, but

Key idea

～けど is the casual, all-purpose "but" of spoken Japanese. It contrasts two ideas, or simply softens what you are about to say.
