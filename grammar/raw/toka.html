～とか: things like / I heard

とか lists vague examples, like a casual や. At the end of a sentence, it can also pass on something the speaker heard but isn't sure about.

In English it maps to "things like", "...and stuff", or "I heard that...".

1. Listing vague examples

It suggests there are more items than the ones named.

週末は映画とか見る。

"On weekends I watch movies and stuff."

  • 週末（しゅうまつ）= weekend
  • は = topic marker
  • 映画（えいが）= movie
  • とか = and stuff
  • 見る（みる）= to watch

2. Passing on something heard

After a plain form, とか reports hearsay that the speaker can't confirm.

明日、雪が降るとか。

"I heard it might snow tomorrow."

  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • 雪（ゆき）= snow
  • が = subject marker
  • 降る（ふる）= to fall
  • とか = I heard

How to form it

Noun + とか (+ noun + とか), Plain form + とか

  • 寿司（すし）= sushi → 寿司とか（すしとか）= sushi and stuff
  • 掃除する（そうじする）= to clean → 掃除するとか（そうじするとか）= things like cleaning
  • 辞める（やめる）= to quit → 辞めるとか（やめるとか）= (I heard) they're quitting

In lists it is often repeated: 寿司とかラーメンとか (sushi, ramen and so on).

Key idea

とか keeps things vague: "things like this", or "something I heard".
