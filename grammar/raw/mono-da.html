～ものだ: how things were, are, or should be

～ものだ attaches to the plain form of a verb or adjective and steps back from one single event to comment on how things are in general. Depending on the form before it, it expresses a nostalgic "I used to...", a general truth ("that's just how it is"), or a social expectation ("one is supposed to...").

1. Nostalgic recollection: "used to"

With the past た-form, ～ものだ looks back fondly on something you did regularly in the past. It often appears with words like よく (often) or 昔 (long ago).

子供の頃はよく泳いだものだ。

"I used to swim a lot as a child."

  • 子供の頃（こどものころ）= childhood, when I was a child
  • は = topic marker
  • よく = often
  • 泳いだものだ（およいだものだ）= used to swim

2. General truth: "that's just how it is"

With the non-past plain form, ～ものだ states something the speaker sees as part of the natural order of things.

人間は誰でも失敗するものだ。

"Everyone makes mistakes, that's just how people are."

  • 人間（にんげん）= human beings, people
  • は = topic marker
  • 誰でも（だれでも）= everyone, anyone
  • 失敗するものだ（しっぱいするものだ）= make mistakes (by nature)

3. Social expectation: "one is supposed to"

～ものだ can also state what people in general ought to do like a shared social rule rather than advice aimed at one person.

年上の人には敬語を使うものだ。

"You're supposed to use polite language with older people."

  • 年上の人（としうえのひと）= older person
  • には = to, toward (topic of the rule)
  • 敬語（けいご）= polite language, honorifics
  • を = object marker
  • 使うものだ（つかうものだ）= are supposed to use

How to form it

Plain form + ものだ (for the nostalgic "used to" meaning, use the past た-form: V-た + ものだ)

  • 泳ぐ（およぐ）= to swim → 泳いだものだ（およいだものだ）= used to swim
  • 失敗する（しっぱいする）= to make a mistake → 失敗するものだ（しっぱいするものだ）= it's natural to make mistakes
  • 使う（つかう）= to use → 使うものだ（つかうものだ）= one is supposed to use

Key idea

～ものだ zooms out from a single event to the general nature of things: what people commonly did in the past, what is simply true about the world, or what everyone is expected to do.
