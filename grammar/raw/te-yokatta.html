～てよかった: I'm glad that

よかった is the past of いい ("good"). After a て-form, ～てよかった says that the speaker is glad about how something turned out.

In English it is "I'm glad (that)...". With a negative, ～なくてよかった, it is relief that something bad didn't happen.

1. Being glad about what happened

It expresses happiness or relief about a situation that went well.

会えてよかった。

"I'm glad I got to see you."

  • 会えて（あえて）= being able to meet
  • よかった = was good, I'm glad

2. Glad that something didn't happen

With ～なくて, the speaker is relieved that something was avoided.

遅刻しなくてよかった。

"I'm glad I wasn't late."

  • 遅刻（ちこく）= being late
  • しなくて = not doing
  • よかった = I'm glad

How to form it

Verb て-form + よかった, Verb ない-form (drop い) + くてよかった

  • 知る（しる）= to know → 知ってよかった（しってよかった）= glad I found out
  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来てよかった（きてよかった）= glad I came
  • 降らない（ふらない）= doesn't rain → 降らなくてよかった（ふらなくてよかった）= glad it didn't rain

Nouns and な-adjectives take でよかった: 無事でよかった (I'm glad you're safe).

Key idea

～てよかった looks back and says "that turned out well". The て-form names what you're glad about.
