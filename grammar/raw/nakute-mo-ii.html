～なくてもいい: you don't have to

～なくてもいい is built from the ない-form plus ても ("even if") and いい ("good"). Literally "even if you don't, it's fine", it says that something is not necessary.

In English it is "you don't have to" or "there's no need to". It is the opposite of ～なければならない ("must").

1. Saying something isn't necessary

It often sounds reassuring: the listener is free not to do something.

明日は来なくてもいいよ。

"You don't have to come tomorrow."

  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • は = topic marker
  • 来なくても（こなくても）= even if (you) don't come
  • いい = fine
  • よ = (you know)

2. Asking whether you have to

As a question, it asks for permission not to do something.

全部食べなくてもいいですか。

"Is it okay if I don't eat all of it?"

  • 全部（ぜんぶ）= all
  • 食べなくても（たべなくても）= even if (I) don't eat
  • いいですか = is it okay?

How to form it

Verb ない-form (drop い) + くてもいい

  • 書く（かく）= to write → 書かなくてもいい（かかなくてもいい）= don't have to write
  • 急ぐ（いそぐ）= to hurry → 急がなくてもいい（いそがなくてもいい）= no need to hurry
  • 心配する（しんぱいする）= to worry → 心配しなくてもいい（しんぱいしなくてもいい）= no need to worry

Common variants with the same meaning: ～なくても大丈夫, ～なくてもかまわない. In casual speech: ～なくていい.

Key idea

～なくてもいい gives permission not to do something: no obligation, no problem.
