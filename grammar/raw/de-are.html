～であれ: whatever / whether (it is)

であれ is a formal form of である ("to be"). After a question word, 何であれ means "whatever it is". Repeated, AであれBであれ means "whether it is A or B".

In English it is "whatever...", "whoever..." or "whether... or...". It is formal and common in writing.

1. Whatever the case

With a question word, it means the conclusion applies no matter what.

理由が何であれ、暴力は許されない。

"Whatever the reason, violence is unacceptable."

  • 理由（りゆう）= reason
  • が = subject marker
  • 何であれ（なんであれ）= whatever (it is)
  • 暴力（ぼうりょく）= violence
  • は = topic marker
  • 許されない（ゆるされない）= is not allowed

2. Whether A or B

Repeated, it lists two cases that are treated the same.

大人であれ子供であれ、ルールは同じだ。

"Whether you're an adult or a child, the rules are the same."

  • 大人（おとな）= adult
  • であれ = whether
  • 子供（こども）= child
  • であれ = or
  • ルール = rules
  • は = topic marker
  • 同じ（おなじ）= the same

How to form it

Question word + であれ, Noun + であれ + noun + であれ

  • 誰（だれ）= who → 誰であれ（だれであれ）= whoever it is
  • どこ = where → どこであれ = wherever it is
  • 晴れ（はれ）= sunny → 晴れであれ雨であれ（はれであれあめであれ）= rain or shine

Key idea

～であれ flattens every difference: whatever it is, the rule stays the same.
