まるで: just like / as if

まるで is an adverb that announces a vivid comparison. Placed early in the sentence, it signals "what follows is a figure of speech", and the comparison is then completed by ようだ (or the casual みたいだ).

English equivalents are "just like" and "exactly as if". The plain simile ～のようだ already means "like"; adding まるで intensifies it, the way "just" or "exactly" does in English.

1. まるで + noun + のようだ

The most common shape: まるで sets up the simile, and Noun + のようだ resolves it at the end.

彼女の笑顔はまるで夢のようだ。

"Her smile is just like a dream."

  • 彼女（かのじょ）= she, her
  • の = possessive particle
  • 笑顔（えがお）= smile
  • は = topic marker
  • まるで = just like, exactly as if
  • 夢（ゆめ）= dream
  • のようだ = is like

2. まるで with のように / みたいだ

まるで also pairs with のように in the middle of a sentence, or with the casual みたいだ in conversation.

彼はまるで子供のように喜んだ。

"He was delighted, just like a child."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • まるで = just like
  • 子供（こども）= child
  • のように = like (describing how)
  • 喜んだ（よろこんだ）= was delighted

How to form it

まるで + Noun + のようだ／のように／のような (or casual みたいだ)

  • 夢（ゆめ）= dream → まるで夢のようだ（まるでゆめのようだ）= is just like a dream
  • 子供（こども）= child → まるで子供のように（まるでこどものように）= just like a child
  • 本物（ほんもの）= the real thing → まるで本物みたいだ（まるでほんものみたいだ）= looks just like the real thing

Key idea

まるで cannot make a comparison by itself. It is a signpost that must be answered by ようだ or みたいだ later in the sentence. Together they say not just "like", but "exactly like".
