～ような: "like" or "such as" before a noun

～ような is the noun-modifying form of ようだ. It links a comparison or an example to the noun that follows: A のような B means "a B like A" or "a B such as A".

A whole clause can also come before ような, describing what the noun is like or seems like.

1. Simile: "like a ..."

Use Noun + のような + Noun to say one thing resembles another.

夢のような話だ。

"It's a story like a dream."

  • 夢（ゆめ）= dream
  • の = links the noun to ような
  • ような = like / resembling (modifies the next noun)
  • 話（はなし）= story
  • だ = is (plain copula)

2. Giving an example: "such as ..."

The same form can introduce a representative example rather than a resemblance.

京都のような古い町が好きだ。

"I like old towns like Kyoto."

  • 京都（きょうと）= Kyoto
  • のような = like / such as
  • 古い（ふるい）= old
  • 町（まち）= town
  • が = subject marker (marks what is liked)
  • 好きだ（すきだ）= like

Note the difference from its adverbial partner ～ように: ような modifies a noun (夢のような話, "a dream-like story"), while ように modifies a verb or adjective (鳥のように飛ぶ, "fly like a bird").

How to form it

Noun + の + ような + Noun, or plain clause + ような + Noun

  • 夢（ゆめ）= dream → 夢のような話（ゆめのようなはなし）= a story like a dream
  • 子供（こども）= child → 子供のような顔（こどものようなかお）= a childlike face
  • 誰でも分かる（だれでもわかる）= anyone can understand → 誰でも分かるような説明（だれでもわかるようなせつめい）= an explanation that anyone can understand

Key idea

～ような hangs a comparison or example onto a noun: whatever comes before ような tells you what the following noun is like.
