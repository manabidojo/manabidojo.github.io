～く: adjective as adverb

The ～く form (連用形) turns an i-adjective into an adverb, so it modifies a verb instead of a noun, much like English "-ly": 早く起きる (はやくおきる) = get up early.

1. Turning an adjective into an adverb

Swap the final い for く and place the word before the verb it describes.

毎朝、早く起きる。

"I get up early every morning."

  • 毎朝（まいあさ）= every morning
  • 早く（はやく）= early (adverb)
  • 起きる（おきる）= to get up

2. ～くなる: "become ..."

With the verb なる ("to become"), the ～く form describes a change of state.

最近、寒くなった。

"It has gotten cold recently."

  • 最近（さいきん）= recently
  • 寒く（さむく）= cold (adverbial)
  • なった = became

How to form it

Drop the final い, add く. This works for every i-adjective; the only quirk is that いい ("good") uses its older form よい, giving よく.

  • 早い（はやい）= early → 早く（はやく）= early (adverb)
  • 大きい（おおきい）= big → 大きく（おおきく）= greatly / (become) big
  • よい／いい = good → よく = well / often

Key idea

～く is the "-ly" of Japanese: it points the adjective at the verb instead of a noun, describing how something is done or what something turns into when used with なる.
