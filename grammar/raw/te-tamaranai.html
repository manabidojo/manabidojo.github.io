～てたまらない: unbearably / dying to

たまらない means "can't stand it". After the て-form of an adjective or a feeling, ～てたまらない says that a feeling or sensation is so strong it's unbearable.

In English it is "unbearably", "really, really" or "dying to".

1. An unbearable sensation

It often describes physical states like heat, pain or hunger.

暑くてたまらない。

"It's unbearably hot."

  • 暑くて（あつくて）= hot (and)
  • たまらない = can't stand it

2. A strong feeling or wish

It also describes looking forward to something so much it's hard to bear.

夏休みが楽しみでたまらない。

"I can't wait for summer vacation."

  • 夏休み（なつやすみ）= summer vacation
  • が = subject marker
  • 楽しみで（たのしみで）= looking forward (and)
  • たまらない = can't stand it

How to form it

い-adjective (drop い) + くてたまらない, な-adjective + でたまらない, Verb ます-stem + たくてたまらない

  • 眠い（ねむい）= sleepy → 眠くてたまらない（ねむくてたまらない）= unbearably sleepy
  • 心配（しんぱい）= worried → 心配でたまらない（しんぱいでたまらない）= terribly worried
  • 会う（あう）= to meet → 会いたくてたまらない（あいたくてたまらない）= dying to see (someone)

It describes the speaker's own feelings. For other people, add ようだ or らしい.

Key idea

～てたまらない is a feeling turned up to the maximum: so strong the speaker can't take it.
