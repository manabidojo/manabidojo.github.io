～ことはない: there's no need to

～ことはない after a verb in the dictionary form says that an action is unnecessary. It often reassures someone or tells them not to overdo it.

In English it is "there's no need to" or "you don't have to".

1. Reassuring someone

It tells the listener that worrying or rushing isn't necessary.

そんなに心配することはないよ。

"There's no need to worry so much."

  • そんなに = so much
  • 心配する（しんぱいする）= to worry
  • ことはない = there's no need
  • よ = (you know)

2. Saying an effort isn't needed

It also says that some trouble is not worth taking.

わざわざ来ることはなかったのに。

"You didn't have to come all this way."

  • わざわざ = go out of one's way
  • 来る（くる）= to come
  • ことはなかった = there was no need
  • のに = (but you did)

～ことはない vs ～たことはない

With the dictionary form, 心配することはない means "there's no need to worry". With the た-form, 心配したことはない means "I have never worried", which is a statement about experience.

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + ことはない

  • 謝る（あやまる）= to apologize → 謝ることはない（あやまることはない）= no need to apologize
  • 急ぐ（いそぐ）= to hurry → 急ぐことはない（いそぐことはない）= no need to hurry
  • 泣く（なく）= to cry → 泣くことはない（なくことはない）= no need to cry

Key idea

～ことはない takes pressure off: the action isn't necessary, so the listener can relax.
