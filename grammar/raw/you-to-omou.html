～（よ）うと思う: I'm thinking of doing

The volitional form (行こう, 食べよう) plus と思う says what the speaker intends to do.

In English it is "I'm thinking of..." or "I plan to...". It sounds less fixed than つもり, as if the plan is still taking shape.

1. Talking about your plans

The volitional form is the "let's / I will" form; と思う frames it as an intention.

来年、日本に行こうと思う。

"I'm thinking of going to Japan next year."

  • 来年（らいねん）= next year
  • 日本（にほん）= Japan
  • に = to
  • 行こう（いこう）= (I) will go
  • と思う（とおもう）= I think

2. A plan you've had for a while

～と思っている suggests the intention has been there for some time.

新しい車を買おうと思っている。

"I've been thinking of buying a new car."

  • 新しい（あたらしい）= new
  • 車（くるま）= car
  • を = object marker
  • 買おう（かおう）= (I) will buy
  • と思っている（とおもっている）= have been thinking

How to form it

Verb volitional form + と思う

  • 書く（かく）= to write → 書こうと思う（かこうとおもう）= I'm thinking of writing
  • 見る（みる）= to watch → 見ようと思う（みようとおもう）= I'm thinking of watching
  • 引っ越す（ひっこす）= to move house → 引っ越そうと思う（ひっこそうとおもう）= I'm thinking of moving

Ru-verbs take よう (見よう), u-verbs change their last sound to the お-row plus う (書こう), する becomes しよう, 来る becomes 来よう.

Key idea

Volitional + と思う is an intention said out loud, still a little open to change.
