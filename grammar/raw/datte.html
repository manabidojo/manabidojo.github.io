だって: but / because (protest)

だって at the start of a sentence introduces an excuse or protest, usually in reply to criticism. It often ends with ～もん.

In English it is a defensive "but..." or "because...". After a noun it means "even", and at the end of a sentence it reports what someone said. All three uses are casual.

1. Defending yourself

The speaker answers a complaint by giving their reason.

だって、知らなかったんだもん。

"But I didn't know!"

  • だって = but
  • 知らなかった（しらなかった）= didn't know
  • んだ = (explanation)
  • もん = because (excuse)

2. After a noun: "even"

After a noun, だって means "even" or "too", like casual でも.

先生だって間違えることはある。

"Even teachers make mistakes sometimes."

  • 先生（せんせい）= teacher
  • だって = even
  • 間違える（まちがえる）= to make a mistake
  • ことはある = it happens

3. At the end of a sentence: "they say"

After a sentence, often as ～んだって, it passes on something the speaker heard, like a casual そうだ.

彼、来ないんだって。

"I hear he isn't coming."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • 来ない（こない）= won't come
  • んだ = (explanation)
  • って = they say

How to form it

だって + reason (+ もん／から), Noun + だって

  • だって = but → だって、疲れたんだもん（だって、つかれたんだもん）= but I'm tired!
  • 子供（こども）= child → 子供だって（こどもだって）= even a child
  • 誰（だれ）= who → 誰だって（だれだって）= anyone

Key idea

だって starts a self-defense: "but you see...". After a noun, it means "even".
