～て／で: the connective te-form

The te-form (て, or its voiced counterpart で) is a connecting form of verbs (食べて, 読んで). I-adjectives have a corresponding ～くて form (忙しくて). It often connects one action or state to what follows. Depending on context, it can express relationships like "X and then Y" or "X happened, so Y happened".

1. Linking actions in sequence

A chain of te-forms lists actions in the order they happen. Only the last verb carries the tense.

朝ご飯を食べて、学校に行った。

"I ate breakfast and went to school."

  • 朝ご飯（あさごはん）= breakfast
  • を = object marker
  • 食べて（たべて）= ate, and (te-form)
  • 学校（がっこう）= school
  • に = direction marker
  • 行った（いった）= went

2. Reason or cause

Especially with adjectives, the te-form gives a light reason: "X, so Y".

忙しくて、友達に会えなかった。

"I was busy, so I couldn't meet my friend."

  • 忙しくて（いそがしくて）= was busy, so (te-form)
  • 友達（ともだち）= friend
  • に = target marker (with)
  • 会えなかった（あえなかった）= could not meet

3. Launching pad for other patterns

Many common patterns are built on the te-form such as ～ている, ～てください, ～てもいい, and more. Whenever a helper word follows a verb, the te-form is usually the hook it hangs on.

ちょっと待ってください。

"Please wait a moment."

  • ちょっと = a moment / a little
  • 待ってください（まってください）= please wait

How to form it

The sound changes are the same as for past ～た, but with て: for godan (u-verbs), う・つ・る → って, む・ぶ・ぬ → んで, く → いて, ぐ → いで, す → して, with the exception 行く → 行って. Ichidan (ru-verbs) drop る and add て. Irregulars: する → して, 来る → 来て. I-adjectives become ～くて, and ない becomes なくて.

  • 買う（かう）= to buy → 買って（かって）= buy, and
  • 読む（よむ）= to read → 読んで（よんで）= read, and
  • 書く（かく）= to write → 書いて（かいて）= write, and
  • 泳ぐ（およぐ）= to swim → 泳いで（およいで）= swim, and
  • 話す（はなす）= to speak → 話して（はなして）= speak, and
  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行って（いって）= go, and (exception)
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べて（たべて）= eat, and
  • する = to do → して = do, and
  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来て（きて）= come, and
  • 高い（たかい）= expensive → 高くて（たかくて）= expensive, and/so
  • ない = not → なくて = not, and/so

Key idea

The te-form is Japanese's universal connector. It carries no tense of its own and simply hands the sentence on to what comes next. The ending of the sentence decides the tense for the whole chain.
