～たち／～ら: plural suffix for people

Japanese nouns usually do not mark plural at all, 子供 can mean "child" or "children". When you want to make a group explicit, the suffixes ～たち（達）and ～ら（等）do the job. They attach mainly to words for people: nouns like 子供 and 学生, and pronouns like 私 and 彼.

～たち is the neutral, safe choice in any register; ～ら sounds casual and can even be rough. Both are optional, Japanese has no mandatory plural (except for pronouns like "we").

1. Groups of people

Add ～たち to a noun for people to say explicitly that there is more than one.

子供たちは公園で遊んでいる。

"The children are playing in the park."

  • 子供たち（こどもたち）= children
  • は = topic marker
  • 公園（こうえん）= park
  • で = at (place of action)
  • 遊んでいる（あそんでいる）= are playing

2. "We" and "they": pronoun + たち／ら

On pronouns the suffixes form the everyday words for "we" and "they": 私たち, 彼ら, 僕ら. The ら versions lean casual, and お前ら is downright blunt.

彼らはもう帰りました。

"They've already gone home."

  • 彼ら（かれら）= they
  • は = topic marker
  • もう = already
  • 帰りました（かえりました）= went home

3. "X and their group"

On a person's name, ～たち usually does not mean several copies of that person, it means that person and the people with them.

田中さんたちはもう着きましたか。

"Have Tanaka and the others arrived yet?"

  • 田中さんたち（たなかさんたち）= Tanaka and his group
  • は = topic marker
  • もう = already
  • 着きましたか（つきましたか）= have (they) arrived?

～たち vs ～ら

～たち works in almost any situation, polite or plain. ～ら belongs to casual speech and to set pronoun combinations like 彼ら, 僕ら, and 君ら; with rough words like お前ら it can sound aggressive.

How to form it

Noun / pronoun for people + たち or ら

  • 私（わたし）= I → 私たち（わたしたち）= we
  • 子供（こども）= child → 子供たち（こどもたち）= children
  • 彼（かれ）= he → 彼ら（かれら）= they
  • 僕（ぼく）= I (casual, male) → 僕ら（ぼくら）= we

Key idea

Japanese plural marking is optional (except for pronouns like "we"). Reach for ～たち or ～ら when the group itself matters. たち is the all-purpose choice, ら the casual one, and on a name ～たち means "that person and the people with them".
