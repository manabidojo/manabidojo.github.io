～と思う: I think that (opinion or guess)

～と思う puts a thought in front of the verb 思う ("to think"). The particle と marks the content of the thought, much like English "that".

In English it is "I think (that)...". Japanese speakers use it a lot to soften statements, even when they are fairly sure.

1. Giving an opinion or a guess

The thought itself is in the plain form. The sentence stays about the speaker's own view.

明日は雨が降ると思う。

"I think it will rain tomorrow."

  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • は = topic marker
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • が = subject marker
  • 降る（ふる）= to fall
  • と思う（とおもう）= I think

2. What someone else thinks

For other people, Japanese uses ～と思っている ("is thinking"). Plain 思う describes the speaker's own thoughts.

兄は留学したいと思っている。

"My brother is thinking he wants to study abroad."

  • 兄（あに）= older brother
  • は = topic marker
  • 留学したい（りゅうがくしたい）= want to study abroad
  • と思っている（とおもっている）= is thinking

How to form it

Plain form + と思う (nouns and な-adjectives take だと思う)

  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来ると思う（くるとおもう）= I think (they'll) come
  • 高い（たかい）= expensive → 高いと思う（たかいとおもう）= I think it's expensive
  • 本当（ほんとう）= true → 本当だと思う（ほんとうだとおもう）= I think it's true

Key idea

と closes off the thought, 思う labels it as an opinion. It turns a flat statement into "I think so".
