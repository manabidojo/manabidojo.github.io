～なんか: things like (dismissive)

なんか after a noun gives a loose example, like a casual など. It often adds a dismissive or humble tone: the thing named is not worth much.

In English it maps to "things like", "stuff like" or a disdainful "(who cares about)...".

1. Looking down on something

The speaker shows that they don't value the thing.

宿題なんかやりたくない。

"I don't want to do stuff like homework."

  • 宿題（しゅくだい）= homework
  • なんか = stuff like
  • やりたくない = don't want to do

2. Being humble about yourself

About the speaker, it sounds modest: "someone like me".

私なんかにはできないよ。

"Someone like me couldn't do that."

  • 私（わたし）= I, me
  • なんか = someone like
  • には = for
  • できない = can't do
  • よ = (you know)

3. As a filler in speech

Like English "like" or "kind of", なんか also works as a filler that softens what follows or shows the speaker is unsure.

なんか、今日は疲れたな。

"I'm kind of tired today."

  • なんか = kind of
  • 今日（きょう）= today
  • は = topic marker
  • 疲れた（つかれた）= got tired
  • な = (I guess)

How to form it

Noun + なんか

  • 雨（あめ）= rain → 雨なんか（あめなんか）= (who cares about) rain
  • お前（おまえ）= you → お前なんか（おまえなんか）= someone like you
  • 映画（えいが）= movie → 映画なんかどう？（えいがなんかどう？）= how about a movie or something?

Key idea

なんか makes the thing named sound small: just an example, or not worth much.
