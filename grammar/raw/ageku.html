～あげく: after all that, it ended badly

～あげく means that after a prolonged effort or a lot of trouble, things ended up in a (usually bad) result. English equivalents are "after all that ..." or "... and in the end".

This grammar highlights the final outcome of a long process, often with the feeling that the effort, worry, or trouble was not worth it.

The first clause describes something long and draining like arguing or waiting. Unlike ～末（に）, which is neutral about the outcome, ～あげく typically introduces a disappointing or regrettable ending.

1. A long struggle with a deflating conclusion

The result in the second clause feels like a letdown after everything that came before it.

さんざん悩んだあげく、やめることにした。

"After agonizing over it, I ended up quitting."

  • さんざん = a great deal, endlessly
  • 悩んだ（なやんだ）= agonized, worried
  • あげく = after all that, in the end
  • やめることにした = decided to quit

2. Wasted time and effort

～あげく is perfect for stories where all the effort came to nothing.

何時間も迷ったあげく、何も買わずに帰った。

"After hours of dithering, I went home without buying anything."

  • 何時間も（なんじかんも）= for hours (and hours)
  • 迷った（まよった）= dithered, couldn't decide
  • あげく = after all that
  • 何も（なにも）= nothing (with a negative verb)
  • 買わずに（かわずに）= without buying
  • 帰った（かえった）= went home

How to form it

Verb た-form + あげく（に） / Noun + のあげく

  • 悩む（なやむ）= to agonize → 悩んだあげく（なやんだあげく）= after agonizing
  • 迷う（まよう）= to waver → 迷ったあげく（まよったあげく）= after wavering
  • 口論（こうろん）= quarrel → 口論のあげく（こうろんのあげく）= after a quarrel

Key idea

～あげく is the sigh at the end of a long ordeal: after all that time and trouble, this (usually something disappointing) is how it turned out.
