～でも: or something (soft example)

でも after a noun gives a loose, soft example. The thing named is just one possibility, so suggestions sound casual and not pushy.

In English it is "...or something". After question words the same でも means "any", and in some sentences it means "even".

1. Soft suggestions and offers

The listener doesn't feel pressured, because any similar option would be fine.

お茶でも飲みませんか。

"Would you like some tea or something?"

  • お茶（おちゃ）= tea
  • でも = or something
  • 飲みませんか（のみませんか）= won't you drink?

2. With question words: "any"

After 何, 誰, いつ or どこ, でも means "any" or "every".

何でもいいよ。

"Anything is fine."

  • 何でも（なんでも）= anything
  • いい = good, fine
  • よ = (you know)

3. After a noun: "even"

In some sentences, でも after a noun means "even", like a casual さえ: even this person or thing can do it.

この問題は子供でも分かる。

"Even a child could understand this problem."

  • この = this
  • 問題（もんだい）= problem
  • は = topic marker
  • 子供（こども）= child
  • でも = even
  • 分かる（わかる）= to understand

How to form it

Noun + でも, Question word + でも

  • 映画（えいが）= movie → 映画でも（えいがでも）= a movie or something
  • 誰（だれ）= who → 誰でも（だれでも）= anyone
  • いつ = when → いつでも = anytime

Don't confuse it with the て-form + も ending, as in 読んでも ("even if I read").

Key idea

Noun + でも keeps an offer light: this is just an example, anything similar is fine too.
