～てはじめて: only after (did I realize)

はじめて means "for the first time". After a て-form, ～てはじめて says that something was realized or became possible only after an experience.

In English it is "only after..." or "it wasn't until... that...". The second clause usually involves understanding, noticing or realizing.

Understanding that comes through experience

The experience opens the speaker's eyes: some things can't be understood until you've been through them.

病気になってはじめて健康の大切さが分かった。

"Only after I got sick did I understand how important health is."

  • 病気になって（びょうきになって）= got sick
  • はじめて = only then
  • 健康（けんこう）= health
  • の = ('s)
  • 大切さ（たいせつさ）= importance
  • 分かった（わかった）= understood

一人暮らしをしてはじめて、親のありがたさに気づいた。

"It wasn't until I lived alone that I realized how much my parents did for me."

  • 一人暮らし（ひとりぐらし）= living alone
  • をして = did
  • はじめて = only then
  • 親（おや）= parents
  • ありがたさ = gratitude, value
  • に気づいた（にきづいた）= realized

How to form it

Verb て-form + はじめて

  • 失う（うしなう）= to lose → 失ってはじめて（うしなってはじめて）= only after losing it
  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行ってはじめて（いってはじめて）= only after going
  • 親になる（おやになる）= to become a parent → 親になってはじめて（おやになってはじめて）= only after becoming a parent

Key idea

～てはじめて says that understanding came late, and only through experience.
