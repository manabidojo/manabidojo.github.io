～（よ）うが／～（よ）うと: no matter what / however much

The volitional form plus が or と means that nothing changes the outcome, no matter what happens or what someone does.

In English it is "no matter (what / how / who)..." or "even if...". It often comes with a question word, or as a pair: ～ようが～まいが.

1. With a question word

Whatever the variable is, the result stays the same.

誰が何と言おうと、私は行く。

"No matter what anyone says, I'm going."

  • 誰が（だれが）= whoever
  • 何と（なんと）= whatever
  • 言おうと（いおうと）= no matter (they) say
  • 私（わたし）= I
  • は = topic marker
  • 行く（いく）= to go

2. Whether or not

The pair ～ようが～まいが means "whether or not".

雨が降ろうが降るまいが、試合は行われる。

"Whether it rains or not, the game will be held."

  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • が = subject marker
  • 降ろうが（ふろうが）= whether (it) falls
  • 降るまいが（ふるまいが）= or not
  • 試合（しあい）= game
  • は = topic marker
  • 行われる（おこなわれる）= will be held

How to form it

Verb volitional form + が／と, い-adjective (drop い) + かろうが, Noun / な-adjective + だろうが

  • 反対する（はんたいする）= to oppose → 反対されようが（はんたいされようが）= no matter how much (I'm) opposed
  • 高い（たかい）= expensive → 高かろうが（たかかろうが）= however expensive
  • 子供（こども）= child → 子供だろうが（こどもだろうが）= even if (it's) a child

Key idea

～ようが shuts out every possibility: whatever happens, the decision stands.
