～くなる: becoming a different state

～くなる describes a change of state with i-adjectives. Take an i-adjective, change the final い to く, and add なる (to become).

In English it matches "to become ~" or "to get ~": to get cold, to become fun, to get better.

1. A simple change

Use ～くなる to say that something changed from one state to another. The thing that changes is usually marked with が.

日本語が楽しくなりました。

"Japanese has become fun."

  • 日本語（にほんご）= Japanese (language)
  • が = subject marker
  • 楽しくなりました（たのしくなりました）= became fun

2. A gradual, ongoing change

To show that a change has been happening over time and is still continuing, combine it with ～てくる: ～くなってきた means "has been getting ~".

最近、寒くなってきた。

"It's been getting cold lately."

  • 最近（さいきん）= lately, recently
  • 寒くなってきた（さむくなってきた）= has been getting cold (寒くなる plus ～てくる in the past)

How to form it

i-adjective (い → く) + なる. For na-adjectives and nouns, use になる instead (静かになる = to become quiet).

  • 寒い（さむい）= cold → 寒くなる（さむくなる）= to get cold
  • 高い（たかい）= high, expensive → 高くなる（たかくなる）= to go up, get more expensive
  • いい = good → よくなる = to get better (irregular: いい changes to よく)

Key idea

なる means "to become", and the く-form lets an i-adjective attach to it. Whenever something turns colder, bigger, easier, or better, Japanese says it with ～くなる.
