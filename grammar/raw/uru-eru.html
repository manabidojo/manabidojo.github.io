～うる / ～える: can conceivably happen

～うる / ～える (written 得る) attaches to the ます-stem of a verb and means that the event can possibly happen or is conceivable: 起こります becomes 起こりうる, "can occur". It is a formal, written-style potential.

Unlike the ordinary potential form (読める, "someone is able to read"), this pattern is not about a person's ability. It states an objective possibility: "it could happen", "it is conceivable".

1. Stating that something is possible

The affirmative form asserts that an outcome lies within the realm of possibility.

それは十分にあり得る話だ。

"That's a perfectly conceivable story."

  • それ = that
  • は = topic marker
  • 十分に（じゅうぶんに）= fully, perfectly well
  • あり得る（ありえる）= can happen, is conceivable (also read ありうる)
  • 話（はなし）= story
  • だ = copula (is)

2. The negative ～えない: impossible

The negative is always ～えない, never うない. あり得ない, "impossible, cannot be", is so common it appears even in casual speech as an exclamation.

そんな話はあり得ない。

"A story like that is impossible."

  • そんな = that kind of
  • 話（はなし）= story
  • は = topic marker
  • あり得ない（ありえない）= impossible, cannot be

A note on reading: the dictionary form can be read うる (more literary) or える, but conjugated forms use え only — あり得ない (ありえない), あり得ます (ありえます).

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + うる / える

  • ある = to exist → あり得る（ありうる）= can happen
  • 起こる（おこる）= to occur → 起こり得る（おこりうる）= can occur
  • 考える（かんがえる）= to think → 考え得る（かんがえうる）= conceivable

Key idea

～うる / ～える measures possibility, not skill. It says an event is within the realm of what could happen. If you mean a person can do something, use the regular potential form. If you mean "such a thing could occur", use ～うる.
