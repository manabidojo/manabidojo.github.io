～ばかり: just did / nothing but

～ばかり has two main jobs, and the form around it tells you which one is in play. After the た-form of a verb, it means an action just happened. After a noun (or in the separate grammar pattern ～てばかりいる), it means "nothing but": someone does one thing to the exclusion of everything else.

1. Just happened: た-form + ばかり

Use this to say that an action finished only a short time ago. The speaker still feels how recent it is.

今着いたばかりです。

"I just arrived."

  • 今（いま）= now, just now
  • 着いた（ついた）= arrived
  • ばかり = just (happened)
  • です = polite "to be"

2. Nothing but: Noun + ばかり

After a noun, ばかり says that one thing fills the whole picture, often with a critical tone. The pattern て-form + ばかりいる works the same way for actions (e.g. 泣いてばかりいる（ないてばかりいる）= does nothing but cry).

弟はゲームばかりしています。

"My little brother does nothing but play games."

  • 弟（おとうと）= little brother
  • は = topic marker
  • ゲーム = games
  • ばかり = nothing but
  • しています = is doing

How to form it

Verb た-form + ばかり (just did) / Noun + ばかり (nothing but)

  • 着く（つく）= to arrive → 着いたばかり（ついたばかり）= just arrived
  • 始まる（はじまる）= to begin → 始まったばかり（はじまったばかり）= just began
  • ゲーム = games → ゲームばかり = nothing but games

Key idea

Check what comes right before ばかり: a た-form means "this happened only a moment ago", while a noun or て-form means "this one thing, and far too much of it".
