～げ: the outward look of a feeling

～げ attaches to the stem of an adjective, most often one describing an emotion, and creates a な-adjective meaning "looking ~". From 寂しい (lonely) you get 寂しげ, "lonely-looking".

The point of ～げ is that you cannot see inside another person's heart: you infer their feeling from their face, voice, or behavior. English equivalents are "-looking" and "seemingly".

1. Describing a noun: ～げな + noun

Before a noun, use ～げな, like any な-adjective.

彼は寂しげな顔をしていた。

"He had a lonely-looking expression."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 寂しげな（さびしげな）= lonely-looking
  • 顔（かお）= face, expression
  • を = object marker
  • していた = was wearing, had (past of している)

2. Describing an action: ～げに + verb

With ～げに, the apparent feeling colors the way an action is performed.

子供たちは楽しげに遊んでいた。

"The children were playing with an air of enjoyment."

  • 子供たち（こどもたち）= children
  • は = topic marker
  • 楽しげに（たのしげに）= looking like they were having fun
  • 遊んでいた（あそんでいた）= were playing

Because ～げ is about observed appearance, it is typically used for other people, not for stating your own feelings directly. It has a somewhat written, literary flavor and is common in novels and descriptions of scenes.

How to form it

Adjective stem + げ (the result is a な-adjective)

  • 寂しい（さびしい）= lonely → 寂しげ（さびしげ）= lonely-looking
  • 悲しい（かなしい）= sad → 悲しげ（かなしげ）= sad-looking
  • 不安（ふあん）= anxious → 不安げ（ふあんげ）= anxious-looking

Key idea

～げ paints the outside of an inner state: not "he is lonely" but "he looks lonely". Reach for it when describing the feelings you read on someone else's face.
