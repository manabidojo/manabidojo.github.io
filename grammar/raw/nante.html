～なんて: such a thing as ...

～なんて attaches to a noun or a plain clause and colors it with the speaker's emotion, usually dismissal ("something like ...") or surprise ("to think that ...!"). It often replaces the particles は or を.

English equivalents include "something like", "such a thing as", or an exclamation like "to think that ...!". It belongs to casual speech.

1. Dismissing or belittling something

Before a noun, なんて treats it as unimportant, unpleasant, or not worth taking seriously.

勉強なんてしたくない。

"I don't want to do something like studying."

  • 勉強（べんきょう）= studying
  • なんて = something like (dismissive)
  • したくない = don't want to do

2. Surprise or disbelief about a whole clause

After a plain clause, なんて presents the event as something unexpected or hard to believe.

ここで友達に会うなんて、びっくりした。

"What a surprise to run into a friend here!"

  • ここで = here (で = place of action)
  • 友達（ともだち）= friend
  • に = target of 会う (meet with)
  • 会う（あう）= to meet, to run into
  • なんて = (to think) such a thing as
  • びっくりした = was surprised

Unlike ～って, which marks a topic or quote neutrally, なんて always adds an emotional charge.

How to form it

Noun + なんて or plain clause + なんて (it usually replaces は or を)

  • 勉強（べんきょう）= studying → 勉強なんて（べんきょうなんて）= something like studying
  • 泣く（なく）= to cry → 泣くなんて（なくなんて）= (the very idea of) crying
  • テスト = test → テストなんて = a mere test

Key idea

なんて does not change the facts of the sentence, it broadcasts how the speaker feels about them: "studying? ugh" or "he came? no way!".
