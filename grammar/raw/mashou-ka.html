～ましょうか: shall I? / shall we?

Adding か to ～ましょう turns "let's" into a question. The result is either an offer to do something for the listener or a suggestion that asks for their opinion.

In English it maps to "shall I...?" or "shall we...?". Which one is meant depends on who would do the action.

1. Offering to help

When the action helps the listener, ～ましょうか is a polite offer.

荷物を持ちましょうか。

"Shall I carry your bag?"

  • 荷物（にもつ）= bag, luggage
  • を = object marker
  • 持ちましょうか（もちましょうか）= shall I carry?

2. Suggesting something to do together

When both people would do it, it means "shall we?" and checks whether the other person agrees.

ちょっと休みましょうか。

"Shall we take a little break?"

  • ちょっと = a little
  • 休みましょうか（やすみましょうか）= shall we rest?

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + ましょうか

  • 開ける（あける）= to open → 開けましょうか（あけましょうか）= shall I open it?
  • 手伝う（てつだう）= to help → 手伝いましょうか（てつだいましょうか）= shall I help?
  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行きましょうか（いきましょうか）= shall we go?

The casual version uses the volitional form: 持とうか？ (shall I carry it?)

Key idea

～ましょうか asks before acting: "shall I do this for you?" or "shall we do this?". Context tells you which.
