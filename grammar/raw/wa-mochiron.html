～はもちろん／～はもとより: not to mention / of course

もちろん means "of course". ～はもちろん names something obviously included, then adds that something else is included too. ～はもとより means the same and is more formal.

In English it is "not to mention..." or "...of course, and also...".

1. The obvious and something more

The first item is a given, the second extends it.

彼は英語はもちろん、中国語も話せる。

"He speaks Chinese, not to mention English."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 英語（えいご）= English
  • はもちろん = not to mention
  • 中国語（ちゅうごくご）= Chinese
  • も = also
  • 話せる（はなせる）= can speak

2. Formal statements

はもとより appears in formal writing and speeches.

この店は地元の人はもとより、観光客にも人気がある。

"This shop is popular with tourists, to say nothing of the locals."

  • この = this
  • 店（みせ）= shop
  • 地元の人（じもとのひと）= locals
  • はもとより = to say nothing of
  • 観光客（かんこうきゃく）= tourists
  • にも = with also
  • 人気がある（にんきがある）= is popular

How to form it

Noun + はもちろん／はもとより + noun + も／まで

  • 子供（こども）= children → 子供はもちろん（こどもはもちろん）= children of course
  • 平日（へいじつ）= weekdays → 平日はもちろん（へいじつはもちろん）= weekdays of course
  • 国内（こくない）= domestic → 国内はもとより（こくないはもとより）= at home, to say nothing of

Key idea

～はもちろん takes the obvious case for granted and then reaches further.
