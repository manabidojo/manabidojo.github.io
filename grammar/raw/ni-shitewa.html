～にしては: surprisingly good (or bad) for ...

～にしては takes a fact as a standard and then points out that the reality does not match the expectation that standard creates. It attaches to nouns or plain-form verbs and corresponds to English "for ..." or "considering ...", as in "good, for a beginner".

1. Noun + にしては

The noun sets the expectation and the main clause reports something that deviates from it, in either a positive or a negative direction.

初めてにしては上手だ。

"You're good, for a first-timer."

  • 初めて（はじめて）= the first time
  • にしては = for, considering
  • 上手（じょうず）= skilled, good at it
  • だ = plain copula (is)

今日は冬にしては暖かい。

"It's warm today, for winter."

  • 今日（きょう）= today
  • は = topic marker
  • 冬（ふゆ）= winter
  • 暖かい（あたたかい）= warm

2. Verb (plain form) + にしては

With a verb, the clause before にしては describes what happened, and the main clause notes that the outcome is unexpected given that.

この字は、子供が書いたにしては上手だ。

"This handwriting is good, considering a child wrote it."

  • この字（このじ）= this handwriting, these characters
  • 子供（こども）= child
  • が = subject marker
  • 書いた（かいた）= wrote
  • 上手（じょうず）= skilled, well done

Note the contrast with ～にしても: にしても concedes a point and pushes past it ("even if it's expensive"), while にしては holds reality up against an expectation and reports the mismatch.

How to form it

Noun + にしては / Verb (plain form) + にしては

  • 初めて（はじめて）= the first time → 初めてにしては（はじめてにしては）= for a first time
  • 冬（ふゆ）= winter → 冬にしては（ふゆにしては）= for winter
  • 書いた（かいた）= wrote → 書いたにしては（かいたにしては）= considering (someone) wrote it

Key idea

～にしては means "given that X, you'd expect one thing, but the reality is different". X sets the bar, and the main clause lands above or below it.
