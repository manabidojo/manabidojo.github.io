～ということだ: I hear that / that means

～ということだ has two common uses: passing on information the speaker heard, and drawing a conclusion from what was said.

In English the first is "I hear that..." or "apparently..."; the second is "that means..." or "so in other words...".

1. Passing on information

It reports news or announcements in a neutral, slightly formal way.

来月から値段が上がるということだ。

"I hear prices are going up from next month."

  • 来月（らいげつ）= next month
  • から = from
  • 値段（ねだん）= price
  • 上がる（あがる）= to rise
  • ということだ = I hear that

2. Drawing a conclusion

It sums up what something means.

返事がないということは、来ないということだね。

"No reply means he isn't coming, right?"

  • 返事（へんじ）= reply
  • ない = there is no
  • ということは = the fact that
  • 来ない（こない）= won't come
  • ということだ = it means
  • ね = right?

How to form it

Plain form + ということだ

  • 中止（ちゅうし）= canceled → 中止だということだ（ちゅうしだということだ）= I hear it's canceled
  • 結婚する（けっこんする）= to marry → 結婚するということだ（けっこんするということだ）= I hear they're getting married
  • 無理（むり）= impossible → 無理ということだ（むりということだ）= that means it's impossible

In casual speech, ～ってこと is common: つまり、来ないってこと？ (so he isn't coming?).

Key idea

～ということだ either passes on what was heard or spells out what it means.
