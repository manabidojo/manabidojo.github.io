～っぽい: -ish / -like / tends to

～っぽい is a casual suffix that turns a noun or a verb stem into an い-adjective meaning "has the qualities of" or "tends to".

It works like English "-ish" (childish) or "-y" (watery). Once formed, the new word conjugates like any い-adjective: 子供っぽくない, 子供っぽかった.

1. Noun + っぽい: -ish, like

Attached to a noun, っぽい says something has the look or feel of that noun, often with a slightly critical tone when describing people.

彼は子供っぽい。

"He's childish."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 子供っぽい（こどもっぽい）= childish

このスープは水っぽい。

"This soup is watery."

  • この = this
  • スープ = soup
  • は = topic marker
  • 水っぽい（みずっぽい）= watery

2. Verb stem + っぽい: tends to

Attached to the ます-stem of certain verbs, っぽい describes a habitual tendency, as in 忘れっぽい (forgetful) or 飽きっぽい (quick to get bored).

父は最近、忘れっぽくなった。

"My father has become forgetful lately."

  • 父（ちち）= (my) father
  • は = topic marker
  • 最近（さいきん）= lately
  • 忘れっぽくなった（わすれっぽくなった）= has become forgetful

Be careful to distinguish ～っぽい from ～らしい: 子供っぽい means childish in a way that does not suit the person (a criticism of an adult), while 子供らしい means childlike in exactly the way a child should be (usually positive).

How to form it

Noun / Verb ます-stem + っぽい

  • 子供（こども）= child → 子供っぽい（こどもっぽい）= childish
  • 水（みず）= water → 水っぽい（みずっぽい）= watery
  • 忘れる（わすれる）= to forget → 忘れっぽい（わすれっぽい）= forgetful

Key idea

～っぽい paints something with the color of another word: not the real thing itself, but strongly tinted with its qualities, "-ish", "-y", or "prone to".
