～すら: even (formal emphasis)

すら after a noun gives an extreme example to stress how far a situation goes. It is the formal, written counterpart of さえ.

In English it is "even". It is usually used with negative sentences: not even the most basic thing.

1. Not even the basics

The example is so basic that lacking it shows how extreme the situation is.

忙しくて、寝る時間すらない。

"I'm so busy I don't even have time to sleep."

  • 忙しくて（いそがしくて）= busy (and)
  • 寝る（ねる）= to sleep
  • 時間（じかん）= time
  • すら = even
  • ない = there is no

2. Even an expert

It can also name a surprising person or thing.

その問題は専門家ですら解けなかった。

"Even the experts couldn't solve that problem."

  • その = that
  • 問題（もんだい）= problem
  • は = topic marker
  • 専門家（せんもんか）= experts
  • ですら = even
  • 解けなかった（とけなかった）= couldn't solve

How to form it

Noun + すら (with a particle: ですら, にすら)

  • 名前（なまえ）= name → 名前すら（なまえすら）= not even (my) name
  • 子供（こども）= child → 子供ですら（こどもですら）= even a child
  • 水（みず）= water → 水すら飲めない（みずすらのめない）= can't even drink water

Key idea

すら picks the most basic or least likely case to show how far things have gone: not even this.
