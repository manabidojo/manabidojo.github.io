～ませんか: polite invitation (won't you?)

～ませんか is the polite negative of a verb plus the question particle か. Asking "won't you do it?" turns the sentence into a soft, polite invitation.

In English it maps to "won't you...?" or "would you like to...?". Because the question is negative, the listener can turn it down easily, which is why it sounds gentler than a direct "let's".

1. Inviting someone politely

Put the activity you want to share before ～ませんか. It is the standard way to invite a classmate, colleague or someone you don't know well.

一緒に映画を見ませんか。

"Won't you watch a movie with me?"

  • 一緒に（いっしょに）= together
  • 映画（えいが）= movie
  • を = object marker
  • 見ませんか（みませんか）= won't you watch?

2. Offering something

The same ending works for offering food, drinks or a seat. でも ("or something") often softens it even further.

コーヒーでも飲みませんか。

"Would you like some coffee or something?"

  • コーヒー = coffee
  • でも = or something
  • 飲みませんか（のみませんか）= won't you drink?

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + ませんか

  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行きませんか（いきませんか）= won't you go?
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べませんか（たべませんか）= won't you eat?
  • 休む（やすむ）= to rest → 休みませんか（やすみませんか）= won't you take a break?

In casual speech, the plain negative with a rising tone does the same job: 行かない？ (want to go?)

Key idea

～ませんか invites by asking. The negative question leaves the decision with the listener, which makes it the politest everyday way to say "let's".
