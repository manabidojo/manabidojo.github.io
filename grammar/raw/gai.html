～がい: worth doing

～がい attaches to the ます-stem of a verb and creates a noun meaning the worth or reward of doing that action: やります becomes やりがい, "the sense that the work is worth doing".

English has no single equivalent; sentences with ～がいがある usually translate as "rewarding" or "worth doing". Note that unlike ～にくい, which builds an adjective, ～がい builds a noun, so it takes particles like が and の.

1. ～がいがある / ～がいがない

The most common frame: say the activity has (or lacks) reward value with がある or がない.

この仕事はやりがいがある。

"This job is fulfilling."

  • この = this
  • 仕事（しごと）= job, work
  • は = topic marker
  • やりがい = the worth of doing it, sense of fulfillment
  • が = subject marker
  • ある = there is, to have

2. As an independent noun

Some ～がい words are everyday vocabulary in their own right. The most famous is 生きがい, "what makes life worth living".

孫の成長が彼女の生きがいだ。

"Her grandchild's growth is what makes her life worth living."

  • 孫（まご）= grandchild
  • の = possessive marker
  • 成長（せいちょう）= growth
  • が = subject marker
  • 彼女（かのじょ）= she
  • 生きがい（いきがい）= reason for living
  • だ = copula (is)

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + がい (the result is a noun)

  • やる = to do → やりがい = the worth of doing it
  • 生きる（いきる）= to live → 生きがい（いきがい）= reason for living
  • 働く（はたらく）= to work → 働きがい（はたらきがい）= the reward of working

Key idea

～がい names the payoff your effort earns. If doing something gives you a sense that the effort was worthwhile, the activity has ～がい; if the effort feels wasted, it is ～がいがない.
