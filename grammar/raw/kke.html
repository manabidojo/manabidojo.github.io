～っけ: was it? (trying to remember)

っけ at the end of a sentence asks about something the speaker has forgotten or wants to confirm. It is very common in casual conversation.

In English it is "...again?" or "was it...?", as in "what was his name again?".

1. Trying to recall something

The speaker knew the answer once and is checking their memory.

あの人、名前何だっけ？

"What was that guy's name again?"

  • あの = that
  • 人（ひと）= person
  • 名前（なまえ）= name
  • 何（なん）= what
  • だっけ = was it again?

2. Checking a detail

It also confirms a plan or fact the speaker is unsure about.

明日の会議、何時からだったっけ？

"What time does tomorrow's meeting start again?"

  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • の = ('s)
  • 会議（かいぎ）= meeting
  • 何時から（なんじから）= from what time
  • だったっけ = was it again?

How to form it

だっけ／だったっけ after nouns, Verb た-form + っけ

  • 誰（だれ）= who → 誰だっけ（だれだっけ）= who was it again?
  • 言った（いった）= said → 言ったっけ（いったっけ）= did I say that?
  • どこ = where → どこだったっけ = where was it again?

In polite speech, でしたっけ is used: お名前は何でしたっけ.

Key idea

っけ is the sound of searching your memory: "what was it again?"
