～ことができる: being able to do something

～ことができる is the basic way to say "can" or "be able to" in Japanese. Take the dictionary form of a verb and add ことができる: こと turns the action into a "thing", and できる means "is possible".

Word for word, 話すことができる is like saying "the act of speaking is possible", in natural English, simply "can speak".

1. Ability

Use ～ことができる to talk about a skill someone has.

日本語を話すことができます。

"I can speak Japanese."

  • 日本語（にほんご）= Japanese (language)
  • を = object marker (marks what is spoken)
  • 話す（はなす）= to speak
  • ことができます = can, am able to

2. Possibility

The same pattern also says that something is possible in a place or under certain conditions, not just personal skill.

ここで切符を買うことができます。

"You can buy tickets here."

  • ここ = here
  • で = marker for the place where an action happens
  • 切符（きっぷ）= ticket
  • を = object marker
  • 買う（かう）= to buy
  • ことができます = can, is possible

To say you cannot do something, change できます to できません. Japanese also has a shorter potential form (話せる = can speak), but ～ことができる is clear, works with every verb, and sounds slightly more formal.

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + ことができる

  • 話す（はなす）= to speak → 話すことができる（はなすことができる）= can speak
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べることができる（たべることができる）= can eat
  • 泳ぐ（およぐ）= to swim → 泳ぐことができる（およぐことができる）= can swim

Key idea

～ことができる wraps an action in こと to make it a "thing", then says that thing is possible, whether because of someone's skill or because the situation allows it.
