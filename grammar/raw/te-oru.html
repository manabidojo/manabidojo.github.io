～ておる: humble or old-fashioned ている

～ておる is made by taking the て-form of a verb and adding おる, a humble or formal equivalent of いる. The meaning is the same as ～ている ("is doing" or a resulting state) only the level of politeness changes.

You will hear it constantly in customer service and business Japanese as ～ております, and you will also meet it in dialects and old-fashioned speech.

1. Humble speech (talking about yourself or your group)

In polite settings, use ～ております instead of ～ています to speak modestly about your own actions, or those of your company or family.

お待ちしております。

"I am (humbly) waiting for you." / "We look forward to seeing you."

  • お待ち（おまち）= waiting (humble form of 待つ, to wait)
  • して = doing (て-form of する)
  • おります = polite form of おる, the humble version of いる

2. Dialect and old-fashioned speech

In western Japan and in literary or old-fashioned style, ～ておる simply replaces ～ている with no humble meaning. In fiction it is a typical way to mark elderly speakers.

おじいさんは昔のことをよく覚えておる。

"Grandpa remembers the old days well."

  • おじいさん = grandfather, old man
  • は = topic marker
  • 昔（むかし）= old days, long ago
  • の = linking particle (of)
  • こと = things, matters
  • を = object marker
  • よく = well
  • 覚えておる（おぼえておる）= remembers

How to form it

Verb て-form + おる（in polite speech, ～ております）

  • 待つ（まつ）= to wait → 待っております（まっております）= am waiting (humble)
  • する = to do → しております = am doing (humble)
  • 知る（しる）= to know → 知っております（しっております）= know (humble)

Key idea

～ておる means the same thing as ～ている. What changes is the register: as ～ております it is humble and polite, while plain ～ておる is often dialectal, literary, or old-fashioned.
