～そうだ: I hear that / reportedly

～そうだ reports information you got from another source, something you heard, read, or were told. It attaches to the plain form of a verb, adjective, or noun + だ.

In English it corresponds to "I hear that ...", "they say ...", or "reportedly". The speaker is passing the information along, not vouching for it personally.

1. Reporting something you heard

Put そうだ／そうです after a plain-form sentence to mark it as secondhand information.

彼は来るそうだ。

"I hear he's coming."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 来る（くる）= to come / will come
  • そうだ = I hear (hearsay marker)

2. Naming the source with によると

～そうだ often pairs with ～によると ("according to ...") to say where the information came from.

ニュースによると、明日は雨が降るそうです。

"According to the news, it's going to rain tomorrow."

  • ニュース = news
  • によると = according to
  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • は = topic marker
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • が = subject marker
  • 降る（ふる）= to fall (of rain)
  • そうです = I hear / reportedly

Hearsay ～そうだ vs appearance ～そうだ

Japanese has a second そうだ that attaches to the verb stem and means "looks like": 雨が降りそうだ = "it looks like rain" (your own observation), while 雨が降るそうだ = "I hear it will rain" (someone told you). The plain form before そうだ is what signals hearsay.

How to form it

Plain form + そうだ／そうです

  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来るそうだ（くるそうだ）= I hear (he) is coming
  • 高い（たかい）= expensive → 高いそうです（たかいそうです）= I hear it is expensive
  • 学生だ（がくせいだ）= is a student → 学生だそうだ（がくせいだそうだ）= I hear (she) is a student

Key idea

～そうだ after a plain form is a quotation of the world: you are relaying what you heard or read, not giving your own impression.
