～ない: the plain way to say "not"

～ない is the plain negative of verbs: 食べる (to eat) becomes 食べない (do not eat). It is the casual counterpart of polite ～ません, and it covers both present and future: "don't" and "won't".

1. Saying what you do not (or will not) do

Attach ない to the negative stem of the verb. Words like 何も (nothing) need a negative verb to complete their meaning.

今日は何も食べない。

"I'm not eating anything today."

  • 今日（きょう）= today
  • は = topic marker
  • 何も（なにも）= nothing, (not) anything
  • 食べない（たべない）= will not eat, am not eating

2. ない conjugates like an i-adjective

Once a verb is in the ない-form, the ending behaves like an i-adjective: the past is ～なかった (did not), and the て-form is ～なくて.

昨日は勉強しなかった。

"I didn't study yesterday."

  • 昨日（きのう）= yesterday
  • は = topic marker
  • 勉強しなかった（べんきょうしなかった）= did not study (past of 勉強しない)

How to form it

う-verbs: change the final -u to -a + ない (verbs ending in う take わ: 買う → 買わない). る-verbs: drop る + ない. Irregular: する → しない, 来る → 来ない.

  • 書く（かく）= to write → 書かない（かかない）= do not write
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べない（たべない）= do not eat
  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来ない（こない）= do not come

Key idea

ない is the plain "not" of Japanese. Attach it to the negative stem, and remember that from then on it conjugates like an i-adjective — so "didn't" is なかった, not a new verb form.
