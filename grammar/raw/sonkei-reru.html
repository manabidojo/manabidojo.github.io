～（ら）れる（尊敬）: honorific (looks like the passive)

The passive endings ～れる／～られる have a second job: they can show respect for the person who does the action. The verb looks passive, but the meaning is active.

There is no English equivalent; the sentence simply becomes more respectful. It is lighter than お～になる and very common in offices and the news.

1. A light honorific

The subject is a respected person, and they are the one doing the action.

先生はもう帰られました。

"The teacher has already left."

  • 先生（せんせい）= teacher
  • は = topic marker
  • もう = already
  • 帰られました（かえられました）= left (honorific)

2. Telling it apart from the passive

In a real passive, someone is affected by another person's action. If a respected subject simply does something themselves, it is the honorific use.

部長は毎朝新聞を読まれる。

"The department head reads the newspaper every morning."

  • 部長（ぶちょう）= department head
  • は = topic marker
  • 毎朝（まいあさ）= every morning
  • 新聞（しんぶん）= newspaper
  • を = object marker
  • 読まれる（よまれる）= reads (honorific)

How to form it

Same form as the passive: u-verb ない-stem + れる, ru-verb stem + られる

  • 話す（はなす）= to speak → 話される（はなされる）= speak (honorific)
  • 出かける（でかける）= to go out → 出かけられる（でかけられる）= go out (honorific)
  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来られる（こられる）= come (honorific)

する becomes される: 先生が説明される (the teacher explains).

Key idea

～れる／～られる can mean "was done to" or "does, respectfully". A respected subject acting on their own is the honorific use.
