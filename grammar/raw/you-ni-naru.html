～ようになる: come to do / become able to do

～ようになる is made by adding ようになる to the dictionary form of a verb. なる means "to become", so the pattern describes a gradual change: something that was not true before is true now.

In English it maps to "come to", "start to", or, with a potential verb, "become able to".

1. Change in ability (potential verb + ようになる)

This pattern very often follows a potential verb (can-form) to show that an ability developed over time.

日本語が話せるようになりました。

"I have become able to speak Japanese."

  • 日本語（にほんご）= Japanese (language)
  • が = subject marker (marks what you can do with potential verbs)
  • 話せる（はなせる）= can speak (potential form of 話す, to speak)
  • ようになりました = have become able to

2. Change in habit or behavior

With an ordinary verb, ～ようになる means that someone started doing something they did not do before.

毎朝早く起きるようになりました。

"I have started waking up early every morning."

  • 毎朝（まいあさ）= every morning
  • 早く（はやく）= early
  • 起きる（おきる）= to wake up
  • ようになりました = have come to (do)

While ～ようになる describes a change that happens to you, ～ようにする describes a deliberate effort you make to bring the change about.

How to form it

Verb dictionary form (often the potential form) + ようになる

  • 話す（はなす）= to speak → 話せるようになる（はなせるようになる）= become able to speak
  • 読む（よむ）= to read → 読めるようになる（よめるようになる）= become able to read
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べるようになる（たべるようになる）= come to eat / start eating

Key idea

～ようになる marks the crossing of a line: before, you did not or could not do it; now you do or can. The change is usually gradual, not a single event.
