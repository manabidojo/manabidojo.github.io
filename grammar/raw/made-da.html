～までだ: will simply (if it comes to it) / just

～までだ at the end of a sentence has two uses. After a dictionary form, it says that if it comes to it, the speaker will simply do this as a last resort. After a た-form, it says that the speaker only did something, nothing more.

In English the first is "I'll just..." or "then I'll simply..."; the second is "I only..." or "that's all".

1. A calm last resort

The speaker is ready to take the next step if the first plan fails.

だめなら、もう一度やるまでだ。

"If it doesn't work, I'll just do it again."

  • だめなら = if it's no good
  • もう一度（もういちど）= once more
  • やる = to do
  • までだ = I'll just

2. Nothing more than that

With a た-form, it plays down the speaker's own action.

当然のことをしたまでです。

"I only did what anyone would do."

  • 当然（とうぜん）= natural, obvious
  • のこと = thing
  • をした = did
  • までです = that's all

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + までだ, Verb た-form + までだ

  • 歩く（あるく）= to walk → 歩くまでだ（あるくまでだ）= I'll just walk
  • 諦める（あきらめる）= to give up → 諦めるまでだ（あきらめるまでだ）= I'll simply give up
  • 言う（いう）= to say → 言ったまでだ（いったまでだ）= I only said it

Key idea

～までだ draws a line: either "then I'll simply do this", or "I did this, and nothing more".
