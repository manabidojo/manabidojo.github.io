～にもかかわらず: in spite of / despite

～にもかかわらず links a fact to an outcome that goes against what that fact would lead you to expect. It attaches to a noun or to a plain-form clause, and corresponds to English "despite" or "in spite of".

It is a formal connective, common in news reports, announcements, and written Japanese. In casual conversation, のに usually does the same job. Unlike ～といっても, which softens your own statement, ～にもかかわらず reports a real result that defied the circumstances.

1. After a noun

Attach にもかかわらず directly to a noun that describes the unfavorable circumstance.

雨にもかかわらず試合は続いた。

"Despite the rain, the match continued."

  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • にもかかわらず = despite, in spite of
  • 試合（しあい）= match, game
  • は = topic marker
  • 続いた（つづいた）= continued

2. After a clause

With verbs and い-adjectives, use the plain form directly before にもかかわらず. The second clause states what happened anyway.

熱があるにもかかわらず、彼は会社に行った。

"Despite having a fever, he went to the office."

  • 熱（ねつ）= fever
  • が = subject marker
  • あるにもかかわらず = despite having
  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • 会社（かいしゃ）= company, office
  • に = direction marker
  • 行った（いった）= went

How to form it

Noun / plain form + にもかかわらず

  • 雨（あめ）= rain → 雨にもかかわらず（あめにもかかわらず）= despite the rain
  • ある = to have, to exist → あるにもかかわらず = despite having
  • 忙しい（いそがしい）= busy → 忙しいにもかかわらず（いそがしいにもかかわらず）= despite being busy

Key idea

～にもかかわらず sets up an expectation and then knocks it down: given the first fact, the second clause should not have happened, but it did. Reach for it in formal speech and writing where casual のに would sound too plain.
