～がほしい: wanting a thing

ほしい is an い-adjective meaning "wanted, desired". With the particle が it says that the speaker wants an object.

In English it is "I want" + noun. It is only for things; wanting to do something uses ～たい instead.

1. Saying what you want

The wanted thing is marked with が, because ほしい describes it as "wanted".

新しいパソコンがほしい。

"I want a new computer."

  • 新しい（あたらしい）= new
  • パソコン = computer
  • が = marks what is wanted
  • ほしい = wanted

2. Asking what someone wants

In questions it asks about the listener's wishes. Since it is quite direct, it fits friends and family.

誕生日に何がほしい？

"What do you want for your birthday?"

  • 誕生日（たんじょうび）= birthday
  • に = for (an occasion)
  • 何（なに）= what
  • が = marks what is wanted
  • ほしい = wanted

～がほしい vs ～たい

ほしい wants a thing: 水がほしい (I want water). ～たい wants to do something: 水が飲みたい (I want to drink water). For other people's wishes, both switch to their がる forms: ほしがる, ～たがる.

How to form it

Noun + が + ほしい

  • 時間（じかん）= time → 時間がほしい（じかんがほしい）= I want (more) time
  • 車（くるま）= car → 車がほしかった（くるまがほしかった）= I wanted a car
  • 何も（なにも）= nothing → 何もほしくない（なにもほしくない）= I don't want anything

ほしい conjugates like any い-adjective: ほしくない (don't want), ほしかった (wanted).

Key idea

ほしい is for wanting things, ～たい is for wanting to do things. The wanted thing takes が.
