～ものがある: there's something (about it)

～ものがある says that the speaker senses a certain quality in something, often something impressive or moving.

In English it is "there's something (moving / impressive) about...". It expresses a strong impression without stating it directly.

1. Something impressive

The quality comes from the speaker's feeling, not from a fact.

彼の演奏には人を感動させるものがある。

"There's something deeply moving about his playing."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • の = ('s)
  • 演奏（えんそう）= performance
  • には = in
  • 人を感動させる（ひとをかんどうさせる）= moves people
  • ものがある = there is something

2. A feeling that's hard to bear

It also expresses feelings like sadness or difficulty.

一人で暮らすのは、寂しいものがある。

"There's something lonely about living alone."

  • 一人で（ひとりで）= alone
  • 暮らす（くらす）= to live
  • のは = (the act of)
  • 寂しい（さびしい）= lonely
  • ものがある = there is something

How to form it

Plain form + ものがある (な-adjectives take な)

  • 驚く（おどろく）= to be surprised → 驚くものがある（おどろくものがある）= there's something surprising
  • 厳しい（きびしい）= harsh → 厳しいものがある（きびしいものがある）= it's rather harsh
  • 見事（みごと）= splendid → 見事なものがある（みごとなものがある）= there's something splendid

Key idea

～ものがある names an impression: the speaker can feel a quality in something, even if they can't point to it exactly.
