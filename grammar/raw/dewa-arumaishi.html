～ではあるまいし: it's not as if

～ではあるまいし combines the noun with ではあるまい ("surely isn't") and し ("so"). It says "it's not as if (you were)..., so...", often to criticize or scold.

In English it is "it's not like you're..." or "you're not a... (so act like it)". In speech it becomes ～じゃあるまいし.

1. Scolding someone

The noun names something the person clearly is not, as a way to criticize them.

子供じゃあるまいし、自分でやりなさい。

"You're not a kid, do it yourself."

  • 子供（こども）= child
  • じゃあるまいし = it's not as if (you were)
  • 自分で（じぶんで）= by yourself
  • やりなさい = do it

2. Dismissing an exaggeration

It also brushes off overreactions.

永遠の別れではあるまいし、そんなに泣かないで。

"It's not as if this is goodbye forever, so don't cry like that."

  • 永遠（えいえん）= eternal
  • の = (of)
  • 別れ（わかれ）= parting
  • ではあるまいし = it's not as if
  • そんなに = so much
  • 泣かないで（なかないで）= don't cry

How to form it

Noun + ではあるまいし／じゃあるまいし

  • 神様（かみさま）= god → 神様じゃあるまいし（かみさまじゃあるまいし）= I'm not God (so how would I know)
  • 素人（しろうと）= amateur → 素人ではあるまいし（しろうとではあるまいし）= you're no amateur
  • 冗談（じょうだん）= joke → 冗談じゃあるまいし（じょうだんじゃあるまいし）= this isn't a joke

Key idea

～ではあるまいし points out what someone obviously is not, and tells them to act accordingly.
