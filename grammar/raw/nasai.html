～なさい: do it! (instruction from above)

～なさい attaches to a verb's ます-stem and turns it into an instruction. It is used by people with authority: parents, teachers, or written test instructions.

In English it is a plain command, "do it". It is firm but not rude, which is why it is the typical way parents talk to children.

1. Parents and teachers giving orders

The speaker is above the listener and expects them to do it.

早く寝なさい。

"Go to bed already."

  • 早く（はやく）= early, quickly
  • 寝なさい（ねなさい）= go to sleep

2. Written instructions

Tests and textbooks use ～なさい for neutral instructions.

正しい答えを選びなさい。

"Choose the correct answer."

  • 正しい（ただしい）= correct
  • 答え（こたえ）= answer
  • を = object marker
  • 選びなさい（えらびなさい）= choose

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + なさい

  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べなさい（たべなさい）= eat
  • 勉強する（べんきょうする）= to study → 勉強しなさい（べんきょうしなさい）= study
  • 起きる（おきる）= to get up → 起きなさい（おきなさい）= get up

In casual speech, なさい is often shortened to な: 早く寝な.

Key idea

～なさい is an order from above. You hear it from parents and teachers, but you don't use it toward them.
