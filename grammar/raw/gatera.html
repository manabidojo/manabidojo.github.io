～がてら: while (combining two purposes)

～がてら says that someone does one thing and uses the chance to do another at the same time. The first activity is usually a walk, an outing or a trip.

In English it is "while...", "on the way..." or "combined with...".

One outing, two purposes

The first activity, usually a walk, an errand or a trip, gives the chance to do something else along the way.

散歩がてら、パンを買いに行った。

"I went to buy bread while out for a walk."

  • 散歩（さんぽ）= walk
  • がてら = while
  • パン = bread
  • を = object marker
  • 買いに（かいに）= to buy
  • 行った（いった）= went

買い物がてら、友達の家に寄った。

"I dropped by a friend's place while I was out shopping."

  • 買い物（かいもの）= shopping
  • がてら = while
  • 友達（ともだち）= friend
  • の = ('s)
  • 家（いえ）= house
  • に = at
  • 寄った（よった）= dropped by

How to form it

Noun / verb ます-stem + がてら

  • 旅行（りょこう）= travel → 旅行がてら（りょこうがてら）= while traveling
  • 見物（けんぶつ）= sightseeing → 見物がてら（けんぶつがてら）= while sightseeing
  • 送る（おくる）= to see off → 駅まで送りがてら（えきまでおくりがてら）= while seeing (someone) to the station

It is close to ～ついでに, but ～がてら is mostly used with movement and sounds more written.

Key idea

～がてら makes one outing serve two purposes.
