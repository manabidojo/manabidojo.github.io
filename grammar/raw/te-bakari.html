～てばかり: doing nothing but

～てばかり is made from the て-form of a verb plus ばかり (only), usually followed by いる. It means someone "does nothing but" that action, the same thing over and over, usually with a critical or exasperated tone.

Compare this with Noun + ばかり: お菓子ばかり食べる limits the noun ("eats nothing but sweets"), while ～てばかりいる targets the action itself ("does nothing but eat").

1. Doing the same thing all the time

Use ～てばかりいる to complain that one action fills all of someone's time.

彼は遊んでばかりいる。

"He does nothing but play."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 遊んで（あそんで）= playing (て-form of 遊ぶ)
  • ばかり = nothing but
  • いる = is (doing)

2. ～てばかりいないで: "stop just V-ing and..."

The negative て-form ～てばかりいないで tells someone to stop doing only that one thing and do something else instead.

泣いてばかりいないで、話してください。

"Don't just keep crying, please talk to me."

  • 泣いて（ないて）= crying (て-form of 泣く)
  • ばかりいないで = instead of doing nothing but
  • 話してください（はなしてください）= please talk

How to form it

Verb て-form + ばかり + いる

  • 遊ぶ（あそぶ）= to play → 遊んでばかりいる（あそんでばかりいる）= does nothing but play
  • 寝る（ねる）= to sleep → 寝てばかりいる（ねてばかりいる）= does nothing but sleep
  • 泣く（なく）= to cry → 泣いてばかりいる（ないてばかりいる）= does nothing but cry

Key idea

～てばかりいる paints a picture of one action on endless repeat. The speaker is not just reporting a habit, they are usually frowning at it.
