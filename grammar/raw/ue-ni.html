～上に: besides / not only that

上 (うえ) means "top". ～上に adds a further point of the same kind: good on top of good, or bad on top of bad.

In English it is "on top of", "besides" or "not only... but also".

1. Piling up bad points

Both points push in the same negative direction.

この部屋は狭い上に、家賃が高い。

"This room is small, and on top of that the rent is high."

  • この = this
  • 部屋（へや）= room
  • は = topic marker
  • 狭い（せまい）= small
  • 上に（うえに）= on top of
  • 家賃（やちん）= rent
  • 高い（たかい）= high

2. Piling up good points

It works just as well for positive points.

彼女は頭がいい上に、性格もいい。

"Not only is she smart, she has a great personality too."

  • 彼女（かのじょ）= she
  • は = topic marker
  • 頭がいい（あたまがいい）= smart
  • 上に（うえに）= on top of
  • 性格（せいかく）= personality
  • も = also
  • いい = good

How to form it

Plain form + 上に (nouns take である上に／の上に, な-adjectives take な)

  • 安い（やすい）= cheap → 安い上に（やすいうえに）= on top of being cheap
  • 便利（べんり）= convenient → 便利な上に（べんりなうえに）= besides being convenient
  • 寝不足（ねぶそく）= lack of sleep → 寝不足の上に（ねぶそくのうえに）= on top of being short on sleep

Both points should be of the same kind. For a contrast, use ～のに or ～けど instead.

Key idea

～上に stacks one point on another of the same kind: and on top of that.
