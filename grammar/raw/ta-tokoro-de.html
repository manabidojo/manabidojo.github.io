～たところで: even if (it won't help)

～たところで after a verb in the た-form says that even if you do it, it won't make a difference. The second clause is usually negative or pessimistic.

In English it is "even if", "no matter how much" or "it's no use...".

1. A pointless effort

The speaker thinks the action is not worth doing because the result won't change.

今から急いだところで、間に合わない。

"Even if we hurry now, we won't make it."

  • 今から（いまから）= from now
  • 急いだ（いそいだ）= hurried
  • ところで = even if
  • 間に合わない（まにあわない）= won't make it in time

2. Downplaying an amount

With amounts, it means that even at most, it's not much.

遅れたところで、五分ぐらいだろう。

"Even if we're late, it'll only be about five minutes."

  • 遅れた（おくれた）= were late
  • ところで = even if
  • 五分（ごふん）= five minutes
  • ぐらい = about
  • だろう = probably

How to form it

Verb た-form + ところで

  • 謝る（あやまる）= to apologize → 謝ったところで（あやまったところで）= even if (you) apologize
  • 言う（いう）= to say → 言ったところで（いったところで）= even if (you) say it
  • 頑張る（がんばる）= to try hard → 頑張ったところで（がんばったところで）= even if (I) try hard

Don't confuse it with the conjunction ところで ("by the way") at the start of a sentence.

Key idea

～たところで dismisses an action in advance: even if you did it, nothing would change.
