～ところ: just did / in the middle of / just about to

ところ literally means "place", but after a verb it points to the place in time an action is at. With the past form (～たところ) it means "just finished doing"; with ～ている (～ているところ) it means "right in the middle of doing"; and with the dictionary form it means "just about to do".

The form of the verb in front of ところ tells you which stage you are at, like a snapshot taken just before, during, or after the action.

1. Just finished: ～たところ

Use た-form + ところ when an action has finished only a moment ago.

今食べたところだ。

"I just finished eating."

  • 今（いま）= now, just now
  • 食べた（たべた）= ate (past form of 食べる, to eat)
  • ところ = the point, the moment (literally "place")
  • だ = plain form of です

2. In the middle of: ～ているところ

Use ～ている + ところ when you are right in the middle of an action. Unlike plain ～ている, which simply says an action is ongoing, ～ているところ highlights the exact moment, often as a reason you can't do something else right now.

今、宿題をしているところです。

"I'm in the middle of doing my homework right now."

  • 今（いま）= now
  • 宿題（しゅくだい）= homework
  • を = object marker
  • している = doing (て-form of する + いる)
  • ところです = am at the point of, in the middle of

3. Just about to: ～るところ

Use the dictionary form + ところ when an action is about to begin.

今から帰るところだ。

"I'm just about to go home."

  • 今から（いまから）= from now, now
  • 帰る（かえる）= to go home
  • ところ = the point, the moment
  • だ = plain form of です

How to form it

Verb た-form + ところ (just did) / Verb て-form + いる + ところ (in the middle of) / Verb dictionary form + ところ (just about to)

  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べたところ（たべたところ）= just ate
  • する = to do → しているところ = in the middle of doing
  • 帰る（かえる）= to go home → 帰るところ（かえるところ）= just about to go home

Key idea

ところ marks a specific point in time. The verb form before it tells you which point you're at: たところ = just finished, ているところ = right in the middle of, るところ = just about to.
