～たらどう: why don't you?

～たらどう is the conditional ～たら plus どう ("how"). Literally "how about if you did...?", it is a casual suggestion.

In English it is "why don't you...?". It is friendly but direct, so it fits friends and family better than superiors.

1. Giving casual advice

The speaker suggests an action and leaves the decision to the listener.

少し休んだらどう？

"Why don't you take a little rest?"

  • 少し（すこし）= a little
  • 休んだら（やすんだら）= if (you) rested
  • どう = how about

2. A more polite version

～たらどうですか is politer, and ～たらいかがですか is politer still.

先生に相談したらいかがですか。

"How about talking it over with your teacher?"

  • 先生（せんせい）= teacher
  • に = with
  • 相談したら（そうだんしたら）= if (you) consulted
  • いかがですか = how about (polite)

How to form it

Verb た-form + ら + どう

  • 聞く（きく）= to ask → 聞いたらどう（きいたらどう）= why don't you ask?
  • 寝る（ねる）= to sleep → 寝たらどう（ねたらどう）= why don't you go to bed?
  • 謝る（あやまる）= to apologize → 謝ったらどう（あやまったらどう）= why don't you apologize?

Key idea

～たらどう wraps advice in a question, so the listener stays free to decide.
