～てはいけない: you must not do it

～てはいけない expresses prohibition: "you must not do X". It is made from the て-form of a verb, the particle は, and いけない, which literally means "it won't go", so the whole pattern says "doing X won't do".

It is the opposite of ～なければならない: one tells you an action is required, the other tells you an action is forbidden.

1. Rules and prohibitions

Use ～てはいけない for rules, warnings, and things that are simply not allowed. The polite form is ～てはいけません.

ここに入ってはいけない。

"You must not enter here."

  • ここ = here
  • に = marker for the place you move into
  • 入ってはいけない（はいってはいけない）= must not enter

ここで写真を撮ってはいけません。

"You must not take photos here."

  • ここ = here
  • で = marker for the place where an action happens
  • 写真（しゃしん）= photo
  • を = object marker
  • 撮ってはいけません（とってはいけません）= must not take (polite)

2. Casual contractions: ～ちゃだめ and ～ちゃいけない

In casual speech, ては contracts to ちゃ (and では to じゃ), and いけない is often replaced by だめ ("no good").

それを食べちゃだめ！

"Don't eat that!"

  • それ = that
  • を = object marker
  • 食べちゃだめ（たべちゃだめ）= must not eat (casual contraction of 食べてはだめ)

How to form it

Verb て-form + は + いけない

  • 入る（はいる）= to enter → 入ってはいけない（はいってはいけない）= must not enter
  • 使う（つかう）= to use → 使ってはいけない（つかってはいけない）= must not use
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べてはいけない（たべてはいけない）= must not eat

Key idea

～てはいけない takes an action and declares "that won't do". When you hear てはいけない, ちゃいけない, or ちゃだめ, someone is drawing a line: this action is not allowed.
