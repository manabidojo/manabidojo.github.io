～ものか／～もんか: no way (strong denial)

～ものか at the end of a sentence is a strong, emotional denial. Although it looks like a question, it means "absolutely not". In casual speech it becomes ～もんか.

In English it is "no way (I'll)...", "like I'd ever..." or "I'll never...".

1. A strong refusal

The speaker declares they will never do something.

あんな店、二度と行くもんか。

"No way I'm ever going to that place again."

  • あんな = that kind of
  • 店（みせ）= shop, place
  • 二度と（にどと）= ever again
  • 行く（いく）= to go
  • もんか = no way (I'll)

2. Denying a statement

It also strongly denies that something is true.

負けるものか。

"Like I'd ever lose!"

  • 負ける（まける）= to lose
  • ものか = no way

How to form it

Plain form + ものか／もんか (nouns and な-adjectives take なものか)

  • 泣く（なく）= to cry → 泣くもんか（なくもんか）= I won't cry
  • 許す（ゆるす）= to forgive → 許すものか（ゆるすものか）= I'll never forgive (you)
  • 平気（へいき）= fine → 平気なもんか（へいきなもんか）= I'm far from fine

The polite version is ～ものですか: 負けるものですか.

Key idea

～ものか dresses a flat "no" as a question: "would I ever? Absolutely not."
