～まま: leaving a state as it is

～まま says that a state stays unchanged while something else happens. It usually follows a verb in the た-form or ない-form, or a noun plus の.

In English it often comes out as "with ... still on", "as it is", or "without doing".

1. た-form + まま: a state left in place

After the た-form, まま means the result of that action is still in effect while the main action happens.

靴を履いたまま入らないで。

"Don't come in with your shoes on."

  • 靴（くつ）= shoes
  • を = object marker
  • 履いた（はいた）= put on (and still wearing)
  • まま = as is / unchanged
  • 入らないで（はいらないで）= don't come in

テレビをつけたまま寝てしまった。

"I fell asleep with the TV on."

  • テレビ = TV
  • を = object marker
  • つけた = turned on (and left on)
  • まま = as is
  • 寝てしまった（ねてしまった）= ended up falling asleep

2. ない-form or noun + のまま: unchanged from before

With a ない-form verb, まま means "without doing". With a noun plus の, it means something is still just as that noun describes.

彼は何も言わないまま帰った。

"He went home without saying anything."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 何も（なにも）= nothing (with a negative)
  • 言わない（いわない）= not say
  • まま = as is / without change
  • 帰った（かえった）= went home

Note the difference from ～ながら: ～ながら joins two actions the same person is actively doing at the same time, while ～まま describes a state that is simply left as it is in the background.

How to form it

Verb た-form / ない-form + まま, or Noun + のまま

  • 履く（はく）= to put on (shoes) → 履いたまま（はいたまま）= with (shoes) still on
  • 言わない（いわない）= not say → 言わないまま（いわないまま）= without saying
  • 昔（むかし）= long ago → 昔のまま（むかしのまま）= just as it was long ago

Key idea

Think of まま as pressing pause on a state: whatever situation was created (having shoes on, TV on, nothing said) stays frozen exactly as it is while the rest of the sentence happens.
