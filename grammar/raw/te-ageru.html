～てあげる: doing something for someone

あげる means "to give". After a て-form it gives an action instead of an object: someone does a favor for another person.

In English it is "do something for someone". The favor goes outward, from the speaker's side to someone else.

1. Doing a favor for someone

The person who benefits is usually marked with に, or understood from context.

弟に本を読んであげた。

"I read a book to my little brother."

  • 弟（おとうと）= younger brother
  • に = to
  • 本（ほん）= book
  • を = object marker
  • 読んであげた（よんであげた）= read (for him)

2. Careful with superiors

Saying ～てあげる directly to a superior can sound condescending, because it stresses the favor. Toward superiors, the humble お～します is used instead.

荷物、持ってあげるよ。

"I'll carry your bag for you."

  • 荷物（にもつ）= bag, luggage
  • 持ってあげる（もってあげる）= carry (for you)
  • よ = (I'm telling you)

～てあげる vs ～てくれる

Both describe favors, but they point in opposite directions. ～てあげる goes away from the speaker: 弟に読んであげた (I read to my brother). ～てくれる comes toward the speaker: 兄が読んでくれた (my brother read to me).

How to form it

Verb て-form + あげる

  • 貸す（かす）= to lend → 貸してあげる（かしてあげる）= lend (to someone)
  • 作る（つくる）= to make → 作ってあげる（つくってあげる）= make (for someone)
  • 教える（おしえる）= to teach → 教えてあげる（おしえてあげる）= teach (someone)

For people below you or animals, the rougher ～てやる is used; for very polite situations, ～てさしあげる.

Key idea

～てあげる means "I do it for you". The favor goes away from the speaker toward someone else.
