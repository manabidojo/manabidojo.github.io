～だろう: probably or surely

～だろう is the plain conjectural ending. It follows the plain form of a verb or い-adjective, or attaches directly to a noun or な-adjective (without だ), and marks the sentence as the speaker's guess or supposition.

It maps to English "probably" or "I suppose". It is the plain-style counterpart of polite ～でしょう, so it is common in writing and in casual speech.

1. Making a guess

Add だろう to a statement to show you believe it is true but are not certain.

明日は晴れるだろう。

"It will probably be sunny tomorrow."

  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • は = topic marker
  • 晴れる（はれる）= to clear up, become sunny
  • だろう = probably (speaker's guess)

2. Guessing about something you cannot check

～だろう also works for past events, when you are inferring what must have happened.

彼はもう家に着いただろう。

"He has probably arrived home by now."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • もう = already
  • 家（いえ）= home, house
  • に = destination marker
  • 着いた（ついた）= arrived
  • だろう = probably

How to form it

Plain form + だろう; for nouns and な-adjectives, attach だろう directly (drop だ).

  • 晴れる（はれる）= to clear up → 晴れるだろう（はれるだろう）= will probably clear up
  • 寒い（さむい）= cold → 寒いだろう（さむいだろう）= is probably cold
  • 雨（あめ）= rain → 雨だろう（あめだろう）= is probably rain

Key idea

～だろう softens a statement into a prediction: the speaker is fairly confident, but is presenting it as a guess rather than a fact. When you need to be polite, switch to ～でしょう.
