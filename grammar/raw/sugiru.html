～すぎる: too much, overly

～すぎる means that an action or a quality goes past a reasonable limit. It attaches to the masu-stem of a verb (食べすぎる = eat too much) or to the stem of an adjective (高すぎる = too expensive).

It maps directly to English "too ~" and "~ too much", and it almost always carries a negative judgment.

1. Doing too much of an action

With a verb, ～すぎる says the action was overdone. It is often combined with ～てしまう to add a feeling of regret.

昨日は食べすぎてしまった。

"I ended up eating too much yesterday."

  • 昨日（きのう）= yesterday
  • は = topic marker
  • 食べすぎてしまった（たべすぎてしまった）= ended up eating too much (食べすぎる plus ～てしまう for regret)

2. Too much of a quality

With an adjective, ～すぎる says the quality is at an excessive level.

この本は難しすぎる。

"This book is too difficult."

  • この = this
  • 本（ほん）= book
  • は = topic marker
  • 難しすぎる（むずかしすぎる）= is too difficult

How to form it

Verb masu-stem + すぎる, or adjective stem (drop い / use the na-adjective base) + すぎる. The result conjugates as a regular る-verb (すぎた, すぎます).

  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べすぎる（たべすぎる）= to eat too much
  • 高い（たかい）= expensive → 高すぎる（たかすぎる）= too expensive
  • 静か（しずか）= quiet → 静かすぎる（しずかすぎる）= too quiet

Key idea

～すぎる draws a line of "just right" and says you went over it, whether it is how much you did something or how strong a quality is.
