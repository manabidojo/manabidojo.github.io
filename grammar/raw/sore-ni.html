それに: besides / moreover

それに adds another point to what was just said, usually one that supports the same conclusion.

In English it is "besides", "plus" or "on top of that". It often follows ～し, which stacks up reasons.

1. Adding another reason

The new point pushes in the same direction as the first.

この店は安いし、それにおいしい。

"This place is cheap, and on top of that it's tasty."

  • この = this
  • 店（みせ）= shop, restaurant
  • は = topic marker
  • 安いし（やすいし）= is cheap, and
  • それに = on top of that
  • おいしい = tasty

2. Opening a new sentence

It can also start a new sentence that adds to the argument.

今日は行かない。それに、雨も降っているし。

"I'm not going today. Besides, it's raining."

  • 今日（きょう）= today
  • 行かない（いかない）= won't go
  • それに = besides
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • 降っている（ふっている）= is falling

How to form it

Sentence A (し). それに、sentence B. それに never changes form.

  • 広い（ひろい）= spacious → それに、明るい（それに、あかるい）= besides, it's bright
  • 疲れた（つかれた）= I'm tired → それに、眠い（それに、ねむい）= plus, I'm sleepy
  • 便利だ（べんりだ）= it's convenient → それに、安い（それに、やすい）= on top of that, it's cheap

Similar words: そのうえ (more formal), しかも (and what's more).

Key idea

それに stacks one more point on top. Use it when the new point supports the same conclusion.
