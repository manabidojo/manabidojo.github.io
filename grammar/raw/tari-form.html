～たり: doing things like A and B

～たり lists actions as representative examples rather than a complete report. Saying 食べたり飲んだりする means you ate, drank, and did other things too. The list is just a sample.

In English it corresponds to "do things like A and B" or "..., among other things".

1. ～たり～たりする: examples from a longer list

The classic pattern chains two (or more) ～たり verbs and closes the list with する.

昨日のパーティーでは、食べたり飲んだりしました。

"At yesterday's party we did things like eat and drink."

  • 昨日（きのう）= yesterday
  • の = linking particle (of)
  • パーティー = party
  • で = at (place of action)
  • は = topic marker
  • 食べたり（たべたり）= ate (for example)
  • 飲んだり（のんだり）= drank (for example)
  • しました = did (polite past)

2. A single ～たりする: one representative example

One ～たり on its own still signals "this is just an example". Other similar activities are implied.

休みの日は映画を見たりする。

"On my days off I do things like watch movies."

  • 休み（やすみ）= day off, rest
  • の = linking particle (of)
  • 日（ひ）= day
  • は = topic marker
  • 映画（えいが）= movie
  • を = object marker
  • 見たり（みたり）= watch (among other things)
  • する = do

～たり vs the て-form

The て-form lists actions in order and implies that is the whole story: 食べて、飲んだ = "we ate and then drank". ～たり gives unordered examples and implies "among other things". The actions did not necessarily happen in that sequence, and the list is not complete.

How to form it

Past-tense form + り, the sound changes are exactly the same as for た. The list is usually closed off with する, which carries the tense and politeness.

  • 読む（よむ）= to read → 読んだり（よんだり）= reading (for example)
  • 書く（かく）= to write → 書いたり（かいたり）= writing (for example)
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べたり（たべたり）= eating (for example)
  • する = to do → したり = doing (for example)
  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来たり（きたり）= coming (for example)

Key idea

～たり pulls a sample out of a longer list. Whenever you would say "..., among other things" in English, that trailing "among other things" is what ～たり adds.
