～とばかりに: as if to say

～とばかりに after a quotation says that someone acts as if they were saying something, without actually saying it.

In English it is "as if to say...". It often describes body language or a sudden reaction.

Actions that speak

The quoted words are what the action seems to say. It often describes body language or a sudden reaction.

彼は「待ってました」とばかりに立ち上がった。

"He stood up as if to say 'I've been waiting for this.'"

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 待ってました（まってました）= (I've) been waiting
  • とばかりに = as if to say
  • 立ち上がった（たちあがった）= stood up

犬は「早く散歩に行こう」とばかりにしっぽを振った。

"The dog wagged its tail as if to say "let's go for a walk already.""

  • 犬（いぬ）= dog
  • は = topic marker
  • 早く（はやく）= quickly
  • 散歩に行こう（さんぽにいこう）= let's go for a walk
  • とばかりに = as if to say
  • しっぽを振った（しっぽをふった）= wagged its tail

How to form it

Quote / plain form + とばかりに

  • 今だ（いまだ）= now's the chance → 今だとばかりに（いまだとばかりに）= as if to say "now's the chance"
  • 帰れ（かえれ）= go home → 帰れとばかりに（かえれとばかりに）= as if to say "go home"
  • 当然だ（とうぜんだ）= it's obvious → 当然だとばかりに（とうぜんだとばかりに）= as if it were obvious

Key idea

～とばかりに turns an action into words: the person didn't say it, but their behavior did.
