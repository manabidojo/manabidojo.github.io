～ついでに: doing a second thing while you're at it

～ついでに links two actions: while doing the main one, you take the opportunity to also do a smaller, secondary one. It attaches to a noun + の or directly to a verb.

It maps to English "while you're at it" or "on the same occasion as". The part before ついでに is the main purpose and what follows is the convenient extra.

1. Noun + のついでに

With an action noun, のついでに means "taking advantage of (that errand)".

買い物のついでに郵便局へ行った。

"I went to the post office while I was out shopping."

  • 買い物（かいもの）= shopping
  • の = links the noun to ついでに
  • ついでに = while at it, on the same occasion
  • 郵便局（ゆうびんきょく）= post office
  • へ = direction marker
  • 行った（いった）= went

2. Verb + ついでに

A verb attaches directly: dictionary form if the extra task happens along the way, た-form if it happens after the main action is already done.

出かけるついでにごみを出した。

"While going out, I took out the trash."

  • 出かける（でかける）= to go out
  • ついでに = while at it
  • ごみ = trash
  • を = object marker
  • 出した（だした）= took out

How to form it

Noun + の + ついでに / Verb (dictionary or た-form) + ついでに

  • 買い物（かいもの）= shopping → 買い物のついでに（かいもののついでに）= while out shopping
  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行くついでに（いくついでに）= while going
  • 洗う（あらう）= to wash → 洗ったついでに（あらったついでに）= while (I) was at it washing

Key idea

～ついでに says: "I'm already doing X, so it costs me nothing extra to do Y too." The main action comes first, the add-on rides along on the same occasion.
