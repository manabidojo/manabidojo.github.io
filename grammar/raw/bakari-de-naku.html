～ばかりでなく: not only X, but also Y

～ばかりでなく attaches to a noun, a plain-form verb, or an adjective and means "not only X". The rest of the sentence then adds something beyond X, usually marked with も ("also").

It maps directly onto English "not only ... but also ...": you state the obvious or expected fact first, then extend the statement past it.

Adding something beyond the obvious case

Use ～ばかりでなく when one fact alone does not tell the whole story.

勉強ばかりでなく、運動もする。

"I not only study but also exercise."

  • 勉強（べんきょう）= study
  • ばかりでなく = not only
  • 運動（うんどう）= exercise
  • も = also (additive particle)
  • する = to do

彼は英語ばかりでなく、中国語も話せる。

"He can speak not only English but also Chinese."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 英語（えいご）= English
  • ばかりでなく = not only
  • 中国語（ちゅうごくご）= Chinese
  • も = also
  • 話せる（はなせる）= can speak

Compare this with ～だけでなく: the two mean the same thing, but ～だけでなく is more common in everyday speech, while ～ばかりでなく sounds a little more formal and fits written Japanese well.

How to form it

Noun / plain-form verb / adjective + ばかりでなく, with も in the second clause

  • 勉強（べんきょう）= study → 勉強ばかりでなく = not only studying
  • 安い（やすい）= cheap → 安いばかりでなく = not only cheap
  • 話す（はなす）= to speak → 話すばかりでなく = not only speaks

Key idea

～ばかりでなく tells the listener "X is true, but don't stop there", the sentence then adds a second fact with も that goes beyond X.
