～ないで: without doing

～ないで describes doing one action without doing another. It is made by taking the ない-form of a verb and adding で: the ないで verb is the thing you did not do, and the main verb at the end is what you actually did.

It matches English "without ~ing" or "instead of ~ing". Do not confuse it with ～なくて, which usually gives a reason ("because ... not") rather than describing how an action was done.

1. Doing B without doing A

The most basic use: action B happens, and action A, which you would normally expect, does not.

朝ご飯を食べないで学校に行った。

"I went to school without eating breakfast."

  • 朝ご飯（あさごはん）= breakfast
  • を = object marker
  • 食べないで（たべないで）= without eating
  • 学校（がっこう）= school
  • に = destination marker
  • 行った（いった）= went

2. Doing B instead of A

When the two actions are alternatives, ～ないで often translates better as "instead of": you chose B in place of A.

昨日は勉強しないで、ゲームをしていた。

"Yesterday I played video games instead of studying."

  • 昨日（きのう）= yesterday
  • は = topic marker
  • 勉強しないで（べんきょうしないで）= without studying / instead of studying
  • ゲーム = video game
  • を = object marker
  • していた = was doing / was playing

How to form it

Verb ない-form + で

  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べないで（たべないで）= without eating
  • 使う（つかう）= to use → 使わないで（つかわないで）= without using
  • する = to do → しないで = without doing

Key idea

～ないで paints the background of the main action: "B happened, and A did not". The verb before ないで is skipped and the verb at the end of the sentence is what really took place.
