～べき: should / ought to

～べき expresses a strong recommendation or a moral obligation: this is what one should or ought to do. It attaches to the dictionary form of a verb, and the verb する has the special short form すべき (するべき is also used).

It is stronger than soft advice like ～たほうがいい: ～べきだ presents the action as the right and proper thing to do.

1. Saying what should be done

Use dictionary form + べきだ to state a duty or a firm recommendation. The negative is ～べきではない ("should not").

約束は守るべきだ。

"You should keep your promises."

  • 約束（やくそく）= promise
  • は = topic marker
  • 守るべきだ（まもるべきだ）= should keep

2. Regret with べきだった

The past form ～べきだった means "should have done" — the right action was not taken, and the speaker regrets it.

もっと早く医者に行くべきだった。

"I should have gone to the doctor sooner."

  • もっと = more
  • 早く（はやく）= early, soon (adverb)
  • 医者（いしゃ）= doctor
  • に = destination marker
  • 行くべきだった（いくべきだった）= should have gone

Notice that the verb before べき stays in the dictionary form; only the final だ changes to だった.

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + べき（だ）; する becomes すべき

  • 守る（まもる）= to keep → 守るべきだ（まもるべきだ）= should keep
  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行くべきだ（いくべきだ）= should go
  • する = to do → すべきだ = should do

Key idea

～べき marks an action as the right thing to do. Use it for duties and firm recommendations, and ～べきだった when the right thing was, regrettably, not done.
