～と言う／～と聞く: quoting what someone said

The particle と marks a quotation. Before verbs like 言う ("to say"), 聞く ("to hear") or 書く ("to write") it reports what was said.

In English it maps to "said that..." or "heard that...". The quoted part keeps its own form and tense.

1. Reporting what someone said

～と言っていた ("was saying") is the usual way to pass on a message from someone.

田中さんは明日来ると言っていた。

"Tanaka said he'd come tomorrow."

  • 田中さん（たなかさん）= Mr. Tanaka
  • は = topic marker
  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • 来る（くる）= to come
  • と = quotation marker
  • 言っていた（いっていた）= was saying

2. Passing on something you heard

With 聞く, the speaker reports information that came from someone else.

来月から店が休みになると聞いた。

"I heard the shop will be closed from next month."

  • 来月（らいげつ）= next month
  • から = from
  • 店（みせ）= shop
  • 休み（やすみ）= closed, day off
  • になる = becomes
  • と聞いた（ときいた）= heard that

How to form it

Plain form + と + 言う／聞く／書く (nouns and な-adjectives take だと)

  • 忙しい（いそがしい）= busy → 忙しいと言った（いそがしいといった）= said (they were) busy
  • 中止（ちゅうし）= canceled → 中止だと聞いた（ちゅうしだときいた）= heard it was canceled
  • 好き（すき）= liked → 好きだと言った（すきだといった）= said (they) like it

Exact words go in 「」: 「ありがとう」と言った. In casual speech と often becomes って: 好きって言った.

Key idea

と is the quotation mark of spoken Japanese: whatever stands before it is what was said, heard or written.
