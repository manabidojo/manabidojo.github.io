～といっても: although I say so, it's less than you think

～といっても takes a statement you have just made and immediately scales it down, so the listener does not get the wrong impression. It comes from と言っても (even if I say...) and attaches to the plain form of a verb or adjective, or directly to a noun.

In English it maps to "although I say..." or "...but really, that just means...". Unlike ～にもかかわらず, which reports an outcome that defies a fact, ～といっても corrects the impression created by your own words.

1. Scaling down a claim

The clause before といっても sounds impressive on its own. The clause after it reveals the modest reality.

料理ができるといっても卵焼きだけです。

"Although I say I can cook, it's only fried eggs."

  • 料理（りょうり）= cooking
  • が = subject marker
  • できるといっても = although I say I can
  • 卵焼き（たまごやき）= rolled omelet / fried eggs
  • だけです = is only

2. Qualifying a label or title

After a noun, といっても warns that the label is technically true but the reality is much smaller than the word suggests.

社長といっても、社員は私一人です。

"Although I say 'president', the only employee is me."

  • 社長（しゃちょう）= company president
  • といっても = although I say
  • 社員（しゃいん）= employee
  • は = topic marker
  • 私（わたし）= I, me
  • 一人（ひとり）= one person
  • です = polite "to be"

How to form it

Plain form / Noun + といっても

  • できる = can do → できるといっても = although I say I can
  • 高い（たかい）= expensive → 高いといっても（たかいといっても）= although I say it's expensive
  • 社長（しゃちょう）= president → 社長といっても（しゃちょうといっても）= although I say 'president'

Key idea

～といっても is a built-in disclaimer: "What I just said is true, but don't expect too much". The clause that follows always shrinks the first statement down to its real size.
