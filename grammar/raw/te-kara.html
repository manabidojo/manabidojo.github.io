～てから: after doing one thing, do another

～てから links two actions in order: the second action happens after the first one is finished. It is made by taking the て-form of a verb and adding から, and it matches English "after doing ...".

Be careful: から attached to a plain verb (行くから) means "because". Only the combination て-form + から means "after".

1. Doing things in order

Use ～てから to say that one action comes first, and another follows it.

家に帰ってから宿題をする。

"I do my homework after coming home."

  • 家（いえ）= home, house
  • に = destination marker (to)
  • 帰ってから（かえってから）= after returning
  • 宿題（しゅくだい）= homework
  • を = object marker
  • する = to do

2. The starting point of a period

～てから can also mark the moment when a situation began, like English "since ...".

日本に来てから、三年になります。

"It has been three years since I came to Japan."

  • 日本（にほん）= Japan
  • に = destination marker (to)
  • 来てから（きてから）= since coming
  • 三年（さんねん）= three years
  • になります = it becomes, it has been

How to form it

Verb て-form + から

  • 帰る（かえる）= to return → 帰ってから（かえってから）= after returning
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べてから（たべてから）= after eating
  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来てから（きてから）= after coming

Key idea

～てから draws a clear line in time: first finish action one, then action two. The first action is the doorway you pass through before the second can happen.
