お～になる: honorific form of an action

お～になる wraps a verb in respect. It is used for the actions of superiors, customers or strangers, raising the person who does the action.

English has no direct equivalent; it is like saying the action in a deferential tone. It belongs to 尊敬語, the language of respect, and is never used for your own actions.

1. Talking respectfully about someone's action

The subject is the respected person.

社長はもうお帰りになりました。

"The president has already gone home."

  • 社長（しゃちょう）= company president
  • は = topic marker
  • もう = already
  • お帰りになりました（おかえりになりました）= went home (honorific)

2. Polite questions to a customer

Staff use it constantly when asking customers about their actions.

こちらの本をお読みになりますか。

"Will you be reading this book?"

  • こちら = this (polite)
  • の = ('s)
  • 本（ほん）= book
  • を = object marker
  • お読みになります（およみになります）= read (honorific)

お～になる vs お～する

They look alike but face opposite ways. お～になる raises the other person and is used for their actions: 先生がお待ちになる. お～する lowers the speaker and is used for the speaker's own actions: 私がお待ちします. Mixing them up is a classic keigo mistake.

How to form it

お + verb ます-stem + になる

  • 待つ（まつ）= to wait → お待ちになる（おまちになる）= wait (honorific)
  • 書く（かく）= to write → お書きになる（おかきになる）= write (honorific)
  • 使う（つかう）= to use → お使いになる（おつかいになる）= use (honorific)

Sino-Japanese verbs take ご: ご利用になる. Some common verbs have special forms instead: 行く／来る become いらっしゃる, 食べる becomes 召し上がる.

Key idea

お～になる lifts the other person up. Use it for what respected people do, never for yourself.
