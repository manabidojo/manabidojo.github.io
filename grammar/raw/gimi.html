～気味: a bit / slightly (a tendency)

気味 (ぎみ) attaches to a noun or a verb's ます-stem and says that there is a slight tendency or a sign of something, usually something unwelcome.

In English it is "a bit", "slightly" or "-ish": 風邪気味 = coming down with a cold.

1. A slight bad condition

It often describes small health problems or feelings.

最近、少し太り気味だ。

"Lately I've been putting on a bit of weight."

  • 最近（さいきん）= lately
  • 少し（すこし）= a little
  • 太り気味（ふとりぎみ）= a bit overweight
  • だ = is

2. Coming down with something

風邪気味 is the standard way to say that you feel a cold coming on.

今日は風邪気味なので、早く帰ります。

"I feel like I'm coming down with a cold, so I'll go home early."

  • 今日（きょう）= today
  • は = topic marker
  • 風邪気味（かぜぎみ）= coming down with a cold
  • なので = so
  • 早く（はやく）= early
  • 帰ります（かえります）= will go home

How to form it

Noun + 気味, Verb ます-stem + 気味 (works as a な-adjective or noun: 気味だ, 気味の + noun)

  • 疲れる（つかれる）= to get tired → 疲れ気味（つかれぎみ）= a bit tired
  • 遅れる（おくれる）= to fall behind → 遅れ気味（おくれぎみ）= running a bit behind
  • 緊張（きんちょう）= nervousness → 緊張気味（きんちょうぎみ）= a little nervous

Compared with ～がち (a frequent habit), ～気味 describes a current, mild state.

Key idea

～気味 says "a touch of": a slight, usually unwelcome sign of something.
