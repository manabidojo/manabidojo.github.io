～じゃない（か）／～じゃん: isn't it? / see? (seeking agreement)

～じゃない with a rising or falling tone is not a negative here. It points something out or seeks agreement, and can also reproach. ～じゃん is its casual short form.

In English it maps to "isn't it?", "see?" or "you know". The more formal version is ～ではないか.

1. "See, I told you"

The speaker reminds the listener of something they should have known.

だから言ったじゃん！

"See, I told you!"

  • だから = that's why
  • 言った（いった）= said
  • じゃん = see? / right?

2. Pointing something out

With a positive opinion, it presents the observation as obvious.

この料理、結構おいしいじゃない。

"This dish is actually pretty good, isn't it?"

  • この = this
  • 料理（りょうり）= dish
  • 結構（けっこう）= quite
  • おいしい = tasty
  • じゃない = isn't it?

How to form it

Plain form + じゃない／じゃん (nouns and な-adjectives drop だ)

  • いい = good → いいじゃん = that's good, isn't it?
  • 言った（いった）= said → 言ったじゃない（いったじゃない）= I told you, didn't I?
  • 本当（ほんとう）= true → 本当じゃん（ほんとうじゃん）= it's true, see?

Context and intonation separate it from the real negative じゃない ("is not").

Key idea

～じゃん says "you can see it too, right?". It points out the obvious, sometimes cheerfully, sometimes with a reproach.
