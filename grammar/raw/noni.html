～のに: although / despite

～のに connects two clauses when the second one goes against what the first one leads you to expect. It attaches to the plain form of verbs and い-adjectives, and to nouns and な-adjectives with な in between (なのに).

In English it maps to "even though" or "despite". Importantly, it carries a feeling of surprise or dissatisfaction: the speaker thinks the result is unfair, disappointing, or strange.

1. A result that contradicts expectation

The clause before のに sets up an expectation and the clause after it states the surprising or disappointing reality.

勉強したのに、テストに落ちた。

"Even though I studied, I failed the test."

  • 勉強した（べんきょうした）= studied
  • のに = even though
  • テスト = test
  • に = marks what was failed
  • 落ちた（おちた）= failed (literally "fell")

2. With nouns and な-adjectives: なのに

After a noun or a な-adjective, add な before のに.

日曜日なのに、仕事がある。

"Even though it's Sunday, I have work."

  • 日曜日（にちようび）= Sunday
  • なのに = even though it is
  • 仕事（しごと）= work
  • が = subject marker
  • ある = there is / (I) have

Unlike ～けど, which is a neutral "but", ～のに adds emotion: the speaker is complaining or surprised that the result turned out this way.

How to form it

Plain form + のに (nouns and な-adjectives take なのに)

  • 勉強する（べんきょうする）= to study → 勉強したのに（べんきょうしたのに）= even though (I) studied
  • 高い（たかい）= expensive → 高いのに（たかいのに）= even though it is expensive
  • 日曜日（にちようび）= Sunday → 日曜日なのに（にちようびなのに）= even though it is Sunday

Key idea

～のに means "even though A, B" and it tells the listener that you find B unexpected or unfair given A.
