～以上（は）: now that / seeing that

～以上（は）（いじょう（は））follows a plain-form clause and means "now that X is the case" or "seeing that X". The first clause states an established fact; the second clause draws the obligation, resolve, or natural conclusion that follows from it.

The second half therefore tends to end in expressions of duty or determination: べきだ, なければならない, つもりだ, わけにはいかない. It is close in meaning to ～からには, but ～以上（は）sounds a touch more formal and written.

1. An obligation that follows from a fact

Given who or what someone is, a certain behavior is naturally expected.

学生である以上、勉強すべきだ。

"Now that you are a student, you ought to study."

  • 学生（がくせい）= student
  • である = to be (formal written form of だ)
  • 以上（いじょう）= now that, seeing that
  • 勉強すべきだ（べんきょうすべきだ）= ought to study

2. A determination that follows from one's own act

After a verb in the past tense, ～以上（は）often introduces the speaker's resolve: having done X, backing out is not an option.

約束した以上、最後までやります。

"Now that I've promised, I'll see it through to the end."

  • 約束した（やくそくした）= promised
  • 以上（いじょう）= now that, seeing that
  • 最後まで（さいごまで）= to the very end
  • やります = will do (it)

How to form it

Verb plain form + 以上（は）; nouns and な-adjectives take である + 以上（は）

  • 引き受ける（ひきうける）= to take on → 引き受けた以上（ひきうけたいじょう）= now that (I) have taken it on
  • やる = to do → やる以上（やるいじょう）= as long as (I'm) doing it
  • 学生だ（がくせいだ）= to be a student → 学生である以上（がくせいであるいじょう）= now that (you) are a student

Key idea

～以上（は） says: the fact is settled, so act accordingly. Once X is true, the speaker treats the duty or resolve in the second clause as the only reasonable consequence.
