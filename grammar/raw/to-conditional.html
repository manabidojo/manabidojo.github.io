～と: when one thing happens, another naturally follows

The conditional ～と connects two clauses so that whenever the first one holds, the second one follows naturally, automatically, or habitually. It is made by putting と after a plain non-past verb or adjective, and it matches English "when ..." or "whenever ...".

Unlike ～たら, which can describe a one-time event or a personal decision, ～と is used for results that reliably follow whenever the condition is met.

1. Natural and habitual results

Use ～と for things that happen as a law of nature or as a regular habit.

春になると桜が咲く。

"When spring comes, the cherry trees bloom."

  • 春（はる）= spring
  • に = result marker (into, used with なる)
  • なると = when (it) becomes
  • 桜（さくら）= cherry trees, cherry blossoms
  • が = subject marker
  • 咲く（さく）= to bloom

2. Machines, directions, and fixed outcomes

～と is the natural choice for explaining how machines work or what always happens in a certain situation.

このボタンを押すと、ドアが開きます。

"When you press this button, the door opens."

  • この = this
  • ボタン = button
  • を = object marker
  • 押すと（おすと）= when (you) press
  • ドア = door
  • が = subject marker
  • 開きます（あきます）= opens

How to form it

Plain non-past form + と

  • なる = to become → なると = when (it) becomes
  • 押す（おす）= to press → 押すと（おすと）= when (you) press
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べると（たべると）= when (you) eat

Key idea

Think of ～と as "X leads to Y, every time". If the result is automatic, natural, or habitual rather than a one-off choice, ～と is the conditional you want.
