～かねない: might do something, sth. might happen (undesirable)

～かねない attaches to the ます-stem of a verb and warns that something bad could easily happen. It maps to English "might well do" or "is liable to do".

Be careful: the ない at the end does not make this negative. The whole unit かねない means "might do", the exact opposite trap of ～かねる, which looks affirmative but means "cannot do".

Warning that something undesirable could happen

Use ～かねない when you judge that a person or situation could realistically lead to a bad outcome. It is almost always used for negative possibilities, not good ones.

彼はそんなことを言いかねない。

"He might well say something like that."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • そんな = that kind of
  • こと = thing
  • を = object marker
  • 言いかねない（いいかねない）= might well say

It is also common with situations rather than people, often after a conditional clause.

急いで運転すると、事故を起こしかねない。

"If you drive in a hurry, you might well cause an accident."

  • 急いで（いそいで）= in a hurry
  • 運転すると（うんてんすると）= if (you) drive
  • 事故（じこ）= accident
  • を = object marker
  • 起こしかねない（おこしかねない）= might well cause

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + かねない

  • 言う（いう）= to say → 言いかねない（いいかねない）= might well say
  • する = to do → しかねない = might well do
  • 起こす（おこす）= to cause → 起こしかねない（おこしかねない）= might well cause

Key idea

～かねない means "I wouldn't put it past this person/situation": despite the ない, it asserts that an undesirable outcome is a real possibility.
