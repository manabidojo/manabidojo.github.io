～ことに（は）: emphasizing a feeling (to my surprise)

～ことに after an emotional word, like 驚いた or 嬉しい, puts the speaker's feeling first before the fact that caused it.

In English it is "to my (surprise / joy / regret)...". It is a bit formal and common in writing and speeches.

Leading with the feeling

The emotion comes first, then the fact that caused it. Surprise, joy and regret all work the same way.

驚いたことに、彼は一人で全部やった。

"To my surprise, he did it all by himself."

  • 驚いた（おどろいた）= was surprised
  • ことに = (to my)
  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 一人で（ひとりで）= by himself
  • 全部（ぜんぶ）= all
  • やった = did

残念なことに、雨で花火大会が中止になった。

"Unfortunately, the fireworks festival was canceled because of rain."

  • 残念な（ざんねんな）= regrettable
  • ことに = (to my)
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • で = because of
  • 花火大会（はなびたいかい）= fireworks festival
  • 中止になった（ちゅうしになった）= was canceled

How to form it

い-adjective / な-adjective + な / verb た-form + ことに（は）

  • 嬉しい（うれしい）= happy → 嬉しいことに（うれしいことに）= happily
  • 不思議（ふしぎ）= strange → 不思議なことに（ふしぎなことに）= strangely
  • 困った（こまった）= was troubled → 困ったことに（こまったことに）= unfortunately, annoyingly

Key idea

～ことに leads with the feeling: before you hear what happened, you know how the speaker feels about it.
