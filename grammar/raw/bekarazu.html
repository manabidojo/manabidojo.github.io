～べからず: must not (on signs)

～べからず is the classical negative of べし ("should"). It is a strong, formal prohibition, typically seen on signs and in old rules.

In English it is "must not", "do not" or "no...", as on a sign.

1. Prohibitions on signs

It gives a short, strict rule.

芝生に入るべからず。

"Keep off the grass."

  • 芝生（しばふ）= lawn, grass
  • に = into
  • 入る（はいる）= to enter
  • べからず = must not

2. Mottos and proverbs

It also appears in famous sayings.

初心忘るべからず。

"Never forget your beginner's spirit."

  • 初心（しょしん）= one's original intention
  • 忘る（わする）= to forget (classical)
  • べからず = must not

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + べからず (する becomes するべからず or すべからず)

  • 捨てる（すてる）= to throw away → ゴミを捨てるべからず（ゴミをすてるべからず）= do not litter
  • 触れる（ふれる）= to touch → 触れるべからず（ふれるべからず）= do not touch
  • 働く（はたらく）= to work → 働かざる者食うべからず（はたらかざるものくうべからず）= those who don't work shall not eat

In modern speech, ～てはいけない or ～べきではない are used instead. 初心忘るべからず is a famous saying attributed to Zeami.

Key idea

～べからず is a rule carved in stone: short, strict and old-fashioned.
