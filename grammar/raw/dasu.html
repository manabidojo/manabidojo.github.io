～出す: suddenly start to

～出す（～だす）attaches to the ます-stem of a verb and means that the action begins suddenly or unexpectedly: 泣き出す "burst into tears", 降り出す "start to rain".

English usually translates this as "suddenly started to ..." or "burst out ...". The similar pattern ～始める is a neutral "begin to", while ～出す adds the feeling that the start was abrupt and often unplanned.

A sudden, unexpected start

Use ～出す when something kicks off all at once, often catching people off guard.

赤ちゃんが急に泣き出した。

"The baby suddenly started crying."

  • 赤ちゃん（あかちゃん）= baby
  • が = subject marker
  • 急に（きゅうに）= suddenly
  • 泣き出した（なきだした）= burst into tears

家に帰る途中で、雨が降り出した。

"On my way home, it started to rain."

  • 家（いえ）= home
  • に = direction marker
  • 帰る（かえる）= to return
  • 途中で（とちゅうで）= on the way
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • が = subject marker
  • 降り出した（ふりだした）= started to fall

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + 出す

  • 泣く（なく）= to cry → 泣き出す（なきだす）= burst into tears
  • 降る（ふる）= to fall (rain) → 降り出す（ふりだす）= start to rain
  • 走る（はしる）= to run → 走り出す（はしりだす）= break into a run

Key idea

～出す marks the moment something bursts into action. If 始める is a calm "begin", 出す is the action suddenly leaping out into the open.
