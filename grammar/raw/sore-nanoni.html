それなのに／なのに: and yet / even so

それなのに at the start of a sentence connects a fact with a result that goes against expectations. なのに is the shorter, more casual form.

In English it is "and yet", "even so" or "despite that". Like ～のに, it carries surprise or dissatisfaction.

1. An unfair or disappointing result

The first sentence sets up an expectation that the second one breaks.

一生懸命勉強した。それなのに落ちた。

"I studied really hard. And yet I failed."

  • 一生懸命（いっしょうけんめい）= as hard as possible
  • 勉強した（べんきょうした）= studied
  • それなのに = and yet
  • 落ちた（おちた）= failed

2. Casual complaint

なのに alone at the start of a sentence sounds more emotional.

約束したよね。なのに、どうして来なかったの？

"You promised, right? So why didn't you come?"

  • 約束した（やくそくした）= promised
  • よね = right?
  • なのに = and yet
  • どうして = why
  • 来なかった（こなかった）= didn't come
  • の = (question)

How to form it

Sentence A. それなのに、sentence B. それなのに never changes form; なのに is the casual short version.

  • 一生懸命練習した（いっしょうけんめいれんしゅうした）= practiced really hard → それなのに、負けた（それなのに、まけた）= and yet (we) lost
  • 約束した（やくそくした）= promised → なのに、来なかった（なのに、こなかった）= and yet (they) didn't come
  • 予報は晴れだった（よほうははれだった）= the forecast said sunny → それなのに、雨が降った（それなのに、あめがふった）= and yet it rained

Key idea

それなのに links two sentences where the second one shouldn't have happened, given the first.
