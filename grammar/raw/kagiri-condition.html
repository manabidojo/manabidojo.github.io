～限り（は）／～ない限り: as long as / unless

限り means "limit". In this use, ～限り says that a result holds as long as a situation continues, and ～ない限り says that it holds unless something happens.

In English it is "as long as" and "unless".

1. As long as something doesn't happen

With the ない-form, it means "unless".

諦めない限り、チャンスはある。

"As long as you don't give up, there's a chance."

  • 諦めない（あきらめない）= not give up
  • 限り（かぎり）= as long as
  • チャンス = chance
  • は = topic marker
  • ある = there is

2. As long as a situation lasts

With a positive form, it ties the result to an ongoing situation.

私が生きている限り、この店は続ける。

"As long as I'm alive, I'll keep this shop going."

  • 私（わたし）= I
  • が = subject marker
  • 生きている（いきている）= am alive
  • 限り（かぎり）= as long as
  • この = this
  • 店（みせ）= shop
  • 続ける（つづける）= to continue

How to form it

Verb dictionary form / ている-form + 限り, Verb ない-form + 限り

  • 雨が降る（あめがふる）= it rains → 雨が降らない限り（あめがふらないかぎり）= unless it rains
  • 知っている（しっている）= to know → 私が知っている限り（わたしがしっているかぎり）= as far as I know
  • 健康である（けんこうである）= be healthy → 健康である限り（けんこうであるかぎり）= as long as (I'm) healthy

The other ～限り, as in できる限り ("as much as possible"), is a different pattern about extent.

Key idea

～限り draws a boundary: within it, the result holds; with ない, the result holds until something breaks it.
