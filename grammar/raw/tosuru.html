～とする: try to / about to, suppose that, as a role

～とする presents something not simply as a fact, but as the basis or frame the rest of the sentence works from. After a volitional form (～よう / ～おう), it describes an action that is about to happen or is being attempted: "try to ~, be about to ~". After a plain clause, it introduces a hypothesis ("suppose that..."). After nouns, it formally treats or designates one thing as another ("regard A as B", "have A as B").

1. Attempt or the verge of an action (volitional + とする)

Volitional + とする describes an action that was attempted or just about to happen, often one that gets interrupted or does not succeed.

家を出ようとしたとき、電話が鳴った。

"Just as I was about to leave the house, the phone rang."

  • 家（いえ）= house, home
  • を = object marker
  • 出ようとした（でようとした）= was about to leave (volitional 出よう plus とする)
  • とき = when, the moment
  • 電話（でんわ）= telephone
  • が = subject marker
  • 鳴った（なった）= rang

2. Assumption: "suppose that ~"

After a plain clause, often as とすると / としたら / とすれば, it lays down a hypothesis and reasons from it.

明日雨が降るとすると、試合は中止になるだろう。

"Supposing it rains tomorrow, the match will probably be called off."

  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • が = subject marker
  • 降る（ふる）= to fall (of rain)
  • とすると = supposing that
  • 試合（しあい）= match, game
  • 中止になる（ちゅうしになる）= to be called off
  • だろう = probably

3. Treating A as B

AをBとする formally treats or designates A as B. This use is common in written and official Japanese.

私たちは彼をリーダーとするチームを作った。

"We formed a team with him as its leader."

  • 私たち（わたしたち）= we
  • 彼（かれ）= he, him
  • を = object marker
  • リーダーとする = having as leader, designating as leader
  • チーム = team
  • 作った（つくった）= made, formed

How to form it

Verb volitional + とする (attempt), plain clause + とすると／としたら (assumption), or Noun を Noun とする (role).

  • 出る（でる）= to leave → 出ようとする（でようとする）= to be about to leave
  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行こうとする（いこうとする）= to try to go
  • 降る（ふる）= to rain → 降るとすると（ふるとすると）= supposing it rains

Key idea

とする sets up a framework the rest of the sentence works within. That framework may be an action that is about to happen, a hypothetical situation, or a formal way of treating or designating something.
