～ものだから: because (explaining an excuse)

～ものだから gives a reason that excuses the result, usually something the speaker couldn't help. In casual speech it becomes ～もんだから.

In English it is "because" or "you see, ...". It sounds more apologetic than から, as the speaker explains why something went wrong.

Explaining why something went wrong

The first clause gives the circumstances, and the second states a result the speaker couldn't help, like being late or freezing up.

道が混んでいたものだから、遅れてしまった。

"The roads were packed, so I ended up late."

  • 道（みち）= road
  • が = subject marker
  • 混んでいた（こんでいた）= was crowded
  • ものだから = because (excuse)
  • 遅れてしまった（おくれてしまった）= ended up late

あまりに驚いたものだから、何も言えなかった。

"I was so surprised that I couldn't say anything."

  • あまりに = so, too
  • 驚いた（おどろいた）= was surprised
  • ものだから = because
  • 何も（なにも）= nothing
  • 言えなかった（いえなかった）= couldn't say

How to form it

Plain form + ものだから (nouns and な-adjectives take なものだから)

  • 忙しい（いそがしい）= busy → 忙しいものだから（いそがしいものだから）= because (I) was busy
  • 初めて（はじめて）= first time → 初めてなものだから（はじめてなものだから）= because it's my first time
  • 知らなかった（しらなかった）= didn't know → 知らなかったもんだから（しらなかったもんだから）= because (I) didn't know

Key idea

～ものだから explains an excuse: the circumstances left the speaker little choice.
