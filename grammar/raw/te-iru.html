～ている: ongoing action or resulting state

～ている is one of the first important Japanese grammar patterns learners meet. It is made by taking the て-form of a verb and adding いる.

For English speakers, it often feels similar to "is/am/are doing", but it can also mean that something has happened and the result is still true now.

1. Ongoing action

Use ～ている when an action is happening right now.

今、本を読んでいます。

"I am reading a book right now."

  • 今（いま）= now
  • 本（ほん）= book
  • を = object marker
  • 読んでいます（よんでいます）= am reading

2. Resulting state

Sometimes ～ている does not mean "is doing". Instead, it means that an action happened before, and the result is still visible or true.

ドアが開いています。

"The door is open."

  • ドア = door
  • が = subject marker
  • 開いています（あいています）= is open

In this example, the door is not "opening right now". Someone opened it earlier, and now it is in the state of being open.

How to form it

Verb て-form + いる

  • 読む（よむ）= to read → 読んでいる（よんでいる）= is reading
  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べている（たべている）= is eating
  • 開く（あく）= to open → 開いている（あいている）= is open

Key idea

Think of ～ている as describing what is true now. That can be an action happening now, or a state that exists now because of something that happened earlier.
