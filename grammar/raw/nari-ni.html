～なりに: in one's own way

～なりに after a noun, often a person, says that someone acts within their own abilities or situation, even if the result is modest.

In English it is "in one's own way" or "as best (I) can". It is often used humbly about oneself.

1. Doing your best in your own way

The speaker admits their limits but stresses their effort.

私なりに頑張ってみた。

"I tried my best in my own way."

  • 私（わたし）= I
  • なりに = in (my) own way
  • 頑張ってみた（がんばってみた）= tried my best

2. Respecting someone's view

About others, it acknowledges that they have their own reasons.

子供は子供なりに悩みがある。

"Children have their own worries too."

  • 子供（こども）= children
  • は = topic marker
  • 子供なりに（こどもなりに）= in their own way as children
  • 悩み（なやみ）= worries
  • がある = there are

How to form it

Noun + なりに／なりの + noun, Plain form + なりに

  • 自分（じぶん）= oneself → 自分なりに（じぶんなりに）= in my own way
  • 彼（かれ）= he → 彼なりの考え（かれなりのかんがえ）= his own way of thinking
  • 若い（わかい）= young → 若いなりに（わかいなりに）= in their own way as young people

Don't confuse it with ～なり ("as soon as") after a verb, as in 帰るなり寝た.

Key idea

～なりに gives credit to effort within limits: not perfect, but done in one's own way.
