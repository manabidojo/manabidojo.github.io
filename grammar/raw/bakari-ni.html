～ばかりに: simply because of that one thing

～ばかりに attaches to a plain-form clause and pinpoints it as the single cause of an undesirable result. It maps to English "simply because" or "just because", with a tone of regret or frustration: if only that one thing had been different, the bad outcome would not have happened.

Do not confuse it with plain ～ばかり (just did / nothing but) — adding に turns it into a cause-and-effect connective, and the effect is almost always negative.

1. A single circumstance, an unwanted result

The clause before ばかりに names the one factor; the clause after it states the consequence the speaker is unhappy about.

お金がないばかりに旅行に行けない。

"It's only because I have no money that I can't travel."

  • お金（おかね）= money
  • が = subject marker
  • ないばかりに = just because (I) don't have
  • 旅行（りょこう）= trip, travel
  • に = direction marker
  • 行けない（いけない）= cannot go

2. Regret over one's own action

With a past-tense clause, ばかりに often expresses regret: one small action or mistake led to a disproportionate consequence.

パスポートを忘れたばかりに、飛行機に乗れませんでした。

"Simply because I forgot my passport, I couldn't board the plane."

  • パスポート = passport
  • を = object marker
  • 忘れたばかりに（わすれたばかりに）= simply because (I) forgot
  • 飛行機（ひこうき）= airplane
  • に = direction marker
  • 乗れませんでした（のれませんでした）= could not board

How to form it

Verb / い-adjective plain form + ばかりに

  • ない = to not have → ないばかりに = just because (I) don't have
  • 忘れる（わすれる）= to forget → 忘れたばかりに（わすれたばかりに）= just because (I) forgot
  • 知らない（しらない）= to not know → 知らないばかりに（しらないばかりに）= just because (I) don't know

Key idea

～ばかりに puts a spotlight on one cause and sighs: "that alone is why things went wrong." For neutral reasons with no regret, use から or ので instead.
