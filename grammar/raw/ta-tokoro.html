～たところ（結果）: when I did it, it turned out (result)

～たところ after a verb in the た-form says that the speaker did something and then found out a result. The second clause describes what was discovered or what happened next.

In English it is "when I did..., (it turned out that)...". It is a bit formal and common in reports and stories.

1. Discovering a result

The action is usually a test or an attempt, and the result is something the speaker learned from it.

先生に聞いてみたところ、すぐに答えてくれた。

"When I asked the teacher, she answered right away."

  • 先生（せんせい）= teacher
  • に = to
  • 聞いてみた（きいてみた）= tried asking
  • ところ = when (I did)
  • すぐに = right away
  • 答えてくれた（こたえてくれた）= answered (for me)

2. An unexpected outcome

The result is often a little surprising.

駅に電話したところ、忘れ物が見つかったそうだ。

"When I called the station, they said they'd found my lost item."

  • 駅（えき）= station
  • に = to
  • 電話した（でんわした）= called
  • ところ = when (I did)
  • 忘れ物（わすれもの）= lost item
  • 見つかった（みつかった）= was found
  • そうだ = they said

How to form it

Verb た-form + ところ

  • 調べる（しらべる）= to look up → 調べたところ（しらべたところ）= when (I) looked it up
  • 頼む（たのむ）= to ask → 頼んだところ（たのんだところ）= when (I) asked
  • 使う（つかう）= to use → 使ってみたところ（つかってみたところ）= when (I) tried using

Don't confuse it with ～たところだ ("just did"), which ends the sentence. Here ところ leads into a second clause.

Key idea

～たところ links an action to what it revealed: "I did this, and here's what happened".
