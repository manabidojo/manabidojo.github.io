～ぬ: literary negative (= ない)

～ぬ is the classical negative ending, the old equivalent of ない. It survives in set phrases, proverbs and literary writing, and is common in historical dramas and samurai characters in anime.

In English it is simply "not". Before a noun, it describes something that "does not" happen.

1. In set phrases

Many everyday expressions still use ぬ.

知らぬ間に眠ってしまった。

"I fell asleep without realizing it."

  • 知らぬ（しらぬ）= not knowing
  • 間に（まに）= while
  • 眠ってしまった（ねむってしまった）= fell asleep

2. In old-fashioned speech

In period dramas and anime, characters use it instead of ない. In speech it often becomes ん.

そんなことは許さぬ。

"I will not allow such a thing."

  • そんなこと = such a thing
  • は = topic marker
  • 許さぬ（ゆるさぬ）= will not allow

How to form it

Verb ない-stem + ぬ (する becomes せぬ)

  • 見る（みる）= to see → 見知らぬ人（みしらぬひと）= a stranger
  • 言う（いう）= to say → 言わぬが花（いわぬがはな）= better left unsaid
  • 思う（おもう）= to think → 思わぬ（おもわぬ）= unexpected

In speech, ぬ often shortens to ん: 知らん, 分からん. The connecting form ～ず (知らず, 言わず) belongs to the same classical negative.

Key idea

～ぬ is ない from an older era, still alive in set phrases and in dramatic speech.
