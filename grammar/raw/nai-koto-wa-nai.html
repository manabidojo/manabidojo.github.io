～ないことはない: it's not that (I) can't / not impossible

～ないことはない is a double negative: "it's not that I don't / can't". It gives a weak, reluctant yes.

In English it is "it's not that I can't...", "I could, I guess" or "it's not impossible".

1. A reluctant possibility

The speaker admits something is possible, but without enthusiasm.

難しいが、できないことはない。

"It's hard, but not impossible."

  • 難しい（むずかしい）= difficult
  • が = but
  • できない = can't do
  • ことはない = it's not the case that

2. A hesitant yes

As an answer, it says "yes, but not really happily".

「お酒、飲む？」「飲めないことはないけど…」

""Do you drink?" "It's not that I can't, but...""

  • お酒（おさけ）= alcohol
  • 飲む（のむ）= to drink
  • 飲めない（のめない）= can't drink
  • ことはない = it's not the case
  • けど = but

How to form it

Verb ない-form + ことはない, い-adjective (drop い) + くないことはない

  • 行ける（いける）= can go → 行けないことはない（いけないことはない）= I could go, I guess
  • 分かる（わかる）= to understand → 分からないことはない（わからないことはない）= I sort of understand
  • 好き（すき）= liked → 好きじゃないことはない（すきじゃないことはない）= it's not that I dislike it

Key idea

～ないことはない is a yes with hesitation: possible, but not something the speaker is eager about.
