～たがる: someone else wants to

～たい expresses the speaker's own wish. For other people, Japanese uses ～たがる, which describes a wish you can only see from the outside.

In English it is "(he / she) wants to". Japanese avoids claiming to know other people's feelings directly, so it describes their behavior instead.

1. Describing someone else's wish

It often describes a general tendency, like children or pets always wanting something.

子供は外で遊びたがる。

"Kids always want to play outside."

  • 子供（こども）= child
  • は = topic marker
  • 外（そと）= outside
  • で = at
  • 遊びたがる（あそびたがる）= want to play

2. A current wish

For a wish someone has right now, ～たがっている is usual.

妹が日本に行きたがっている。

"My little sister wants to go to Japan."

  • 妹（いもうと）= younger sister
  • が = subject marker
  • 日本（にほん）= Japan
  • に = to
  • 行きたがっている（いきたがっている）= wants to go

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + たがる

  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べたがる（たべたがる）= want to eat
  • 見る（みる）= to see → 見たがっている（みたがっている）= wants to see
  • 話す（はなす）= to talk → 話したがらない（はなしたがらない）= doesn't want to talk

たがる conjugates like a u-verb: たがらない, たがった, たがっている.

Key idea

～たい is for me, ～たがる is for others: it describes a wish that shows in someone's behavior.
