～んばかり: as if about to

～んばかり after a verb's ない-stem says that something is so close to happening that it almost seems to happen. ん is an old form of the volitional.

In English it is "as if about to" or "almost". It is literary and often describes strong feelings shown on the face or body.

1. On the verge of an action

It describes how someone looks or acts, almost doing something.

彼女は泣き出さんばかりの顔をしていた。

"She looked as if she was about to burst into tears."

  • 彼女（かのじょ）= she
  • は = topic marker
  • 泣き出さん（なきださん）= (about to) burst into tears
  • ばかりの = as if
  • 顔（かお）= face
  • をしていた = had

2. Exaggerated description

It also exaggerates for effect.

会場は割れんばかりの拍手に包まれた。

"The hall was filled with thunderous applause."

  • 会場（かいじょう）= hall
  • は = topic marker
  • 割れん（われん）= (about to) break
  • ばかりの = as if
  • 拍手（はくしゅ）= applause
  • に包まれた（につつまれた）= was wrapped in

How to form it

Verb ない-stem + んばかり（に／の／だ） (する becomes せんばかり)

  • 言う（いう）= to say → 言わんばかり（いわんばかり）= as if to say
  • 溢れる（あふれる）= to overflow → 溢れんばかり（あふれんばかり）= about to overflow
  • 飛び上がる（とびあがる）= to jump up → 飛び上がらんばかり（とびあがらんばかり）= almost jumping up

言わんばかりに ("as if to say") is a very common combination.

Key idea

～んばかり freezes a moment just before something happens: almost, but not quite.
