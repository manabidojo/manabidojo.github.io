～まい: will not / probably not

～まい is a literary negative ending with two meanings: a strong intention not to do something, and a guess that something won't happen.

In English it is "I will never..." or "probably won't". It is formal or old-fashioned, but appears in novels, speeches and set phrases.

1. A firm resolution

With the speaker as subject, it expresses a strong decision not to do something.

二度とあの店には行くまい。

"I'll never go to that place again."

  • 二度と（にどと）= never again
  • あの = that
  • 店（みせ）= shop, place
  • には = to
  • 行くまい（いくまい）= will not go

2. A negative guess

With other subjects, it means "probably not".

この雨では、彼は来るまい。

"With this rain, he probably won't come."

  • この = this
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • では = with
  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 来るまい（くるまい）= probably won't come

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + まい (ru-verbs can also take ます-stem + まい: 見まい; する becomes するまい or しまい)

  • 言う（いう）= to say → 言うまい（いうまい）= will not say
  • 負ける（まける）= to lose → 負けまい（まけまい）= will not lose
  • 忘れる（わすれる）= to forget → 忘れまい（わすれまい）= will never forget

In everyday speech, ～ないつもりだ or ～ないだろう are used instead.

Key idea

～まい is a formal "no": either a vow not to do something, or a guess that it won't happen.
