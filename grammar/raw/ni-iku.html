～に行く／～に来る（目的）: going or coming to do something (purpose)

A verb's ます-stem plus に in front of a movement verb such as 行く, 来る or 帰る gives the purpose of the movement.

In English it maps to "go to do" or "come to do". The stem says what you will do, the movement verb says how you get there.

1. Going somewhere for a purpose

The activity comes first, then に, then the verb of movement.

友達と映画を見に行く。

"I'm going to see a movie with a friend."

  • 友達（ともだち）= friend
  • と = with
  • 映画（えいが）= movie
  • を = object marker
  • 見に（みに）= to see
  • 行く（いく）= to go

2. Coming to do something

With 来る the movement is toward the speaker.

姉が手伝いに来てくれた。

"My older sister came to help me."

  • 姉（あね）= older sister
  • が = subject marker
  • 手伝いに（てつだいに）= to help
  • 来てくれた（きてくれた）= came (for me)

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + に + 行く／来る／帰る

  • 食べる（たべる）= to eat → 食べに行く（たべにいく）= go to eat
  • 遊ぶ（あそぶ）= to play → 遊びに来る（あそびにくる）= come over to hang out
  • 取る（とる）= to fetch → 取りに帰る（とりにかえる）= go back home to get

With nouns for activities, に attaches to the noun directly: 買い物に行く (go shopping), 旅行に行く (go on a trip).

Key idea

～に行く packs a reason into a movement: whatever stands before に is why you go or come.
