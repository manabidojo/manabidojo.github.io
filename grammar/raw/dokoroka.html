～どころか: far from / let alone

～どころか takes what the listener might expect and flatly contradicts it. It attaches to a noun or a plain-form verb or adjective and maps to English "far from" or "let alone".

Unlike a neutral correction with ～ではなくて ("not X but Y"), ～どころか carries emphasis and sometimes surprise: the reality is on a completely different level from the expectation.

1. Far from X — actually the opposite

The word before どころか names the expectation; what follows states the surprising reality, often the exact opposite.

安いどころか高い。

"Far from being cheap, it's expensive."

  • 安い（やすい）= cheap
  • どころか = far from (being)
  • 高い（たかい）= expensive

2. Let alone X — not even the easier thing

～どころか also denies something big by pointing out that even something much smaller is not true. The second clause often uses も / さえ with a negative.

弟は漢字どころか、ひらがなも読めない。

"My little brother can't even read hiragana, let alone kanji."

  • 弟（おとうと）= younger brother
  • は = topic marker
  • 漢字（かんじ）= kanji
  • どころか = let alone
  • ひらがな = hiragana
  • も = even (here)
  • 読めない（よめない）= cannot read

How to form it

Noun / plain-form verb / い-adjective + どころか

  • 安い（やすい）= cheap → 安いどころか（やすいどころか）= far from cheap
  • 漢字（かんじ）= kanji → 漢字どころか（かんじどころか）= let alone kanji
  • 寝る（ねる）= to sleep → 寝るどころか（ねるどころか）= far from sleeping

Key idea

～どころか knocks down an expectation and replaces it with something more extreme: "X? Not at all. The truth is Y, and Y is on a whole different level."
