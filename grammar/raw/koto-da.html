～ことだ: you should (firm advice)

～ことだ after a verb gives firm advice about what is best to do. It sounds like a teacher or an older person stating the right course of action.

In English it is "you should" or "the best thing is to". With the ない-form, it says what to avoid.

1. Advice on how to succeed

It often follows a goal stated with ～たければ or ～なら.

上手になりたければ、毎日練習することだ。

"If you want to get better, practice every day."

  • 上手に（じょうずに）= skilled
  • なりたければ = if (you) want to become
  • 毎日（まいにち）= every day
  • 練習する（れんしゅうする）= to practice
  • ことだ = you should

2. Advice on what to avoid

With the ない-form, it warns against something.

風邪を引いたら、無理をしないことだ。

"If you catch a cold, don't push yourself."

  • 風邪（かぜ）= cold
  • 引いたら（ひいたら）= if (you) catch
  • 無理をしない（むりをしない）= not overdo it
  • ことだ = you should

How to form it

Verb dictionary form / ない-form + ことだ

  • 寝る（ねる）= to sleep → よく寝ることだ（よくねることだ）= you should sleep well
  • 諦める（あきらめる）= to give up → 諦めないことだ（あきらめないことだ）= you shouldn't give up
  • 聞く（きく）= to ask → 人に聞くことだ（ひとにきくことだ）= you should ask someone

Key idea

～ことだ is advice from experience: "this is what you should do", said with some authority.
