～が: but / however

～が connects two sentences into one, just like English "but". You attach が to the end of a complete clause, then continue with a second clause.

Be careful: this is a different が from the subject marker. The subject marker comes after a noun (犬が = "the dog"), while this が comes after a whole sentence. Compared with the casual connector ～けど, ～が sounds a little more formal and is common in writing.

1. Contrast: "but"

The most common use is to join two clauses that point in opposite directions.

駅まで走ったが、間に合わなかった。

"I ran to the station, but didn't make it."

  • 駅（えき）= station
  • まで = "as far as" (limit marker)
  • 走った（はしった）= ran
  • が = but
  • 間に合わなかった（まにあわなかった）= didn't make it in time

2. Soft opener

～が can also act as a polite cushion. Here it does not really mean "but"; it just softens what comes next.

すみませんが、駅はどこですか。

"Excuse me, but where is the station?"

  • すみません = excuse me
  • が = softener ("but")
  • 駅（えき）= station
  • は = topic marker
  • どこ = where
  • ですか = polite question ending

How to form it

Clause (plain or polite form) + が + clause

  • 行きました（いきました）= went → 行きましたが（いきましたが）= went, but
  • 高い（たかい）= expensive → 高いですが（たかいですが）= it is expensive, but
  • 食べた（たべた）= ate → 食べたが（たべたが）= ate, but

Key idea

～が connects two clauses. It can express contrast like "but", or act as a polite softener before a question, request, or explanation.
