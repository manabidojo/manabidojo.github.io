～つもり: intend to / plan to

～つもり expresses the speaker's intention or plan. It is made by putting つもりだ／つもりです after the dictionary form of a verb.

For English speakers, it maps closely to "I plan to ..." or "I intend to ...". It's a decision you have already made, not just a wish.

1. Stating a plan

Use the dictionary form of a verb plus つもりです to say what you have decided to do.

来年日本に行くつもりです。

"I plan to go to Japan next year."

  • 来年（らいねん）= next year
  • 日本（にほん）= Japan
  • に = direction marker ("to")
  • 行く（いく）= to go
  • つもりです = plan to / intend to

2. Stating what you do not plan to do

To say you intend not to do something, put the ない-form of the verb before つもり.

明日は何も買わないつもりです。

"I don't plan to buy anything tomorrow."

  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • は = topic marker
  • 何も（なにも）= nothing (used with a negative verb)
  • 買わない（かわない）= will not buy
  • つもりです = intend to

～つもり vs ～たい

～たい ("want to") only expresses a desire, while ～つもり expresses a decision: 行きたい（いきたい）says you would like to go, but 行くつもりだ（いくつもりだ）says you have actually decided to go.

How to form it

Verb (dictionary form) + つもりだ／つもりです (negative plan: ない-form + つもりだ)

  • 行く（いく）= to go → 行くつもりだ（いくつもりだ）= intend to go
  • 帰る（かえる）= to go home → 帰るつもりです（かえるつもりです）= plan to go home
  • 食べない（たべない）= not eat → 食べないつもりだ（たべないつもりだ）= plan not to eat

Key idea

～つもり announces a decision you have already made: "this is what I mean to do." If it is still only a hope or a wish, use ～たい instead.
