～そう: looks like or appears

～そう expresses a guess based on what you can see right now. It attaches to the stem of a verb or adjective: the ます-stem of a verb (降ります → 降り), or an い-adjective with the final い dropped (美味しい → 美味し).

In English it maps to "looks ..." or "looks like it will ...". Do not confuse this with the hearsay pattern ～そうだ ("I hear that..." / "they say that..."), as in 雨が降るそうだ。("I hear it's going to rain.").

1. With adjectives: "looks ..."

Attached to an adjective stem, ～そう describes how something appears, before you have actually confirmed it.

このケーキは美味しそう。

"This cake looks delicious."

  • この = this
  • ケーキ = cake
  • は = topic marker
  • 美味しそう（おいしそう）= looks delicious

You have not tasted the cake yet — you are judging by its appearance.

2. With verbs: "looks like it will happen"

Attached to a verb stem, ～そう means something appears to be on the verge of happening.

空が暗いです。雨が降りそうです。

"The sky is dark. It looks like it's going to rain."

  • 空（そら）= sky
  • が = subject marker
  • 暗いです（くらいです）= is dark
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • 降りそうです（ふりそうです）= looks like it will fall

How to form it

Verb ます-stem + そう / い-adjective stem (drop い) + そう. The adjective いい is irregular: it becomes よさそう.

  • 降る（ふる）= to fall (rain) → 降りそう（ふりそう）= looks like it will rain
  • 美味しい（おいしい）= delicious → 美味しそう（おいしそう）= looks delicious
  • いい = good → よさそう = looks good

Key idea

This use of ～そう means "judging by what I see". Do not confuse 雨が降りそうだ ("It looks like it's going to rain") with 雨が降るそうだ ("I hear it's going to rain").
