～だらけ: covered in / full of something undesirable

～だらけ attaches directly to a noun and means that something is covered all over with or riddled with that thing. It almost always describes something unwelcome: mud, mistakes, scratches, dust.

In English it maps to "covered in ~" or "full of ~" with a complaining tone. Unlike ～でいっぱい, which is a neutral "full of" and can describe good things, ～だらけ implies an unpleasant amount of something, everywhere.

1. Physically covered in something

Use ～だらけ when a surface is messily covered with something. Before another noun it takes の (泥だらけの靴 = mud-covered shoes).

服が泥だらけになった。

"My clothes got covered in mud."

  • 服（ふく）= clothes
  • が = subject marker
  • 泥だらけ（どろだらけ）= covered in mud
  • になった = became (turned into that state)

2. Full of mistakes or problems

～だらけ also works for abstract things that appear over and over, like errors or problems. The tone is critical: there are far too many of them.

このレポートは間違いだらけだ。

"This report is full of mistakes."

  • この = this
  • レポート = report
  • は = topic marker
  • 間違いだらけ（まちがいだらけ）= full of mistakes
  • だ = is (plain copula)

How to form it

Noun + だらけ

  • 泥（どろ）= mud → 泥だらけ（どろだらけ）= covered in mud
  • 間違い（まちがい）= mistake → 間違いだらけ（まちがいだらけ）= full of mistakes
  • 傷（きず）= scratch → 傷だらけ（きずだらけ）= covered in scratches

Key idea

～だらけ paints a picture of something undesirable scattered all over a surface or all through a thing. If you would say it with a sigh in English, ～だらけ is the right choice.
