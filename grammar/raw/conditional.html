～ば／～たら: saying "if" or "when"

～ば and ～たら are the two most common ways to say "if" in Japanese. ～ば is made by changing the end of a verb or adjective into the ば-form, and ～たら is simply the plain past (た) form plus ら.

Both map to English "if X, then ...", and ～たら can also mean "when X happens" for things that are sure to happen. Unlike ～なら, which reacts to something the listener has just said ("if that is the case"), ～ば and ～たら state an ordinary condition.

1. ～ば: a general condition

Use ～ば for a general or hypothetical condition: if the first part is true, the second part follows.

安ければ買う。

"If it's cheap, I'll buy it."

  • 安ければ（やすければ）= if it is cheap
  • 買う（かう）= will buy

2. ～たら: "if" and "when"

～たら works in almost any "if" sentence, which makes it the most flexible conditional in everyday speech.

雨が降ったら、家にいます。

"If it rains, I'll stay home."

  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • が = subject marker
  • 降ったら（ふったら）= if it rains
  • 家（いえ）= home
  • に = location marker
  • います = will stay

Because the たら clause is imagined as already finished, ～たら can also mean "when" for something certain to happen.

日本に着いたら、電話してください。

"When you arrive in Japan, please call me."

  • 日本（にほん）= Japan
  • に = destination marker
  • 着いたら（ついたら）= when (you) arrive
  • 電話してください（でんわしてください）= please call

How to form it

ば: change the verb's final -u sound to -e and add ば; for い-adjectives, replace い with ければ.

たら: take the plain past (た) form and add ら.

  • 買う（かう）= to buy → 買えば（かえば）= if (someone) buys
  • 読む（よむ）= to read → 読めば（よめば）= if (someone) reads
  • 安い（やすい）= cheap → 安ければ（やすければ）= if it is cheap
  • 降る（ふる）= to fall (rain, snow) → 降ったら（ふったら）= if/when it falls

Key idea

Both patterns hang a condition on the front of a sentence. ～ば states a plain "if", while ～たら pictures the condition as already completed "once X has happened, then ...", which is why it can mean both "if" and "when".
