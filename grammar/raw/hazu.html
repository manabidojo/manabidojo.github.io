～はずだ: should be / is supposed to (a confident expectation)

～はずだ follows a plain-form clause and expresses a confident expectation based on reasoning: given what the speaker knows, things should be that way. It does not state a fact, it states a well-founded conclusion.

English equivalents are "should" or "is supposed to" in the sense of expectation. Be careful: unlike ～べきだ, which is "should" as in duty ("you should apologize"), ～はずだ is "should" as in prediction ("he should arrive soon"). The negative ～はずがない ("there is no way that...") is a separate pattern.

1. Expectation about the present or future

The speaker has grounds like a schedule, a promise, common knowledge and concludes that something is or will be the case.

彼は来るはずだ。

"He should be coming."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • 来る（くる）= to come
  • はずだ = should / is expected to

2. Expectation about the past

With a past-tense clause, ～はずだ means something should have happened by now, as far as the speaker can reason.

彼女はもう家に着いたはずです。

"She should have arrived home by now."

  • 彼女（かのじょ）= she
  • もう = already, by now
  • 家（いえ）= home
  • に = destination marker
  • 着いた（ついた）= arrived
  • はずです = should have (polite expectation)

How to form it

Plain form + はずだ (nouns take のはずだ, な-adjectives take なはずだ)

  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来るはずだ（くるはずだ）= should come
  • 終わる（おわる）= to end → 終わったはずだ（おわったはずだ）= should have ended
  • 学生（がくせい）= student → 学生のはずだ（がくせいのはずだ）= should be a student

Key idea

～はずだ says: "based on what I know, this is what I expect to be true." If the evidence points one way, use はずだ; if you are telling someone their duty, that is べきだ.
