～し: listing reasons

～し attaches to the plain form of a verb, adjective, or noun + だ and lists reasons or qualities side by side: "X, and what's more, Y". Each item sounds like one reason in a list, and the list feels like it could go on.

Unlike the て-form, which links items in a neutral way, ～し presents each item as a reason. It often builds up to a conclusion the way ～から does, but feels softer, because it hints that there are even more reasons left unsaid.

1. Listing two or more reasons

Stack qualities with し to explain why something is good (or bad).

安いし、便利だ。

"It's cheap, and (besides) convenient."

  • 安い（やすい）= cheap
  • し = and (lists a reason)
  • 便利だ（べんりだ）= is convenient

The list often leads straight into a conclusion at the end of the sentence.

この店は安いし、おいしいし、よく来ます。

"This restaurant is cheap and the food is good, so I come here often."

  • この = this
  • 店（みせ）= restaurant, shop
  • は = topic marker
  • 安い（やすい）= cheap
  • し = and (lists a reason)
  • おいしい = delicious
  • よく = often
  • 来ます（きます）= come

2. A single し hinting at more reasons

Even one し can carry the "among other things" feeling: you give one reason and imply there are others.

今日は雨だし、家にいよう。

"It's raining today (among other things), so let's stay home."

  • 今日（きょう）= today
  • は = topic marker
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • だ = is (copula)
  • し = and what's more (lists a reason)
  • 家（いえ）= home
  • に = location marker
  • いよう = let's stay

How to form it

Plain form + し (nouns and な-adjectives take だし)

  • 安い（やすい）= cheap → 安いし（やすいし）= it is cheap, and ...
  • 便利だ（べんりだ）= is convenient → 便利だし（べんりだし）= it is convenient, and ...
  • ある = there is → あるし = there is ..., and ...

Key idea

Each し stamps an item as "one more reason". Because the list always feels open-ended, your conclusion sounds well supported without you having to spell out every reason.
