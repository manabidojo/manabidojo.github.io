まだ～ていない: not yet

まだ means "still" and ～ていない means "is not in a done state". Together they say that something hasn't happened yet but is still expected to.

In English it is "not yet" or "still haven't". Japanese uses ～ていない here, not the past negative; ～なかった would mean the chance is over.

1. Something hasn't happened yet

The action is still open: it may well happen later.

宿題はまだ終わっていない。

"I haven't finished my homework yet."

  • 宿題（しゅくだい）= homework
  • は = topic marker
  • まだ = still, yet
  • 終わっていない（おわっていない）= isn't finished

2. Answering "have you already...?"

A question with もう ("already") is answered with まだ when the answer is no. Often まだです alone is enough.

「もう昼ご飯を食べた？」「ううん、まだ食べていない。」

""Have you had lunch yet?" "No, not yet.""

  • もう = already
  • 昼ご飯（ひるごはん）= lunch
  • ううん = no (casual)
  • まだ = yet
  • 食べていない（たべていない）= haven't eaten

まだ～ていない vs ～なかった

まだ食べていない means "I haven't eaten yet": it may still happen. 食べなかった means "I didn't eat": the chance is over. Answering a もう question with ～なかった sounds as if the speaker gave up on it.

How to form it

まだ + verb て-form + いない

  • 来る（くる）= to come → まだ来ていない（まだきていない）= hasn't come yet
  • 決める（きめる）= to decide → まだ決めていない（まだきめていない）= haven't decided yet
  • 読む（よむ）= to read → まだ読んでいない（まだよんでいない）= haven't read it yet

In speech the い often drops: まだ食べてない. The polite form is まだ～ていません.

Key idea

まだ～ていない keeps the door open: it isn't done yet, but it still might be. It is the natural "no" to a もう question.
