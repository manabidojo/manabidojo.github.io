～みたい: like / seems like

～みたい is a colloquial marker of comparison ("like X") and conjecture ("it seems that ..."). It attaches directly to a noun or to the plain form of a verb or adjective, and behaves like a な-adjective afterwards (みたいだ, みたいな + noun, みたいに + verb).

It is the conversational counterpart of ようだ: same meanings, more casual register.

1. Comparison: "just like X"

After a noun, みたい draws a resemblance. The adverb まるで often sets up the comparison.

まるで夢みたいだ。

"It's just like a dream."

  • まるで = just like / completely (emphasizes the comparison)
  • 夢（ゆめ）= dream
  • みたいだ = is like

2. Conjecture: "it seems that ..."

After a plain-form clause, みたい marks a guess based on what you see, hear, or feel.

田中さんはもう帰ったみたいだ。

"It seems Tanaka has already gone home."

  • 田中さん（たなかさん）= Mr./Ms. Tanaka
  • は = topic marker
  • もう = already
  • 帰った（かえった）= went home
  • みたいだ = it seems

How to form it

Noun / plain form + みたい（だ） — then inflect like a な-adjective

  • 夢（ゆめ）= dream → 夢みたいだ（ゆめみたいだ）= is like a dream
  • 帰った（かえった）= went home → 帰ったみたいだ（かえったみたいだ）= it seems (he) went home
  • 子供（こども）= child → 子供みたいに泣く（こどもみたいになく）= cry like a child

Key idea

～みたい says "based on what I can see or feel, X resembles Y or appears to be the case", a casual, everyday ようだ.
