～より～のほうが: comparing two things (more than)

To compare two things, Japanese marks the standard with より ("than") and the thing that wins with のほうが ("the side of").

In English this is "B is more ... than A". The adjective at the end says in what way B wins.

1. Saying which one is more

The thing after より is the one being compared against; the one with のほうが is the winner.

電車より車のほうが速い。

"The car is faster than the train."

  • 電車（でんしゃ）= train
  • より = than
  • 車（くるま）= car
  • のほうが = (the side of)
  • 速い（はやい）= fast

2. Asking which one

For questions, list both options and ask with どちらのほうが ("which one").

夏と冬と、どちらのほうが好きですか。

"Which do you like better, summer or winter?"

  • 夏（なつ）= summer
  • と = and
  • 冬（ふゆ）= winter
  • どちらのほうが = which one
  • 好き（すき）= liked

How to form it

A より B のほうが + adjective

  • 東京（とうきょう）= Tokyo → 東京より（とうきょうより）= than Tokyo
  • 歩く（あるく）= to walk → 歩くほうが（あるくほうが）= walking (is more)
  • 今日（きょう）= today → 今日のほうが（きょうのほうが）= today (is more)

Either half can be left out when it is clear from context: 車のほうが速い (the car is faster).

Key idea

より marks what you measure against, のほうが marks the one that is "more". Together they cover almost every A versus B comparison.
