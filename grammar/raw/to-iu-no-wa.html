～というのは: explaining what something means

～というのは takes a word or situation as the topic in order to define or explain it. It is often answered with ～という意味だ or ～ことだ.

In English it is "what (it) means is..." or "the thing is...".

1. Defining a word

The word being defined goes before というのは, and the definition comes after.

「OK」というのは「大丈夫」という意味だ。

"'OK' means 'it's fine'."

  • 「OK」 = OK
  • というのは = as for (the word)
  • 大丈夫（だいじょうぶ）= it's fine
  • という意味だ（といういみだ）= means

2. Explaining a situation

It also explains the reason behind something.

遅れたというのは、電車が止まったからだ。

"The reason I was late is that the train stopped."

  • 遅れた（おくれた）= was late
  • というのは = the thing is
  • 電車（でんしゃ）= train
  • が = subject marker
  • 止まった（とまった）= stopped
  • からだ = it's because

How to form it

Noun / plain form + というのは

  • 友達（ともだち）= friend → 友達というのは（ともだちというのは）= a friend is (someone who)
  • 社会人（しゃかいじん）= working adult → 社会人というのは（しゃかいじんというのは）= being a working adult means
  • 来られない（こられない）= can't come → 来られないというのは（こられないというのは）= the fact that (you) can't come

In casual speech it shortens to って: OKって「大丈夫」って意味だよ.

Key idea

～というのは picks up a word or fact so that the rest of the sentence can explain it.
