～的: of that nature (like English -ic or -al)

的 (てき) attaches to a noun, usually a Sino-Japanese word, and turns it into a な-adjective meaning "of that nature" or "from that point of view".

In English it works like the endings "-ic", "-al" or "-like": 経済的 = economical, 個人的 = personal.

1. Describing the nature of something

Before a noun, 的 takes な.

これは日本的な考え方だ。

"This is a very Japanese way of thinking."

  • これ = this
  • は = topic marker
  • 日本的な（にほんてきな）= Japanese (in nature)
  • 考え方（かんがえかた）= way of thinking
  • だ = is

2. As an adverb

With に, it describes how or from which viewpoint something is done.

個人的には、この案に賛成だ。

"Personally, I'm in favor of this plan."

  • 個人的には（こじんてきには）= personally
  • この = this
  • 案（あん）= plan
  • に = (toward)
  • 賛成（さんせい）= in favor

How to form it

Noun + 的 (works as a な-adjective: 的な + noun, 的に + verb)

  • 科学（かがく）= science → 科学的な（かがくてきな）= scientific
  • 経済（けいざい）= economy → 経済的な（けいざいてきな）= economical
  • 現実（げんじつ）= reality → 現実的に（げんじつてきに）= realistically

In casual speech, 的 is also stuck onto other words for a vague "kind of": わたし的には (as for me, kind of).

Key idea

～的 turns a noun into "of that kind", much like "-ic" or "-al" in English.
