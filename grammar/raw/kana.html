～かな: I wonder

かな at the end of a sentence shows that the speaker is unsure and wondering, often half to themselves. It is the question particle か plus the soft particle な.

In English it is "I wonder...". It is casual, and unlike a real question it doesn't press anyone for an answer.

1. Wondering to yourself

The speaker thinks out loud about something they can't know yet.

明日は晴れるかな。

"I wonder if it'll be sunny tomorrow."

  • 明日（あした）= tomorrow
  • は = topic marker
  • 晴れる（はれる）= to be sunny
  • かな = I wonder

2. Soft requests

With a negative, ～てくれないかな turns into a gentle request: "I wonder if you could...".

ちょっと手伝ってくれないかな。

"I wonder if you could help me a bit."

  • ちょっと = a little
  • 手伝って（てつだって）= help
  • くれない = won't (do for me)
  • かな = I wonder

How to form it

Plain form + かな (nouns and な-adjectives drop だ)

  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来るかな（くるかな）= I wonder if (they'll) come
  • 本当（ほんとう）= true → 本当かな（ほんとうかな）= I wonder if it's true
  • 何にしよう（なににしよう）= what shall I have → 何にしようかな（なににしようかな）= what should I have, I wonder

かしら is a feminine version with the same meaning.

Key idea

かな is thinking out loud: the speaker isn't sure, and doesn't demand that anyone answer.
