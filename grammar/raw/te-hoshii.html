～てほしい: I want you (or someone) to do it

～てほしい expresses that the speaker wants someone else to do something. It is made by taking the て-form of a verb and adding ほしい, and it matches English "I want you to ...". The person you want to act is marked with に.

Compare it with ～たい: 行きたい means you want to go, while 行ってほしい means you want someone else to go.

1. Wanting someone to do something

Use ～てほしい to ask for or wish for another person's action.

ちょっと手伝ってほしい。

"I want you to help me a little."

  • ちょっと = a little, a bit
  • 手伝ってほしい（てつだってほしい）= want (you) to help

2. Wanting someone not to do something

To say you want someone not to do something, use the negative ない-form + でほしい.

ここでタバコを吸わないでほしいです。

"I want you not to smoke here."

  • ここ = here
  • で = location marker (at, in)
  • タバコ = cigarette, tobacco
  • を = object marker
  • 吸わないでほしい（すわないでほしい）= want (you) not to smoke
  • です = polite copula

How to form it

Verb て-form + ほしい (negative: ない-form + でほしい)

  • 手伝う（てつだう）= to help → 手伝ってほしい（てつだってほしい）= want (someone) to help
  • 来る（くる）= to come → 来てほしい（きてほしい）= want (someone) to come
  • 吸う（すう）= to smoke → 吸わないでほしい（すわないでほしい）= want (someone) not to smoke

Key idea

～てほしい points your desire at another person's action. You are not the one doing it, you are the one hoping for it.
