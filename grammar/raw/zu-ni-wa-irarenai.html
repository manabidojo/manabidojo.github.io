～ずにはいられない: can't help but do something

～ずにはいられない expresses an urge so strong that you cannot suppress it. It attaches ずには (a classical "without doing") to the negative stem of a verb and follows it with いられない, the negative potential of いる: literally, "I cannot remain without doing X."

In English it corresponds to "can't help (doing)" or "can't keep from (doing)". The は plays the same role as in ～ては: it singles out the condition (staying in a state of not doing X) and declares that condition impossible to maintain.

1. An irresistible urge

Use it when an emotion or impulse overwhelms your self-control, typically as a reaction to something you see, hear, or feel.

笑わずにはいられない。

"I can't help but laugh."

  • 笑わずには（わらわずには）= without laughing (笑う, to laugh, in the classical negative ず)
  • いられない = cannot remain (negative potential of いる, to be/stay)

2. する verbs become せずにはいられない

The irregular verb する takes the classical stem せ, giving せずにはいられない. The ending also conjugates for tense: いられなかった for the past.

心配で、電話せずにはいられなかった。

"I was so worried that I couldn't help calling."

  • 心配（しんぱい）= worry, anxiety
  • で = and so (て-form of だ, giving the cause)
  • 電話せずには（でんわせずには）= without phoning (電話する, to phone)
  • いられなかった = could not remain (past of いられない)

How to form it

Verb negative stem + ずにはいられない (する becomes せずにはいられない)

  • 笑う（わらう）= to laugh → 笑わずにはいられない（わらわずにはいられない）= can't help laughing
  • 泣く（なく）= to cry → 泣かずにはいられない（なかずにはいられない）= can't help crying
  • する = to do → せずにはいられない = can't help doing

Key idea

Read it literally: "I cannot stay in a state of not doing X." The feeling is stronger than the speaker's will, so the action is bound to break through.
