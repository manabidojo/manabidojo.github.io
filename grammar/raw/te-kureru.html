～てくれる: someone does something for me

～てくれる is made by taking the て-form of a verb and adding くれる ("to give (to me)"). It shows that someone does the action as a favor for the speaker or for someone in the speaker's group.

English often leaves this unsaid, so the closest translation is adding "for me" or "kindly": the pattern carries a feeling of gratitude toward the person who acted.

1. A favor done for you

The person doing the favor is the subject, marked with が. The favor flows toward you.

友達が手伝ってくれた。

"My friend helped me out."

  • 友達（ともだち）= friend
  • が = subject marker (the person doing the favor)
  • 手伝ってくれた（てつだってくれた）= helped (me)

母がお弁当を作ってくれました。

"My mother made me a boxed lunch."

  • 母（はは）= my mother
  • が = subject marker
  • お弁当（おべんとう）= boxed lunch
  • を = object marker
  • 作ってくれました（つくってくれました）= made (for me)

2. Asking for a favor

In the negative question form ～てくれませんか (casually ～てくれる？), the pattern becomes a friendly request.

ちょっと待ってくれませんか。

"Could you wait a moment for me?"

  • ちょっと = a little / a moment
  • 待ってくれませんか（まってくれませんか）= won't you wait (for me)?

Compare with ～てもらう: in ～てくれる the helper is the subject (友達が手伝ってくれた = My friend helped me), while in ～てもらう the receiver is the subject and the helper takes に (友達に手伝ってもらった = I (implied) received help from a friend). Both describe the same event from different sides.

How to form it

Verb て-form + くれる

  • 手伝う（てつだう）= to help → 手伝ってくれる（てつだってくれる）= helps me
  • 作る（つくる）= to make → 作ってくれる（つくってくれる）= makes (something) for me
  • 教える（おしえる）= to teach → 教えてくれる（おしえてくれる）= teaches me / tells me

Key idea

くれる means giving toward you. Adding it after the て-form says: someone did this action, the benefit came to me, and I appreciate it.
