～までもない: there's no need to (it's obvious)

～までもない after a verb in the dictionary form says that something is so obvious or simple that the action isn't necessary.

In English it is "there's no need to" or "needless to (say)". The set phrase 言うまでもなく means "needless to say".

1. Too obvious to say

The fixed phrase 言うまでもなく introduces something everyone knows.

言うまでもなく、健康は大切だ。

"Needless to say, health is important."

  • 言う（いう）= to say
  • までもなく = without even needing to
  • 健康（けんこう）= health
  • は = topic marker
  • 大切（たいせつ）= important

2. Not worth the effort

It also says that some trouble is unnecessary.

そのくらいのことなら、わざわざ会って話すまでもない。

"If that's all it is, there's no need to meet and talk about it."

  • そのくらいのこと = something that small
  • なら = if
  • わざわざ = go out of one's way
  • 会って（あって）= meet
  • 話す（はなす）= to talk
  • までもない = there's no need to

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + までもない

  • 説明する（せつめいする）= to explain → 説明するまでもない（せつめいするまでもない）= no need to explain
  • 調べる（しらべる）= to look up → 調べるまでもない（しらべるまでもない）= no need to look it up
  • 聞く（きく）= to ask → 聞くまでもない（きくまでもない）= no need to ask

Key idea

～までもない waves away an action: the answer is already obvious, so why bother.
