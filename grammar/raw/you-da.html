～ようだ: it seems / it looks like

～ようだ expresses a conjecture: a guess the speaker makes from what they can see, hear, or otherwise sense. It follows a plain-form clause and corresponds to English "it seems ..." or "it looks like ...".

The polite form is ようです. After a noun it becomes のようだ, and after a な-adjective, なようだ.

1. A guess based on evidence

Use ～ようだ when some clue makes you think something is true, even though you are not completely sure.

彼はもう帰ったようだ。

"It seems he has already gone home."

  • 彼（かれ）= he
  • は = topic marker
  • もう = already
  • 帰った（かえった）= went home
  • ようだ = it seems

Perhaps his desk is empty and the lights are off. The speaker did not see him leave, but the evidence points that way.

外は雨が降っているようです。

"It looks like it is raining outside."

  • 外（そと）= outside
  • は = topic marker
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • が = subject marker
  • 降っている（ふっている）= is falling / is raining
  • ようです = it seems (polite)

2. Saying one thing is like another

With Noun + のようだ, the same pattern makes a comparison: "is like ...".

この町は絵のようだ。

"This town is like a picture."

  • この = this
  • 町（まち）= town
  • は = topic marker
  • 絵（え）= picture, painting
  • のようだ = is like

How to form it

Plain form + ようだ (noun + のようだ, な-adjective + なようだ)

  • 帰る（かえる）= to go home → 帰ったようだ（かえったようだ）= seems to have gone home
  • 忙しい（いそがしい）= busy → 忙しいようだ（いそがしいようだ）= seems busy
  • 学生（がくせい）= student → 学生のようだ（がくせいのようだ）= seems to be a student

Key idea

～ようだ means "from what I can tell, it seems ...". The speaker has sensed some evidence and is drawing a careful, subjective conclusion from it.
