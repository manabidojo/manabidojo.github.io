～たばかり: it just happened

～たばかり says that an action happened only a moment or a short time ago. It is made by taking the た-form (plain past) of a verb and adding ばかり, and it matches English "have just done ...".

1. Right after an action

Use ～たばかり to say something happened very recently.

日本に着いたばかりです。

"I have just arrived in Japan."

  • 日本（にほん）= Japan
  • に = destination marker (in, at)
  • 着いたばかり（ついたばかり）= have just arrived
  • です = polite copula

2. Recent in the speaker's mind

Even something from last week or last month can take ～たばかり if the speaker feels it was only just now.

この時計は先週買ったばかりです。

"I just bought this watch last week."

  • この = this
  • 時計（とけい）= watch, clock
  • は = topic marker
  • 先週（せんしゅう）= last week
  • 買ったばかり（かったばかり）= just bought
  • です = polite copula

How to form it

Verb た-form + ばかり

  • 着く（つく）= to arrive → 着いたばかり（ついたばかり）= have just arrived
  • 買う（かう）= to buy → 買ったばかり（かったばかり）= have just bought
  • 始まる（はじまる）= to begin → 始まったばかり（はじまったばかり）= has just begun

Key idea

～たばかり stamps an action with the label "fresh". What counts as "just now" is decided by how recent it feels to the speaker, not by the clock.
