たとえ～ても: even if (no matter what)

たとえ at the start of a clause strengthens the following ～ても ("even if"). Together they say that the result stays the same no matter what.

In English it is "even if". たとえ itself adds no meaning, but it signals early that a concession is coming.

Nothing will change the outcome

Whatever happens in the first clause, the second one stays true. It often expresses a firm decision or strong determination.

たとえ雨が降っても、試合は行う。

"Even if it rains, the game will go ahead."

  • たとえ = even if
  • 雨（あめ）= rain
  • 降っても（ふっても）= even if (it) falls
  • 試合（しあい）= game
  • は = topic marker
  • 行う（おこなう）= to hold

たとえみんなに反対されても、私は諦めない。

"Even if everyone is against me, I won't give up."

  • たとえ = even if
  • みんな = everyone
  • 反対されても（はんたいされても）= even if (I'm) opposed
  • 諦めない（あきらめない）= won't give up

How to form it

たとえ + verb て-form + も (nouns and な-adjectives take でも)

  • 失敗する（しっぱいする）= to fail → たとえ失敗しても（たとえしっぱいしても）= even if (I) fail
  • 遠い（とおい）= far → たとえ遠くても（たとえとおくても）= even if it's far
  • 子供（こども）= child → たとえ子供でも（たとえこどもでも）= even if (it's) a child

Key idea

たとえ～ても announces up front that nothing in the first clause will change the outcome.
