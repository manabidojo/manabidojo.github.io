面倒くさい (めんどうくさい)

The core idea

面倒くさい means something is such a hassle that you don’t want to do it. 面倒 is “trouble” or “bother”, and the ending くさい adds a sense of “reeking of”. In speech it’s usually shortened to めんどくさい or just めんどい.

It can describe a task, a situation or a person. Calling someone めんどくさい means they’re high-maintenance or exhausting to deal with. Some anime characters say it so often that it becomes their whole personality.

In short

Too much trouble, I don’t want to do it.

Ways it’s used

A chore

Something you’d rather skip.

掃除そうじするの、面倒めんどうくさい。

Cleaning is such a pain.

A difficult person

Someone who’s tiring to deal with.

あの人ひと、ちょっとめんどくさいよね。

He’s kind of a lot to deal with, isn’t he?

Not wanting to do anything

The lazy, all-purpose complaint.

あー、めんどくさ。

Ugh, can’t be bothered.
