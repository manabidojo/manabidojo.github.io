いい加減 (いいかげん)

The core idea

いい加減 literally means “a good amount” (加減 is “adjustment” or “degree”). In conversation it has drifted to the opposite, careless or irresponsible. It also works as an adverb meaning “high time” or “really”, as in いい加減疲れた (“I’m getting really tired of this”).

The phrase いい加減にしろ, or the softer いい加減にしなさい, means “that’s enough!” or “knock it off!”, said when someone has pushed things too far. Parents say it to kids, and in anime it’s a common angry line.

In short

Careless and sloppy, or “that’s enough!”

Ways it’s used

Careless

About work, or a person who doesn’t take things seriously.

いい加減かげんな仕事しごとをするな。

Don’t do sloppy work.

That’s enough!

Telling someone to stop.

いい加減かげんにしろ！

Knock it off!

High time

As an adverb, when things have gone on too long.

いい加減かげんあきらめたら？

Isn’t it about time you gave up?
