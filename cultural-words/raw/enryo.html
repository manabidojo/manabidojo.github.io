遠慮 (えんりょ)

The core idea

遠慮 means holding back out of consideration for others. Not taking the last piece, not asking for too much, turning down an offer at first. The kanji are “far” and “consider”, and the word comes from a line in Confucius’s Analects about thinking far ahead. Over time, careful forethought turned into restraint.

Hosts often say 遠慮しないで (“don’t hold back”) or 遠慮なく (“help yourself”) because guests are expected to hold back at first. It’s also a polite way to decline. 遠慮しておきます means “I’ll pass”, and ご遠慮ください on a sign means “please don’t”.

In short

Politely not taking or asking for too much.

Ways it’s used

Holding back

Not taking or asking for too much.

遠慮えんりょして、最後さいごの一個いっこを取とらなかった。

I held back and didn’t take the last one.

Don’t hold back

Encouraging a guest.

遠慮えんりょしないで、たくさん食たべてね。

Don’t be shy, eat as much as you like.

Declining politely

Saying no without quite saying no.

今回こんかいは遠慮えんりょしておきます。

I think I’ll pass this time.

Please don’t

On signs and in announcements.

店内てんないでの撮影さつえいはご遠慮えんりょください。

Please refrain from taking photos inside the store.
