我慢 (がまん)

The core idea

我慢 means putting up with something difficult without complaining or giving in. That can be pain, a strong urge or an annoying situation.

The word started out in Buddhism, where it meant pride, the ego clinging to itself. That stubbornness slowly turned into today’s meaning, holding out and enduring. 我慢 is often praised, but people also say 我慢しないで (“don’t hold it in”) when someone is suffering quietly.

In short

Putting up with something hard without complaining.

Ways it’s used

Enduring pain or discomfort

Putting up with it.

痛いたいけど、もう少すこし我慢がまんして。

I know it hurts, but hang in there a little longer.

Holding back an urge

Not doing what you want to do.

笑わらうのを我慢がまんできなかった。

I couldn’t stop myself from laughing.

Reaching the limit

When you can’t take any more.

もう我慢がまんの限界げんかいだ！

I’ve had it!

Don’t hold it in

Encouraging someone not to suffer in silence.

つらいなら我慢がまんしないで。

If it’s hard, you don’t have to keep it to yourself.
