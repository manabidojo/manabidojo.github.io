もったいない

The core idea

もったいない is the feeling that something valuable is being wasted. It applies to food left on the plate, money spent badly, unused talent, or even time. English has “what a waste”, but もったいない is said much more often and about many more things.

The word comes from Buddhist vocabulary, where 勿体 referred to the proper state or dignity of a thing. So もったいない suggests something isn’t being treated with the respect it deserves. It can also be said humbly about something that feels too good for you.

In short

It’s a shame to waste something valuable.

Ways it’s used

Wasting food or things

The most common use.

残のこしたらもったいないよ。

It’d be a waste to leave it.

Wasted potential

Someone not using a talent or a chance.

そんなに上手じょうずなのに、やめるのはもったいない。

You’re so good at it, it’d be a shame to quit.

Too good for you

A humble way to accept praise or a gift.

私わたしにはもったいないです。

This is too good for me.
