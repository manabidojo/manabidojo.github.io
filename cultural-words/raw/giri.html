義理 (ぎり)

The core idea

義理 is a sense of duty toward people you have a relationship with, the obligation to return favors and do what’s expected, whether or not you feel like it. 義 is “righteousness” and 理 is “reason” or “principle”.

The best-known example is 義理チョコ, “obligation chocolate”, which some women give to male coworkers on Valentine’s Day, as opposed to 本命チョコ for someone they actually like. 義理 also means family by marriage, as in 義理の母 (“mother-in-law”).

In short

Doing what you owe people, whether or not you feel like it.

Ways it’s used

Social obligation

Doing something because you should.

義理ぎりで飲のみ会かいに参加さんかした。

I went to the drinking party out of obligation.

Obligation chocolate

Valentine’s Day gifts with no romance attached.

これは義理ぎりチョコだからね！

Just so you know, this is only obligation chocolate!

Owing someone

Feeling you have no right to refuse.

彼かれには義理ぎりがあるから断ことわれない。

I owe him, so I can’t say no.

Family by marriage

Relatives through marriage.

義理ぎりの兄あにと飲のみに行いった。

I went for drinks with my brother-in-law.
