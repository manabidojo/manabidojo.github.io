別に (べつに)

The core idea

別に literally means “separately” or “particularly”. On its own as a reply, it means “not really” or “nothing”, and it often sounds cold or dismissive, like a teenager’s “whatever”.

Followed by a negative, it softens things, as in 別にいいよ (“it’s fine, really”) or 別に怒ってない (“I’m not mad or anything”). The tone of voice decides whether it sounds relaxed or annoyed.

In short

Not really, nothing, often said in a cold way.

Ways it’s used

Not really

A short, flat answer.

「どうしたの？」「別べつに。」

“What’s wrong?” “Nothing.”

It’s fine

Saying something doesn’t matter.

別べつにいいけど。

I mean, it’s fine, I guess.

Not particularly

With a negative.

別べつに用ようはないんだけど、電話でんわしちゃった。

I didn’t have any reason in particular, I just felt like calling.
