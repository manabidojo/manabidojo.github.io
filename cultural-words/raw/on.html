恩 (おん)

The core idea

恩 is a debt of gratitude, a kindness someone did for you that you carry with you. Parents, teachers, a mentor or someone who helped you through a hard time can all be people you owe 恩 to.

The important part is that 恩 is meant to be repaid. 恩返し means paying the kindness back, and it’s the theme of many folk tales, like 鶴の恩返し (“The Grateful Crane”), where a rescued crane comes back to repay the man who saved it. Forgetting or betraying 恩 is a serious failing, as in the phrase 恩を仇で返す, “to repay kindness with harm”.

In short

A kindness you don’t forget, and one you hope to repay.

Ways it’s used

Being indebted

Someone who helped you a lot.

先生せんせいには大おおきな恩おんがある。

I owe my teacher a great deal.

Repaying kindness

恩返し, paying it back.

いつか両親りょうしんに恩返おんがえしがしたい。

Someday I want to repay my parents for everything.

A lifesaver

命の恩人, someone who saved your life.

あなたは命いのちの恩人おんじんです。

You saved my life.

Betrayal

Repaying kindness with harm.

恩おんを仇あだで返かえすつもりか。

Is this how you repay me?
