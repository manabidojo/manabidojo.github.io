甘える (あまえる)

The core idea

甘える means leaning on someone’s goodwill, acting a bit dependent and expecting them to indulge you. It comes from 甘い, “sweet”. A child clinging to a parent is the classic picture, but adults do it too, with partners, close friends or even bosses.

English words for this usually sound negative, like “clingy” or “spoiled”. In Japanese it’s often affectionate. Letting someone 甘える shows you’re close, and accepting kindness with お言葉に甘えて (“I’ll take you up on your kind offer”) is simply polite.

In short

Relying on someone’s kindness, like a child with a parent.

Ways it’s used

Wanting attention

Children with parents, partners with each other, or pets with their owners.

猫ねこが甘あまえてくる。

The cat keeps coming over for attention.

Accepting an offer

The set phrase お言葉に甘えて is a polite way to say yes.

では、お言葉ことばに甘あまえて。

Well then, I’ll take you up on that.

Relying on others too much

Said as a criticism.

甘あまえるな！

Stop expecting everyone else to do it for you!
