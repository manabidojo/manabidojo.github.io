お疲れ様 (おつかれさま)

The core idea

お疲れ様 literally recognizes that someone is tired, from 疲れる, “to get tired”. In practice it’s an all-purpose greeting between coworkers or teammates. You say it when someone finishes a task, when leaving work, when passing a colleague in the hall, and even at the start of a work message.

Subtitles switch between “good work”, “thanks”, “see you” and “hi”, because no single English phrase covers all of these. It’s safe to say to anyone, unlike ご苦労様, which normally only goes from someone higher up to someone below.

In short

Thanks for your hard work, also used as hello and goodbye at work.

Ways it’s used

Finishing work

Said when someone leaves or a job is done.

今日きょうもお疲つかれ様さまでした。

Thanks for your hard work today.

Greeting a coworker

Passing someone at work or opening a message.

お疲つかれ様さまです。資料しりょうを送おくります。

Hi, I’m sending over the documents.

Casual version

Between friends, often shortened.

おつかれー！

Nice work!
