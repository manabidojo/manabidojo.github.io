おかげさまで

The core idea

おかげさまで means “thanks to you”, but people say it even when the listener had nothing to do with it. Asked “how have you been?” or “how did the exam go?”, a reply starting with おかげさまで is a polite way of crediting the people and luck around you instead of yourself.

おかげ (お陰) comes from 陰, “shade”, and originally referred to the protection of gods and buddhas. Today it thanks everyone in general.

In short

I’m doing well, and I’m grateful for it.

Ways it’s used

Answering “how are you?”

A polite, modest way to say things are good.

おかげさまで、元気げんきにしています。

I’m doing well, thank you.

Sharing good news

Without taking all the credit.

おかげさまで、無事ぶじに合格ごうかくしました。

Thankfully, I passed.

Thanking someone who really helped

The literal meaning still works too.

おかげさまで間まに合あいました。ありがとうございます。

Thanks to you, I made it in time. Thank you so much.
