しょうがない・仕方がない (しかたがない)

The core idea

しょうがない and 仕方がない both mean there’s no way to do anything about it. 仕方 is “a way of doing”, so the phrase literally says there’s no method left. しょうがない comes from しようがない, which means the same thing, and it’s the more casual, spoken version. 仕方ない, without が, is also common.

The phrase is used for accepting things you can’t change, from bad weather to bigger losses. It can sound calm and mature, or like giving up too easily, depending on the situation. It also has an affectionate use, about someone whose faults you’ve decided to put up with.

In short

Nothing can be done about it, so accept it.

Ways it’s used

Accepting bad luck

Nothing anyone could have done.

雨あめだからしょうがないね。

It’s raining, so there’s nothing we can do.

Making the best of it

Going along with something you don’t love.

仕方しかたがない、歩あるいて帰かえろう。

Oh well, let’s walk home.

Fond annoyance

About someone you can’t stay mad at.

しょうがないなあ、今回こんかいだけだよ。

Oh, fine. Just this once.

No point

〜てもしょうがない means it’s no use doing something.

今いまさら悩なやんでもしょうがない。

There’s no point worrying about it now.
