適当 (てきとう)

The core idea

適当 has two meanings that sit far apart. In writing and formal speech it means “suitable” or “appropriate”, which is what the kanji say (適 is “to fit” and 当 is “to hit the mark”). In everyday conversation it usually means doing something roughly, without much thought, just enough to get by.

The casual meaning grew out of the formal one. Something that fits well enough slid toward “whatever fits, I don’t care”. So when a test says 適当な言葉を選びなさい it means “choose a suitable word”, but when a friend says 適当でいいよ it means “don’t overthink it”.

In short

Suitable, or done roughly without much care.

Ways it’s used

Suitable

The original meaning, common in instructions and formal writing.

適当てきとうな言葉ことばを選えらんでください。

Please choose a suitable word.

Rough, without much thought

The everyday meaning, often said about yourself without any shame.

料理りょうりはいつも適当てきとうに作つくってる。

I always just throw my cooking together.

Careless or unreliable

About a person, it’s a complaint.

あの人ひと、本当ほんとうに適当てきとうだよね。

That guy really doesn’t take anything seriously.

Whatever works

Telling someone not to worry about the details.

適当てきとうに座すわってね。

Sit wherever you like.
