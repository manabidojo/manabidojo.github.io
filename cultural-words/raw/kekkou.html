結構 (けっこう)

The core idea

結構 has three common meanings, and two of them seem to point in opposite directions. As an adjective it means “fine” or “good enough”. In 結構です it can be a polite “no thank you”, or a “yes, that’s fine”, depending on the question. As an adverb it means “quite” or “pretty”, as in 結構高い (“pretty expensive”).

The “no thanks” use catches people out the most. If someone offers you something, 結構です usually means no. If someone asks whether something is acceptable, it means yes.

In short

Fine, no thank you, or quite, depending on context.

Ways it’s used

No, thank you

Turning down an offer.

袋ふくろは結構けっこうです。

I don’t need a bag, thanks.

Fine, acceptable

Approving something.

明日あしたで結構けっこうですよ。

Tomorrow is fine.

Quite, pretty

Before an adjective or verb, it means more than expected.

この店みせ、結構けっこうおいしいね。

This place is pretty good, huh.
