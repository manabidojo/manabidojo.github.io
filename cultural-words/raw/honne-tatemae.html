本音と建前 (ほんねとたてまえ)

The core idea

本音 is what you really think. 建前 is what you say in public, the position you show because the situation calls for it. The two words often appear together as 本音と建前, meaning the gap between them. 本音 literally means “true sound”.

建前 is often translated as “a facade”, which makes it sound like lying. In practice it’s closer to tact, or the answer everyone expects. Saying “I’d love to, but I’m busy” when you just don’t want to go is 建前, and most listeners understand that. Every language has this, and Japanese simply has everyday words for it.

In short

What you really think, and what you say in public.

Ways it’s used

Real feelings

What gets said for appearances.

To know when to say what you think and when to say what’s expected.
