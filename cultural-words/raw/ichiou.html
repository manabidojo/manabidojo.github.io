一応 (いちおう)

The core idea

一応 means something has been done, or is true, at a basic level, and the speaker isn’t promising more than that. Saying it is a bit like ticking a box and moving on. The word was originally written 一往, meaning “going through once”, as in giving something a single pass and not looking any closer.

Speakers use it to avoid sounding overconfident, or to cover themselves in case something isn’t quite right. Depending on the situation, that can come across as modest or a little evasive.

In short

Done or true at a basic level, with no promises beyond that.

Ways it’s used

Just in case

Doing something as a precaution.

傘かさ、一応いちおう持もっていこう。

I’ll take an umbrella, just in case.

“Technically” or “more or less”

It counts, but don’t look too closely.

宿題しゅくだいは一応いちおう終おわった。

My homework’s done… more or less.

Modesty

Not overclaiming about yourself.

一応いちおう、料理りょうりはできます。

I can cook, at least the basics.

Downplaying

About people or relationships, it can sound shy or lukewarm.

一応いちおう、彼氏かれしです。

He’s my boyfriend, I guess.

Leaving room for correction

You’ll hear this a lot at work, when something is finished but might still need fixing.

一応いちおうできましたが、確認かくにんしていただけますか。

It’s done, but could you check it?
